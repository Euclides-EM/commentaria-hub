# Candidate Arguments

Use this file to draft and compare possible conference arguments.

## Working Premise

The argument should be rebuilt from the larger corpus. The older presentation is a useful foil, but not the mold.

Current direction:

The strongest emerging argument is not purely social and not purely intellectual. Title pages appear to mediate between **communities of use** and **ideals of mathematical knowledge**. They identify who mathematics is for, while also advertising what kind of mathematics is valuable: useful, easy, demonstrative, corrected, enriched, ancient, institutional, practical, or portable.

This lets the paper keep the abstract's concern with communities of learning while expanding it beyond Euclid's Elements alone.

## Candidate Argument A: Title Pages As Maps Of Mathematical Communities

Claim:

Title pages map the social terrain of early modern mathematics by naming students, academies, Jesuit colleges, artisans, engineers, soldiers, merchants, nobles, readers, and institutional authorities.

Evidence needed:

- Audience and institution feature prevalence by subject family.
- Social group crosswalks.
- Cases where named publics are unusually explicit.

Strong cases:

- `bib-86`: instrument book addressing geometers, surveyors, civil architects, military men, painters, sculptors, bombardiers, merchants.
- `bib-156`: mechanics/instruments for captains, engineers, architects, artisans.
- `Paris_1516a`: Parisian academic/student mathematical context.
- `Lisbon_1735`: royal classroom and Jesuit college.

Counter-evidence:

- Many title pages have little or no explicit audience.
- Patronage/dedication is not the same as audience.

## Candidate Argument C: Euclid As Anchor In A Wider Mathematical Ecology

## Candidate Argument F: Title Pages As Transmission Machines

Claim:

Early modern mathematical title pages do not simply label books by subject. They stage mathematical knowledge as a set of socially authorized procedures: translating, correcting, augmenting, demonstrating, extracting, making useful, restoring ancient authority, and fitting texts to particular publics.

Why this may be the strongest umbrella:

- It lets the social and intellectual analyses stay balanced: social arenas explain why certain intellectual virtues matter, while intellectual claims show what those social worlds expect mathematics to do.
- It avoids forcing the corpus into a pure/applied split.
- It explains why Euclid/Elements looks unstable: Euclidean identity can be canonical, pedagogical, practical, humanist, institutional, or composite depending on the title-page rhetoric.
- It makes absence meaningful: sparse canonical title pages can rely on author/title/textual authority without advertising utility or audience.

Evidence needed:

- `phase4c_deeper_social_intellectual_paths.md`
- `phase4d_deeper_social_arenas.md`
- `derived_data/rich_title_page_archetype_counts.csv`
- `derived_data/rich_social_arena_claim_crosswalk.csv`
- Close readings from `derived_data/rich_claim_mode_examples.csv` and `derived_data/rich_social_arena_examples.csv`.

Strong case types:

- Sparse canonical identity: Euclid/Elements title pages with little social or pedagogical explanation.
- Procedural pedagogical identity: Euclidean/geometrical books framed by method, ease, demonstration, figures, correction, or use.
- Composite workshop books: title pages that advertise additions, tables, notes, comments, bound-with works, or practical extracts.
- Utility public books: works where audience/profession and practical use reinforce each other.
- Humanist transfer books: ancient authority moved through translation, restoration, edition, and learned apparatus.

Counter-evidence:

- Rich exploratory tags are broader than conservative classifications; individual cases must be verified.
- Some social categories are small or under-detected, especially explicit audiences and specific trades.
- Not every book with Euclid/Elements language belongs to the same historical pattern.

Slide structure:

This could replace Candidate E as the umbrella. Candidate E becomes one supporting chapter inside it.

**Euclid/Elements is a mobile authority: recognizable enough to anchor title-page identity, but flexible enough to be recomposed for different social and intellectual settings.**

Metadata Elements refinement:

Within the metadata-defined Elements corpus, `1-6 + 11-12` appears to be a distinct practical-pedagogical package rather than a merely abbreviated Elements. In 39 representative cases, it over-indexes practical/public rhetoric, method/demonstration/order, utility/application, accessibility/ease, institutional authority, religious institutional settings, and military/fortification settings. It is especially concentrated after 1650 and is more English/French/Spanish than the rest of the Elements corpus, though Latin remains substantial.

Provisional claim:

**Books 1-6 plus 11-12 package Euclid as teachable and usable geometry: elementary plane geometry plus enough solid geometry to support institutional, military/technical, and practical mathematical settings.**

Result after testing against plain `1-6`:

The contrast holds, but becomes more nuanced. Plain `1-6` is an older and broader elementary-canonical package, with strong Latin/Dutch presence and more translation, composite framing, sparse canonical identity, and vernacular access. `1-6 + 11-12` is later and more sharply practical-pedagogical: it over-indexes practical/public mode, utility, ease/accessibility, method/access, religious-institutional authority, and military/academy settings. A 1650+ check preserves the contrast. Removing Dechales/Tacquet weakens the ease/method/Jesuit strand, but the military/practical/public strand remains strong.

Revised claim:

**Different partial Elements editions perform different work. Plain `1-6` often presents elementary Euclid; `1-6 + 11-12` more often presents usable Euclid.**

Need to test next:

- Compare `1-6 + 11-12` with neighboring non-Elements practical geometry.
- Treat Dutch plain `1-6` editions as a possible parallel practical-vernacular route.
- Check diagrams/apparatus evidence against the book-coverage distinction.

Result after comparing with non-Elements practical geometry:

`1-6 + 11-12` is a bridge form, but it bridges from the side of Euclidean authority. Compared with non-metadata practical geometry, it is much more method/institution/theory-heavy: procedural/pedagogical identity, method/demonstration/order, religious-institutional authority, theoretical mathematics, ease/accessibility, learned/scholarly framing, school framing, correction, and professor/lecturer authority are all stronger. Utility/practice/application is almost equal, meaning `1-6 + 11-12` is not more useful than practical geometry; it is more Euclidean-institutional while still making use claims.

Closest non-Elements neighbors are often hybrid works that invoke Euclid/Elements language, join theoretical and practical geometry, discuss planes and solids, or use institutional/professorial authority. This supports a gradient model: canonical Euclid -> usable Euclid -> Euclidean practical geometry -> professional/material practical arts.

Natural-mode refinement:

The metadata Elements corpus does not divide into clean subcorpora. Its natural modes overlap heavily. The dense center is:

**ancient/canonical authority + institutional authority + pedagogy/method + apparatus.**

Edges:

- `practical/public`: Euclid becomes usable without ceasing to be canonical; most practical/public Elements cases are also pedagogical/method and humanist/ancient.
- `vernacular/transfer`: Euclid travels across languages and publics while remaining canonical/pedagogical.
- `sparse/canonical`: possible reliance on canonical identity, but this must be controlled for title-page fashion, especially period, city, language, format, and school/institutional context.
- `1-6 + 11-12`: especially strong practical/public book-coverage package.
- Dutch plain `1-6`: possible earlier practical-vernacular route.

Format matters:

- Folios over-index composite/apparatus.
- Quartos over-index vernacular/transfer.
- Duodecimos are highly institutional and fairly practical/public.

Broader-corpus contrast:

The Elements subset is distinctive not because it alone has method, utility, or pedagogy, but because it binds those values to ancient/canonical authority. Non-Elements books also talk about method, usefulness, pedagogy, and practice. The special thing about Elements editions is that these values are attached to Euclid/Elements as an ancient, canonical authority.

Sparse/fashion-control result:

Sparse-canonical should not be a major thesis by itself yet. It is a real title-page posture, but it is strongly entangled with city and language, and moderately entangled with format and period. Explicit school/institution markers usually make title pages less sparse in the current feature set. The safest claim is:

**Canonical silence is one possible strategy inside the Elements corpus, and we can only identify it after subtracting local title-page fashion.**

Use sparse-canonical as:

- a contrast mode against procedural/pedagogical, practical/public, and composite/apparatus Elements;
- a methodological caution about title-page fashion;
- a close-reading category after controls.

Do not use sparse-canonical as immediate evidence that a book had no audience, no use, or sufficient canonical authority.

Author/editor portfolio result:

This is corpus-internal, not full-bibliography evidence. We do not have every named person's complete bibliography, so these results show how authors/editors appear within the represented title-page corpus, not whether Euclid was central or marginal to their whole career.

Within that limit, the Elements behaves in named portfolios as a site of **canon-work**. Among 13 bridge portfolios with at least two Elements and two non-Elements cases represented in this corpus, ancient authority/restoration is higher in Elements for all 13. Average Elements-minus-non-Elements deltas are especially high for ancient authority/restoration (+86.3 pp), translation/transfer (+35.2 pp), correction/revision (+23.8 pp), and method/order (+19.4 pp). Utility/practice is lower in Elements (-13.2 pp), while no visible social arena is also lower (-30.3 pp).

This suggests that the Elements is not merely a theoretical subject subset. It is a canonical corpus that repeatedly calls forth mediation: restoration, correction, translation, augmentation, selection, demonstration, and social relocation.

Revised high-level claim:

**In the represented title-page ecology, the Elements functions as canon-work: recognizable ancient authority made usable, teachable, portable, and institutionally/socially meaningful through title-page acts of mediation.**

Good close-reading anchors:

- Dou: Dutch plain `1-6` as practical-pedagogical Elements route.

Elements title pages often add ancient authority and some form of canonical/textual mediation: correction, translation, augmentation, selection, or method. First controlled close-reading synthesis:

The close readings show that the Elements/non-Elements contrast is not theory versus practice. It is better described as **canonical mediation versus more direct practical/procedural application**. Elements title pages repeatedly translate, comment, correct, augment, select, demonstrate, and socially relocate Euclid. Neighboring non-Elements works more directly advertise utility, instruments, military technique, surveying, arithmetic procedure, and professional application.

Slide-level formulation:

**The Elements often looks less like the most practical book in the local mathematical ecology and more like the canonical grammar through which practical, pedagogical, and institutional mathematics can be authorized.**

Best examples:

- Henrion: Euclid translated/revised/augmented against military arithmetic and instruments.
- Forcadel: Euclid translated/commented inside royal-university authority; strong same-format control and useful complication.
- Clavius: Jesuit institutional apparatus around Euclid.
- Errard: royal engineering identity shared across fortification/practical geometry and Elements mediation.
- Rudd: English contrast between Euclid contracted/demonstrated and practical geometry for surveyors/engineers/students.

Dutch plain `1-6` result:

Dutch plain `1-6`, especially Dou, is a separate practical-vernacular route. It reaches the same practical/public level as `1-6 + 11-12` but without adding books 11-12. It makes the first six books usable through Dutch translation, explanation, correction, added utilities, geometrical operations, public lovers of the free art, and land-surveyor/wine-gauger authority.

Revised practical-Elements claim:

There are at least two practical Elements pathways:

1. Dutch/Dou plain `1-6`: vernacular, civic-measuring, public lovers/practitioners, operational plane geometry.
2. Later `1-6 + 11-12`: institutional/military/academy/course-oriented usable Euclid, often via solid geometry.

New/reorganized Elements result:

The "anti-philological pole" exists but should be modeled as a spectrum rather than a binary. The strict reconstructive cases are rare: `Paris_1667` (`Nouveaux Elemens`, new order, new demonstrations, new means/measures/ways) and `Livorno_1709` (`Euclides reformatus`, new method, clearer/easier/firmer demonstration). But a larger hybrid field claims new/easy method, selected propositions, contraction, reduction, symbolic/algebraic retooling, or new demonstrations while still invoking Euclid, correction, translation, augmentation, or ancient authority.

Spectrum:

1. Philological-restorative: restore, correct, purge, return to Greek/ancient authority.
2. Hybrid pedagogical mediation: correct/translate/augment Euclid while making him easier, shorter, clearer, more useful, or more demonstrable.
3. Reconstructive: new Elements, reformed Euclid, new order, new demonstrations, symbolic/algebraic retooling.

Refinement:

The reconstructive pole is not simply anti-philological. It may be a rival restoration ideal. Instead of restoring the ancient wording or textual transmission, it restores Euclid's logical function: demonstrative order, clarity, teachability, and rational force.

Useful phrasing:

**logical restoration** or **pedagogical restoration of Euclid's demonstrative order**.

This becomes especially visible from the mid-seventeenth century onward. It is often near vernacular/translingual and pedagogical contexts, and sometimes practical/technical publics, but it is not limited to them.

Restoration/reconstruction clusters:

The core question becomes: **Which Euclid should be restored?**

- Philological / ancient-text restoration: Euclid as authentic ancient text.
- Hybrid corrective-pedagogical mediation: Euclid as corrected, translated, augmented, teachable text.
- Logical / demonstrative restoration: Euclid as rational order and proof architecture.
- Symbolic / analytic retooling: Euclid as portable proof system.
- Practical / technical refunctionalization: Euclid as operational instrument for users.
- Selection / contraction / portable Euclid: Euclid as manageable curriculum or extractable package.

Slide-level version:

**The question was not only "How faithful is this edition to Euclid?" It was "Which Euclid should be restored?"**

Best cases:

- `Paris_1667`: strongest new/reordered Elements case.
- `Livorno_1709`: `Euclides reformatus`.
- `Cologne_1556`: selected propositions from later books ordered for demonstration.
- `Paris_1640`: tenth book reduced to 62 propositions with new easier demonstrations.
- `Kiel_and_Leipzig_1699`: Euclid through algebraic signs from the newest analytic art.
- `The_Hague_1758`: first six books put in new order for youth.

Future route:

Analyze which deductive/mathematical parts title pages name: propositions, demonstrations, axioms, scholia, diagrams/figures, theorems, corollaries, notes, definitions, postulates, lemmas, enunciations.

Deductive-parts result:

Named deductive or mathematical parts appear in 359/843 representative rows, 42.6%. They are much more common in the metadata-defined Elements subset: 165/286, 57.7%, versus 194/557, 34.8% in the non-Elements ecology.

The distinctive Elements signals are demonstrations/proofs, scholia/commentary, principles, propositions, theorems, and enunciations. The broader non-Elements ecology more often advertises problems, operations/constructions, examples, and notes/observations. Figures/diagrams are not an Elements specialty: they appear at almost the same rate in Elements and non-Elements.

This strengthens the core historical argument. The Elements is not simply one geometry subject among others. It is publicly framed as a canonical demonstrative-commentarial corpus: propositions, demonstrations, scholia, principles, figures, selected apparatus, and uses. Neighboring mathematical books more often advertise mathematics as procedure, operation, problem-solving, visual aid, table, instrument, or application.

The reconstructive-restoration analysis is also sharpened. Reconstructive Euclid is usually not advertised as a return to axioms, postulates, definitions, or lemmas. Those parts are rarely foregrounded on title pages. Instead, reconstruction works through demonstrations, propositions, order, selected/reduced proposition sets, easier method, and use. It is a restoration of Euclid's demonstrative functioning, not primarily of an axiomatic skeleton.

Important controls:

- Demonstration/proof language in Elements rises after 1600 and is strongest in 1650-1699.
- Scholia/commentary are more visible earlier and in institutional/commentarial forms.
- Folio Elements lean toward scholia/commentary; smaller formats are often more proposition/proof portable.
- Practical-pedagogical Elements combine propositions, principles, figures, and occasional problems/operations rather than abandoning Euclid's demonstrative identity.
