# Phase 15 First Controlled Close Readings

Question:

What do the strongest controlled same-person clusters actually show when we read the title-page annotation fields, rather than only the flags?

Scope:

This phase reads annotation fields for selected controlled pairs from Phase 14. It is still corpus-internal and title-page-based. It does not claim full author bibliography coverage.

## Main Finding

The controlled close readings support the revised canon-work claim, but they also sharpen it:

Elements title pages do not simply become "more theoretical" or "less practical." Instead, they often reframe mathematical work as the mediation of a named ancient/canonical corpus: translating, commenting, correcting, augmenting, selecting, demonstrating, or attaching Euclid to an institutional/professional role.

Neighboring non-Elements works often state usefulness, practice, instruments, arithmetic, fortification, surveying, or broad practical audience more directly.

So the contrast is not:

Elements = theory; non-Elements = practice.

Better:

Elements = canonical mediation; non-Elements = more open practical/procedural application, though the two can overlap.

## Henrion: Canon-Work Against Practical Mathematical Range

Best pairs:

- `Paris_1623` against same-year Paris non-Elements cases;
- `Paris_1630` against `MorisS-16-11`;
- `Paris_1615` against nearby Paris non-Elements works.

### `Paris_1623`: Elements As Translation, Revision, Augmentation

Title-page signals:

- base content: `Les Qvinze livres des elements d’Evclide`;
- actions: `Traduicts`, `Reueu`, `augmenté`;
- edition detail: second edition, revised and augmented;
- origin/destination: Latin to French;
- added material: a summary of algebra;
- social/editorial identity: Henrion as mathematician.

Interpretation:

This is a strong canon-work page. The title page does not merely identify Euclid; it presents the Elements as a text requiring translation, revision, augmentation, and auxiliary explanation.

### Same-Year Contrast: `MorisS-16-1`

The non-Elements comparison is not less rich. It is differently rich:

- `De l’Arithmetique militaire`;
- practical contents: tables of sines/tangents/secants, triangles, geometric problems, dimensions of lines/surfaces/bodies, compass of proportion, fortresses;
- audience: the French nobility;
- edition work: revised, corrected, augmented;
- added treatise on fortifications in the Low Countries.

Interpretation:

Henrion's non-Elements title page also uses correction and augmentation, but its center of gravity is practical and military: a toolbox of procedures for noble/military mathematical use. The Elements page is canon-work; the arithmetic/military page is applied procedure.

### `Paris_1630`: Selected Euclid

Signals:

- `LES SIX PREMIERS LIVRES DES ELEMENS D’EVCLIDE`;
- `Extraicts`, `tirez`, `Traduits`;
- extracted from mathematical works;
- Latin to French.

Against `MorisS-16-11`, whose visible title-page evidence is basically Henrion as mathematician attached to cartography/instrument/practical geometry subjects.

Interpretation:

Here the Elements becomes a selected/extracted pedagogical-canonical package. The non-Elements neighboring work is subject-practical, but not title-page rich in the same way.

### Henrion Takeaway

Henrion is the strongest controlled anchor for the argument:

Within a same city/language/moment cluster, Elements title pages foreground Euclid as translated, revised, augmented, excerpted, and explained. Nearby non-Elements works foreground practical mathematical operations, military arithmetic, instruments, fortification, or broad procedural usefulness.

## Forcadel: The Best Complication

Best strict same-format pair:

- `Paris_1565` Elements;
- `UEIZFO` arithmetic;
- both Paris, French, quarto, 1565, Charles Perier.

### `Paris_1565`: Euclid As Translated And Commented University/Court Work

Signals:

- seventh, eighth, and ninth books of Euclid's Elements;
- translated and commented;
- attached content on light/heavy or weight;
- editor identity: ordinary royal reader in mathematics at the University of Paris;
- institution: University of Paris.

Interpretation:

The Elements page is strongly institutional: royal/university mathematical authority plus translation/commentary. It is a canon-work page because Euclid is mediated through translation and commentary.

### `UEIZFO`: Arithmetic As Complete And Abridged

Signals:

- `ARITHMETIQVE`;
- `ENTIERE ET ABREGEE`;
- Forcadel as royal reader in mathematics.

Interpretation:

The non-Elements title page is much terser and framed by abridgment/completeness. It shares the court/royal mathematical persona, but not the Euclidean translation/commentary apparatus.

### Why Forcadel Complicates The Claim

Forcadel's Elements title pages do not always carry the method/order signal. Some nearby non-Elements works are more explicitly methodical or procedural. The Elements signal is more about translation, commentary, institutional authority, and ancient/canonical attachment than about method in every case.

This is good. It prevents the argument from flattening canon-work into "method."

### Forcadel Takeaway

Forcadel is the best controlled example for saying:

Elements canon-work can be institutional and translational without being especially practical or even explicitly method-heavy.

## Clavius: Stable Institution, Elements-Specific Apparatus

Best pairs:

- `Rome_1603` vs `bib-6` Rome 1604;
- `Rome_1589` vs `X2T7PY` Rome 1586.

### `Rome_1603`: Jesuit Elements As Demonstrated, Scholarly, Augmented

Signals:

- `EVCLIDIS ELEMENTORVM LIBRI XV`;
- later books;
- fourth edition;
- augmented after the first edition;
- clear demonstrations and accurate scholia;
- Clavius of the Society of Jesus;
- added sixteenth book on regular solids.

Interpretation:

This is textbook canon-work in the literal sense: institutional editor, ancient text, edition history, additions, demonstrations, scholia, and expanded geometric apparatus.

### `bib-6`: Same Institution, Less Canon-Work On The Title Page

The comparison shares the Jesuit identity, but the title-page evidence is much thinner in the current annotations.

Interpretation:

Clavius is useful because the social/institutional setting is stable. The difference is not "Jesuit versus non-Jesuit"; it is that the Elements title page activates a thicker apparatus of edition, demonstration, augmentation, and ancient textual identity.

## Ozanam: Public/Military Euclid, Not Generic Utility

Best pairs:

- `Paris_1693` vs `bib-250` 1694;
- `Paris_1697` vs `bib-250`, `bib-58`, `bib-80`.

### Elements Side: `Paris_1693` And `Paris_1697`

Signals:

- `les Elemens d’Euclide`;
- Ozanam as professor of mathematics;
- audience: a man of war and all those who want to perfect themselves in mathematics;
- later edition revised/corrected.

Interpretation:

Ozanam's Elements is not socially silent. It addresses military and self-improving mathematical publics. But the explicit utility language is less prominent than in neighboring practical works.

### Non-Elements Neighbors

`bib-58`:

- compass of proportion;
- explained and demonstrated in a short and easy manner;
- corrected and augmented edition;
- added treatise on division of fields.

`bib-80`:

- architects, engineers, masons, surveyors, gaugers, geometers;
- augmented with notes and a treatise of arithmetic by geometry.

`bib-250`:

- problems of arithmetic, geometry, optics, gnomonics, cosmography, mechanics, pyrotechnics, physics.

Interpretation:

The non-Elements Ozanam world is more explicitly procedural, useful, and trade/profession facing. The Elements title pages attach Euclid to military/public improvement and correction, but they do not become the most practically advertised works in the local cluster.

### Ozanam Takeaway

Ozanam helps us avoid a false choice:

Elements can be public and military without being the most utility-forward title page. Its publicness is routed through Euclid as a curriculum of mathematical perfection.

## Errard: Professional Continuity Plus Elements-Specific Mediation

Best pairs:

- `Paris_1598a` vs `bib-280`;
- `Paris_1605` vs `ustc-29`.

### Elements Side

`Paris_1598a`:

- first six books of Euclid;
- translated and commented;
- dedicated;
- Errard as engineer of the king.

`Paris_1605`:

- first nine books;
- translated and commented;
- revised and corrected;
- Errard as engineer of the king.

### Non-Elements Side

`bib-280`:

- fortification reduced into art;
- Errard as royal engineer.

`ustc-29`:

- geometry and general practice of it;
- second edition;
- Errard as ordinary engineer of the king.

Interpretation:

Errard is not a switch from practical to canonical worlds. The professional/royal engineering identity is continuous. What changes is the work performed on the text: the Elements pages add translation, commentary, selection by books, and revision/correction.

### Errard Takeaway

Errard is a bridge case:

The same professional social identity can carry both practical geometry/fortification and Elements canon-work. The Elements does not leave practice behind; it canonizes or regularizes geometry inside a professional/royal engineering persona.

## Rudd: English Single-Pair Illustration

Pair:

- `London_1651` Elements;
- `bib-133` Practical Geometry, 1650.

### Elements Side

Signals:

- Euclid's Elements, first six books;
- contracted and demonstrated;
- by Captain Thomas Rudd, chief engineer to the late king;
- with John Dee's mathematical preface.

### Non-Elements Side

Signals:

- Practical Geometry;
- arithmetic operations, reduction, rule of proportion, geometric questions, solutions and demonstrations;
- explicitly necessary for all men, especially surveyors, engineers, and students in mathematics.

Interpretation:

Rudd is almost a perfect miniature of the distinction:

- Elements: contracted, demonstrated, ancient/canonical, anchored by Dee and a military engineer.
- Non-Elements: practical, procedural, broadly necessary, addressed to surveyors, engineers, and students.

Because it is one pair, use it illustratively, not statistically.

## Cross-Cluster Synthesis

The controlled close readings suggest four recurring relationships between Elements and neighboring mathematical print:

1. **Textual mediation**: translation, commentary, revision, correction, augmentation.
2. **Canonical anchoring**: Euclid/Elements gives authority and identity.
3. **Social relocation**: the ancient corpus is placed into schools, Jesuit institutions, royal engineering, military readers, French readers, or English practical readers.
4. **Practical adjacency**: practical and useful mathematics often sits next to the Elements, sometimes sharing personnel and social worlds, but non-Elements works usually advertise utility more directly.

## Revised Argument Formulation

The Elements lives in the early modern mathematical ecology not simply as a theoretical text and not simply as a schoolbook. It is a canonical corpus repeatedly remade for use: translated, commented, corrected, augmented, excerpted, demonstrated, and attached to local social identities.

Its neighboring non-Elements works often make usefulness, instruments, military technique, arithmetic procedure, surveying, and professional application louder. That contrast helps define the Elements' place: it is often not the most practical-looking object in the local ecology, but it provides a canonical grammar through which practical and pedagogical mathematics can be authorized.
