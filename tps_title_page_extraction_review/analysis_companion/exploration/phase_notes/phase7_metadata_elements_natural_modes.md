# Phase 7 Natural Modes Inside The Metadata Elements Corpus

This pass deliberately **does not use Wardhaugh/textual lineage labels** as an organizing structure. Those labels may still be useful as weak bibliographic context, but the analysis here asks what divisions emerge from our own evidence: title-page features, subject classifications, book coverage, language, social arenas, and intellectual claim modes.

Corpus: 286 representative metadata Elements works in the title-page analysis corpus.

## Provisional Taxonomy Without External Lineages

The metadata Elements corpus seems to have **six overlapping natural modes**:

1. **Canonical/sparse Elements**: identity carried mainly by Euclid/title/text, with limited social or promotional framing.
2. **Pedagogical/method Elements**: order, demonstration, ease, brevity, explanation, school or learner settings.
3. **Vernacular/transfer Elements**: translation and language movement as an intellectual act.
4. **Institutional Elements**: colleges, Jesuit settings, academies, professors, royal classrooms, learned credentials.
5. **Composite/apparatus Elements**: Data, Optics, Archimedes, figures, tables, appendices, corollaries, additions, bound-with material.
6. **Practical/public Elements**: use, practice, instruments, propositions applied across mathematics, readers/lovers, surveyors or professional publics.

These are not separate bins. They are axes. An edition can be, for example, vernacular + pedagogical + practical; or Latin + institutional + composite; or sparse + canonical + complete.

## Main Historical Claim

The Elements corpus does not have one native division. It has a **field of flexible boundaries**. The strongest boundaries are book coverage, language/translation, institutional setting, and apparatus/pedagogy. But those boundaries cross one another rather than aligning into clean sub-corpora.

This means the Elements lives in the broader mathematical ecology as a canonical object that can be repeatedly repackaged: as school text, learned ancient text, vernacular method book, practical instrument of use, institutional credential, or composite mathematical archive.
