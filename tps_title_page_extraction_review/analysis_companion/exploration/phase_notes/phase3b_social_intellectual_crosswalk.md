## Reading So Far

1. Professional and practical publics are not just social labels; they are associated with promoted values such as utility, method/demonstration, correction, and enrichment.
2. Institutional and religious settings often connect authority and pedagogy: colleges, academies, Jesuit identities, named ancient or educational authorities, and curated additions.
3. Royal/court and dedicatee-heavy title pages may be a separate political-patronage layer; we should distinguish patronage from audience.
4. The social groups can help explain why specific intellectual ideals are foregrounded: mathematical knowledge is framed differently for students, artisans, soldiers, engineers, merchants, academies, and learned readers.
5. Absences matter: some Euclidean/title-identity pages have little social address, suggesting a different mode of authority centered on text, antiquity, or canonical identity.
