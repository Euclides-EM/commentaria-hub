# Phase 4D Deeper Social Arenas

This pass treats the social layer as a set of **arenas** rather than a single audience field. The earlier social topology separated audience, institutions, editor credentials, and patronage; this pass asks what broader social worlds those fragments point toward.

These tags are deliberately broad and heuristic. They are meant to show where to read, not to replace close reading.

The striking result is not just that many title pages name social worlds. It is that they name **different mechanisms of legitimacy**:

- court/state service makes mathematics useful to power, administration, surveying, military work, and prestige.
- pedagogy/schools make mathematics teachable, ordered, and disciplinary.
- religious institutions make mathematics part of learned institutional authority.
- professional/practical arenas connect mathematics to offices, trades, instruments, land, navigation, military technique, or architecture.
- general publics and lovers of knowledge make mathematics into readable culture, not only expert craft.
- patronage/prestige can coexist with any of these, but it is not the same thing as audience.

The important reading here is that social arenas do not merely add names to title pages. They reshape what can count as intellectual value. Professional/practical and military settings tend to intensify utility, method, visual/material aids, and composite apparatus. Patronage often travels with canonical authority and prestige, but it must be handled separately from practical readership.

The social story may be chronological, but probably not as a simple rise of practicality. A safer hypothesis is that title pages increasingly multiply the social worlds attached to mathematical books: school, court, professional office, learned institution, public reader, patron.

## Social Claims We Need To Keep Separate

1. **Audience**: who the book says it is for.
2. **Office/credential**: why the maker is entitled to speak.
3. **Institution**: where knowledge is authorized or taught.
4. **Patronage**: who protects, sponsors, receives, or dignifies the book.
5. **Public culture**: when the title page addresses readers, lovers, amateurs, or the curious.

If we collapse these, the analysis will become too blunt. A title page dedicated to a prince, written by a royal mathematician, for learners or practitioners, is not making one social claim. It is stacking several social claims that may pull the intellectual rhetoric in different directions.

## Stronger Social Hypotheses

1. **Mathematical authority is socially distributed.** It can come from ancient authors, offices, institutions, professions, patrons, or publics.
2. **Practicality is often a social claim before it is a subject label.** A geometry book becomes practical when tied to land surveyors, engineers, soldiers, merchants, instruments, or exercises.
3. **Pedagogy is not confined to students.** General readers, professionals, and courtly users also receive books framed through clarity, order, figures, and method.
4. **Patronage is not readership.** It often marks hierarchy and prestige, while audience marks imagined use.
5. **The absence of a visible social arena is meaningful.** It often points back to canonical textual identity, book-trade identity, or scholarly authority carried without explicit audience.
