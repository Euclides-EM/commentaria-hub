# Phase 16 Dutch Plain `1-6` As Practical-Vernacular Route

Question:

Do Dutch plain `1-6` Elements editions form a practical-vernacular pathway distinct from the later `1-6 + 11-12` practical-pedagogical package?

This phase is corpus-internal and title-page-based. It focuses on the metadata-defined Elements corpus and compares Dutch plain `1-6` editions with:

- non-Dutch plain `1-6`;
- `1-6 + 11-12`;
- nearby Dutch non-Elements practical geometry / surveying / measurement books.

## Main Result

Yes: Dutch plain `1-6` looks like a distinct practical-vernacular route.

It reaches almost the same practical/public rate as the `1-6 + 11-12` package:

- Dutch plain `1-6`: 60.0%;
- `1-6 + 11-12`: 59.0%.

But it gets there differently.

`1-6 + 11-12` becomes practical by adding solid geometry and institutional/practical publics, especially in later French/English/Spanish/Latin contexts.

Dutch plain `1-6`, especially Dou, becomes practical by making the first six books into a vernacular, explained, corrected, augmented, public-facing, land-measurer geometry.

## Dou As Route, Not Just Author

The Dou title pages repeatedly combine:

- the first six books of Euclid;
- Dutch translation: `Overgeset` / `Overgheset`;
- explanation and exposition: `verclaert`, `uytgheleyt`;
- revision/correction: `oversien`, `verbetert`;
- augmentation: `vermeerdert`, `bygevoeght`;
- audience: eager lovers of the free art;
- professional identity: city land-surveyor and wine-gauger of Leiden;
- added utilities from the books;
- species in geometrical figures: making, changing, joining, subtracting, multiplying, dividing.

This is not sparse elementary Euclid. It is a worked, public, vernacular, professionalized Euclid.

## Jacob Willemsz And Coets

Jacob Willemsz cases are more stripped down:

- translated into Dutch;
- first six books;
- principles of mathematics/geometry;
- Haarlem identity.

They support the vernacular-transfer side of the route, but not the full Dou-style practical apparatus.

Henrick Coets, `Amsterdam_1702`, adds demonstration and Leiden mathematical lectureship:

- first six books;
- demonstrated;
- lecturer in mathematics at Leiden.

This keeps the Dutch plain `1-6` route pedagogical, but with a more institutional/academic face than Dou's land-measuring one.

## Difference From `1-6 + 11-12`

Both routes make Euclid usable, but they do so differently.

### `1-6 + 11-12`

Typical profile:

- later, especially after 1650;
- more English/French/Spanish and also Latin;
- often institutional, Jesuit, military, academy, or course-oriented;
- practical/public through added solid geometry;
- more ancient-authority saturated;
- less vernacular/translation-marked in the title-page features.

### Dutch plain `1-6`

Typical profile:

- Dutch, mostly Dou;
- first six books only;
- practical/public without adding books 11-12;
- public of eager lovers of the free art;
- civic/professional land-surveyor identity;
- translation/explanation/exposition;
- utilities extracted from the books;
- geometrical operations and figures;
- repeated correction/revision/augmentation.

## Historical Interpretation

Dutch plain `1-6` is not merely an abbreviated Elements and not just a sparse elementary school text.

It is a practical-vernacular Euclid:

**plane geometry made public, teachable, and operational for lovers, learners, and measuring practitioners through Dutch translation, explanation, correction, added utilities, and land-surveyor authority.**

This route lets Euclid live in the same ecology as surveying, land-measuring, gauging, instruments, and practical geometry, without needing the `1-6 + 11-12` solid-geometry package.

## How This Changes The Argument

Earlier:

`1-6 + 11-12` looked like the major practical-pedagogical Elements form.

Now:

There are at least two practical Elements pathways:

1. **Dutch/Dou plain `1-6`**: vernacular, civic-measuring, public lovers/practitioners, operational plane geometry.
2. **Later `1-6 + 11-12`**: institutional/military/academy/course-oriented usable Euclid, often via solid geometry.

This matters because the place of the Elements in the broader ecology is not one-dimensional. Euclid becomes practical in multiple ways.

## Best Slide-Level Claim

**The practical Elements did not require adding solid geometry. In the Dutch Dou route, the first six books alone became usable geometry through translation, explanation, correction, added utilities, and the authority of the land-surveyor.**
