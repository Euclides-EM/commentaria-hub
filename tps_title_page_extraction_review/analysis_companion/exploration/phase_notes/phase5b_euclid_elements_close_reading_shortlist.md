## Case Notes

### `Venice_1482`: Sparse canonical incunable Euclid
- Metadata: 1482, Venice, LATIN; primary classes: Theoretical Mathematics.
- Base/content: Elementorum Euclidis megarensis
- Elements designation: Elementorum Euclidis megarensis
- Euclid reference: Euclidis
- Title-page claims: Elementorum Euclidis megarensis | in artem geometrie incipit | Elementorum Euclidis megarensis | Euclidis megarensis | Euclidis
- Existing translation note: The most renowned work, The Elements by Euclid of Megara, begins most auspiciously in the art of geometry.
- Why it matters: A baseline case: Euclid as recognizable ancient/textual authority. Useful for showing that some title pages need very little social or pedagogical rhetoric.

## Interpretive Payoff

The old “base designation is consistent” claim can become a better argument: Euclid/Elements is recognizable enough to travel, but flexible enough to be recomposed. Its title-page identity can be sparse, school-oriented, professional, useful, humanist, composite, institutional, or even displaced into non-Euclidean elemental vocabularies.
