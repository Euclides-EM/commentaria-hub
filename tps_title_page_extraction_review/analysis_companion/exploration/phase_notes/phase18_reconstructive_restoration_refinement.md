# Phase 18 Reconstructive Restoration Refinement

Question:

Is the reconstructive pole really opposed to ancient restoration, or is it another kind of restoration: restoring Euclid's logic, method, order, demonstrative force, or pedagogical intelligibility rather than restoring the ancient text philologically?

## Short Answer

Yes: the better model is not opposition, but competing restoration ideals.

The reconstructive cases often do not reject Euclid. They claim to recover, clarify, reform, reorder, or make usable what Euclid truly is supposed to do: demonstrate, order, teach, and make geometry intelligible.

So the contrast is:

- **philological restoration**: restore Euclid's text, Greekness, ancient authority, freedom from corruption, fidelity to transmission;
- **logical/pedagogical restoration**: restore Euclid's demonstrative logic, order, accessibility, teachability, or applicability;
- **practical restoration**: restore Euclid's usefulness for measuring, military work, engineering, mathematical practice, or institutional curricula.

This makes `Paris_1667` less "anti-Euclid" than "real Euclid by other means."

## Chronology

The strongest reconstructive cases are mostly later.

Strict strong cases:

| case | year | language | profile |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `Paris_1667` | 1667 | French | new Elements, new order, new demonstrations, new means/measures/ways |
| `Livorno_1709` | 1709 | Latin | `Euclides reformatus`, new method, clearer/easier/firmer demonstration |

New order + new demonstration cases:

| case | year | language | profile |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `London_1685a` | 1685 | English | Dechales/Reeve Williams, new/easy method, use of every proposition |
| `London_1703` | 1703 | English | same Dechales/Reeve Williams route |
| `473J72` | 1726 | English | new, plain, easy method |

Broader reordered/new-demonstration cases cluster especially in 1650-1699:

- 13/18 reordered/new-demonstration cases are 1650-1699.
- 2/18 are 1700+.
- Earlier examples exist, but they are rarer and often more local/specific.

Interpretation:

The reconstructive/restorative-by-method pattern intensifies after the mid-seventeenth century, but it has earlier roots in selection, ordering, and contraction.

## Language

The strongest strict cases are not simply vernacular:

- `Paris_1667`: French;
- `Livorno_1709`: Latin.

But the broader new-order/new-demonstration field is strongly vernacular or translingual:

- English: Dechales/Reeve Williams, Hill, Jonas Moore;
- German: Reyher, Burckhard von Pirckenstein;
- French: Arnauld, Le Tenneur, König/Kuypers;
- Italian: Viviani;
- Latin remains important, especially for institutional or scholarly reform.

Interpretation:

Reconstructive rhetoric is not reducible to vernacularization. But vernacular settings make reconstruction especially visible, because title pages must explain why this version is newly accessible, easy, ordered, or useful.

## Practical / Public Connection

The reconstructive pole often touches practical/public settings, but not always.

Examples with practical/public force:

- `Livorno_1709`: practical-pedagogical, university/mechanics professor, clearer/easier/firmer demonstration.
- `London_1685a` / `London_1703`: `1-6 + 11-12`, uses of every proposition in all parts of mathematics.
- `London_1680-81`: symbolic demonstrations, surveyor general / Royal Society context.
- `Vienna_1694` and `Lübeck_and_Frankfurt_1699`: German practical publics: generals, engineers, builders, artists, craftsmen.
- `Paris_1640`: easier demonstrations, reduced propositions, French scientific explanation.

Examples less directly practical:

- `Paris_1667`: pedagogical/method, but no practical/public flag.
- `Cologne_1556`: ordered selected propositions for demonstration, but socially quiet.
- `Leiden_1692`: new demonstrations, but socially quiet in the current title-page features.

Interpretation:

Reconstruction is connected to pedagogy more consistently than to practical application. Practicality appears when "new order/method" is tied to use, professions, military/engineering audiences, or proposition-utility.

## Audience / Social Location

The reconstructive cases do not have one audience.

Several routes appear:

1. **Pedagogical youth/student route**
   - `The_Hague_1758`: first six books in a new order, for youth.
   - `Paris_1640`: French explanation of sciences and easier demonstrations.

2. **Institutional professor route**
   - `Livorno_1709`: professor of mechanical sciences at Pisa.
   - `Wittenberg_1661`: professor in Academia Leucorea.
   - `Kiel_and_Leipzig_1699`: university professor.

3. **Practical/technical route**
   - `Vienna_1694`: generals, engineers, builders, artists, craftsmen.
   - `London_1680-81`: surveyor general / Royal Society / mathematical school context.

4. **Quiet method route**
   - `Paris_1667`: socially quiet, strongly intellectual/methodological.
   - `Cologne_1556`: socially quiet, proposition/order focused.

Interpretation:

Reconstructive rhetoric is not one social world. It is a portable intellectual claim that can serve several publics: students, professors, technical users, or abstract method-minded readers.

## Does It Restore "The Real Euclid"?

This is the most promising formulation.

The reconstructive pole often implies:

- the received order is not the best order;
- ordinary demonstrations are not the easiest or clearest;
- propositions can be selected, reduced, reordered, or re-demonstrated;
- the value of Euclid lies in demonstrative structure, not merely textual inheritance;
- a modern editor can be faithful to Euclid's rational force by altering the presentation.

This is restoration of function rather than restoration of wording.

Useful phrase:

**logical restoration**

or:

**pedagogical restoration of Euclid's demonstrative order**

This sits beside, not simply against, philological restoration.

## Three Restoration Ideals

### 1. Textual / Philological Restoration

Goal:

Recover the authentic ancient Euclid.

Title-page vocabulary:

- Greek/Latin;
- ancient scholia;
- Theon;
- corrected/castigated/emended;
- restored;
- purged of corruptions;
- fidelity to ancient sources.

### 2. Demonstrative / Logical Restoration

Goal:

Recover the rational order, demonstrative clarity, or true method of geometry.

Title-page vocabulary:

- new order;
- new demonstrations;
- clearer/easier/firmer method;
- propositions selected and ordered for demonstration;
- reduced proposition count;
- algebraic signs;
- symbolic demonstration.

### 3. Pedagogical / Practical Restoration

Goal:

Make Euclid function for learners, technical users, institutions, or publics.

Title-page vocabulary:

- easy method;
- use of each proposition;
- for youth;
- for generals, engineers, builders, craftsmen;
- for all parts of mathematics;
- practical/public audiences.

## Relation To The Conference Argument

This gives a stronger intellectual argument:

The Elements is not a stable ancient object passively transmitted. It is a canonical structure that editors repeatedly claim to restore in different ways.

Some restore the text.

Some restore the logic.

Some restore the use.

This helps integrate the social and intellectual halves:

- social setting explains which restoration ideal matters;
- intellectual ideal explains why the same canonical corpus can be corrected, translated, reordered, contracted, or reformed.

## Best Cases For This Refinement

1. `Paris_1667`: logical/pedagogical restoration through new order and new demonstrations.
2. `Livorno_1709`: reform of Euclid through clearer/easier/firmer method.
3. `Cologne_1556`: selected propositions ordered for demonstration.
4. `Paris_1640`: tenth book reduced and newly demonstrated for French scientific explanation.
5. `Kiel_and_Leipzig_1699`: algebraic signs as restoration/retooling of proof portability.
6. `Vienna_1694`: new/easy German Euclid for technical users.
7. `London_1685a`: hybrid case where new/easy method coexists with correction/augmentation/translation.

## Provisional Claim

The reconstructive pole is not simply anti-philological. It offers a rival restoration ideal: Euclid is restored not by recovering the ancient words, but by recovering the demonstrative order, clarity, and pedagogical force that the Elements ought to have.

This rival restoration ideal becomes especially visible from the mid-seventeenth century onward, often in vernacular or translingual settings and often near pedagogical or practical publics, but it is not limited to vernacular print or practical audiences.
