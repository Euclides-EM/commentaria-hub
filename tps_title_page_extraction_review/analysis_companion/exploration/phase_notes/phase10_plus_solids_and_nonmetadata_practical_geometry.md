# Phase 10 `1-6 + 11-12` And Neighboring Non-Elements Practical Geometry

This pass asks whether the metadata Elements `1-6 + 11-12` package belongs mainly inside the Elements corpus, or whether it sits at a fuzzy border with non-Elements practical geometry.

Comparison groups:

- `1-6 + 11-12` metadata Elements: 39 representatives.
- Non-metadata practical geometry: 260 representatives.
- Non-metadata military/applied mathematics: 89 representatives.
- Non-metadata practical-or-military pool for nearest-neighbor search: 296 representatives.

Similarity scores compare social/intellectual/subject behavior and intentionally exclude Euclid-specific identity fields, so the score is not just detecting the word Euclid.

## Core Reading

`1-6 + 11-12` is a bridge, but not by becoming ordinary practical geometry.

Compared with non-Elements practical geometry, it is much more Euclidean-institutional: more procedural/pedagogical identity, method/demonstration/order, religious-institutional authority, theoretical mathematics, ease/accessibility, learned/scholarly framing, school framing, correction, and professor/lecturer authority.

Compared with non-Elements practical geometry, it is not especially more utilitarian: utility/practice/application is almost equal. Non-Elements practical geometry is stronger in explicit Practical Geometry classification, architecture, perspective, visual/material aids, professional/practical arenas, and no-visible-social-arena cases.

So the bridge is asymmetric:

- From the Elements side, `1-6 + 11-12` moves Euclid toward use, publicness, military/academy settings, and practical mathematics.
- From the practical-geometry side, the nearest neighbors are the practical works that borrow Euclidean language, theoretical/practical pairing, solids, method, institutional authority, or learned apparatus.

## Interpretation

The `1-6 + 11-12` package is not absorbed into practical geometry. It retains a distinctive Elements profile: canonical/theoretical identity, method, demonstration, ease, correction, and institutional authority. But it is exactly the kind of Elements edition that can touch practical geometry because it adds solid geometry and advertises use, publicness, military relevance, and method.

The neighboring non-Elements books confirm the same ecology from the other side. Practical geometry books become closest to `1-6 + 11-12` when they do at least one of the following:

1. invoke Euclid or Elements language;
2. join theoretical and practical geometry;
3. emphasize planes and solids;
4. address military, engineering, architectural, or mathematical publics;
5. use institutional or professorial authority;
6. promise method, demonstration, correction, or ease.

This gives us a better boundary model: not Elements versus practical geometry, but a gradient from canonical Euclid to usable Euclid to Euclidean practical geometry to professional/material practical arts.

## Strong Cases For Close Reading

Most bridge-like:

- `bib-9` (1544, Strasbourg): practical geometry of lengths, planes, solids, and mechanics, explicitly corollary from demonstrated Euclidean elements.
- `bib-75` / `bib-31` (1646, Zurich): theoretical and practical geometry, surveying, instruments, addressed to a broad/all audience and tied to a military captain.
- `bib-110` (1692, Paris): Elements of geometry drawing from Euclid and Archimedes, with Oratorian authority.
- `bib-124` (1624, Rome): practical geometry addressed to mathematicians, architects, geographers, cosmographers, bombardiers, engineers, and similar users.
- `London_1700`: practical geometry/surveying using selected Euclid books, including 11 and 12, by a teacher of mathematics.
- `35H84Y` (1642, Bologna): applied/mechanical-mathematical composite addressed to philosophers, mathematicians, physicists, anatomists, military men, machinists, architects, and painters.

Best contrast cases:

- Visual-artisan geometry/perspective books show practical geometry without the same Euclidean-institutional profile.
- Many non-Elements practical geometry books are socially silent or simply subject-labeled, unlike the method-heavy `1-6 + 11-12` package.

## Provisional Claim

`1-6 + 11-12` is a bridge form between canonical Elements and practical geometry, but it bridges from the side of Euclidean authority. It does not become a trade manual. It makes Euclid usable by joining first principles, solids, method, institutional authority, and selected practical publics.

## Caveats

- Similarity is exploratory and feature-based; it points to close-reading candidates rather than proving direct influence.
- Some nearest neighbors are non-metadata Elements-like works, so the boundary between metadata Elements and title-page Euclid/Elements language remains important.
- Practical geometry is internally diverse: visual-artisan, military, surveying, instrumental, and learned-theoretical cases do not behave the same way.
