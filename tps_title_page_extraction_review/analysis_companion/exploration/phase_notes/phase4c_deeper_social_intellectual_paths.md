These archetypes are more useful for argument-building than a flat feature list:

- **Composite workshop book**: knowledge appears as accumulated apparatus: notes, tables, additions, figures, bound-with texts, comments, practical extracts.
- **Humanist transfer book**: ancient or authoritative knowledge is moved through language, edition, recovery, or translation.
- **Method/access book**: value is claimed through order, demonstration, ease, clarity, or learnability.
- **Utility public book**: intellectual value is tied to use and to a named or implied public.
- **Sparse canonical identity**: the title page asks canonical identity to do the work; it says little about audience or pedagogical promise.
- **Procedural pedagogical identity**: canonical identity remains important, but is surrounded by method, language, additions, use, visual aids, or correction.

Absence may become a major argument. The corpus contains many places where title pages do **not** name an explicit audience, institution, correction, translation, utility, or method. For canonical Euclidean/Elements-like works, this silence is not necessarily emptiness. It can mean that authority is carried by author, title, textual identity, format, or the book trade rather than by explicit pedagogical promise.

## Deeper Historical Claims To Test

1. **Mathematical title pages do not simply classify subjects; they stage acts of transmission.** The recurring verbs and claims are about moving, correcting, ordering, augmenting, demonstrating, extracting, and applying knowledge.
2. **The social and intellectual stories are independent but mutually explanatory.** Institutions, credentials, audiences, and patrons do not map one-to-one onto subjects; they modulate what kind of value a title page can plausibly claim.
3. **Euclid/Elements is not a stable base designation but a mobile authority.** It can appear as canonical identity, school geometry, vernacular practical geometry, humanist recovery, or as one component in a larger apparatus.
4. **Composite publication is an intellectual ideal, not just clutter.** Additions, tables, figures, notes, comments, and bound-with texts signal that mathematics is useful when rearranged and furnished.
5. **Pedagogy often appears without schools.** Ease, order, demonstration, brevity, figures, and exercises can encode pedagogical ideals even when no formal student audience is named.
6. **Some title pages suppress use and audience because they do not need them.** Sparse canonical title pages may rely on textual authority, ancient names, and edition lineage instead.
