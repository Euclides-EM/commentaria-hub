## Reading

The hypothesis mostly holds. The `1-6 + 11-12` package is not simply an abbreviated Elements. It is unusually associated with:

- institutional authority;
- method/demonstration/order;
- accessibility/ease;
- explicit utility/practice;
- practical/public positioning;
- military/academy/engineering settings;
- learned/religious/professorial authority.

The solids books seem to matter because they let the edition remain recognizably Euclidean while expanding toward useful geometry, institutional teaching, military academies, and applied mathematical practice.

But the package is not one clean sub-corpus. It has several local styles:

1. **Dechales/easy-use style**: English/French editions organized around easy method and uses of every proposition.
2. **Tacquet/Archimedes apparatus style**: Latin institutional editions with Archimedes, corollaries, diagrams, and professorial/Jesuit authority.
3. **Military/academy style**: Woolwich, royal/military academy, artillery, war, engineers, generals, builders.
4. **Sparse/reduced style**: a few editions retain the book configuration without much explicit practical or pedagogical rhetoric.

## Provisional Claim

`1-6 + 11-12` is a **flexible pedagogical package**: it gives readers elementary plane geometry plus enough solid geometry to make Euclid useful in institutional, practical, and military/technical contexts. It does not replace the full Elements; it repackages Euclidean authority for teachable and usable mathematics.

## Caveats

- Several cases are reprint/translation clusters around Dechales/Tacquet-like packages, so we should avoid treating every edition as wholly independent.
- The category is multilingual and geographically broad, but English and French cases are especially rhetorically loud in our title-page features; this specific metadata book-coverage group has no Dutch representatives.
- The package should be tested through close reading against full/near-complete editions and plain six-book editions.
