# Phase 5 Euclid And Elements Mini-Atlas

This pass tests the user suspicion directly: Euclid/Elements is not a single stable base designation. It is a moving title-page resource. Sometimes it names a canonical ancient text; sometimes it anchors school geometry; sometimes it is made useful, easy, corrected, translated, excerpted, or furnished with apparatus; sometimes "elements" language travels beyond explicit Euclid.

The numbers below are representative-level unless otherwise noted. Reprints inherit the representative classification. The "elements without Euclid" bucket excludes rows with explicit `references_to_euclid`/`description_of_euclid` evidence and also normalizes split forms such as `EV- clidis`.

## Preliminary Interpretation

1. **Euclid/Elements is not stable; it is reusable.** The same authority can support canonical textual identity, pedagogy, practical utility, translation, correction, and composite apparatus.
2. **The old claim should be inverted.** Rather than saying base designation is highly consistent, we can say that title-page identity is powerful because it can remain recognizable while being recomposed for different social and intellectual settings.
3. **Sparse canonical cases matter.** They show one mode of authority: title, ancient author, and textual identity carry the burden without explicit social or pedagogical promises.
4. **Procedural Euclid matters just as much.** Other title pages wrap Euclid in ease, method, figures, use, correction, translation, notes, and additions.
5. **"Elements" language exceeds explicit Euclid.** Some books use elemental/fundamental vocabulary without direct Euclidean reference, suggesting a broader pedagogical grammar of mathematical beginnings.

## Next Close-Reading Task

Compare three bundles:

- sparse canonical Euclid/Elements title pages;
- procedural/composite Euclid title pages;
- non-Euclidean or weakly Euclidean "elements/fundamentals/principles" title pages.

For each bundle, choose one ordinary case and one edge case. That will let the talk say something robust without pretending the corpus is cleaner than it is.
