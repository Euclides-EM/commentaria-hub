# Phase 9 Plain `1-6` Versus `1-6 + 11-12` Elements

This pass tests the nearest alternative to Phase 8. If `1-6 + 11-12` looks practical-pedagogical, is that because books 11-12 matter, or because short Elements editions in general already behave that way?

Corpus slices:

- Plain `1-6`: 81 representative metadata Elements works.
- `1-6 + 11-12`: 39 representative metadata Elements works.

## Core Reading

The two packages are related, but not interchangeable.

Plain `1-6` is the broader and older short-Elements form. It appears from the sixteenth century onward, has a large Dutch/Latin component, and often works through translation, explanation, institutional/composite framing, or vernacular access. It can be practical, especially in the Dutch Dou-style six-book editions, but it is also often quiet, textual, school-oriented, or composite.

`1-6 + 11-12` is later and more sharply pointed. In the current metadata corpus it is essentially a post-1650 phenomenon. It is much more associated with practical/public positioning, utility, ease, military or academy contexts, religious institutional authority, and method/access rhetoric. The solids books seem to change the package from elementary Euclid alone into elementary Euclid plus usable solid geometry.

But the practical and military-public signal survives, and even becomes clearer in the remaining cases. That means the package should not be narrated as one single Dechales/Tacquet phenomenon. It has at least two centers: a Jesuit/method/easy-use center and a military/academy/practical-public center.

## Historical Interpretation

Plain `1-6` and `1-6 + 11-12` probably mark two different ways of making a partial Elements.

1. Plain `1-6` often means **elementary Euclid**: a foundation course, a vernacular transfer object, a school or readerly text, or a short canonical object. It is flexible and older.
2. `1-6 + 11-12` often means **usable Euclid**: enough plane geometry to teach the foundations, plus enough solid geometry to support military, academy, architectural, engineering, and practical mathematical contexts.
3. The boundary is fuzzy. Plain `1-6` can be practical, especially in Dutch Dou-style editions, and `1-6 + 11-12` can be sparse. But the center of gravity differs.

This is stronger than saying that the Elements is simply abbreviated. Different abridgments perform different work.

## Provisional Claim

`1-6` is an elementary-canonical package; `1-6 + 11-12` is a practical-pedagogical package. The addition of books 11-12 does not merely add more Euclid. It changes what a partial Elements can promise on the title page: from first principles alone toward usable geometry for institutions, academies, military settings, and public mathematical practice.

## Caveats

- The `1-6 + 11-12` pattern is partly cluster-driven, especially by Dechales/Tacquet for ease, method, correction, and Jesuit/institutional rhetoric.
- Plain `1-6` is internally diverse and includes practical-public cases; it should not be treated as purely canonical or non-practical.
- The comparison uses title-page features and metadata book coverage, so close reading should verify the best examples before using them as slide cases.
