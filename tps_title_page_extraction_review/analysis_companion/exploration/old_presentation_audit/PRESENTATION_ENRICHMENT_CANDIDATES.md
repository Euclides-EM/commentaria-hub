# Arguments From The New Analysis That Can Enrich The Old Presentation

This note maps newer findings onto the old presentation's natural structure. It is not a slide draft; it is a menu of arguments that can be integrated without breaking the old presentation's flow.

## Where The Old Presentation Is Strongest

The old presentation's strongest frame is still usable:

> Title pages present the *Elements* as a canonical text in motion.

The new analysis strengthens this frame with larger denominators and sharper distinctions:

- 286 metadata-defined Elements representatives.
- 557 non-Elements representatives as surrounding ecology.
- 843 representative title-page rows total.
- Pre-1700 like-for-like subset: 243 Elements and 541 non-Elements.

## Add This Near "A Canonical Text In Motion"

### New argument: strong identity, variable designation

This directly replaces the old "base designation is highly consistent" claim.

**Claim to add:**

The *Elements* has a strong public identity, but not a uniform title-page designation. The stability is an authority signal, not a fixed wording.

**Evidence:**

- Explicit Euclid/book identity: 86.7% in metadata Elements vs 26.6% in non-Elements.
- `elements_designation_has`: 84.3% of metadata Elements.
- But 241 non-empty Elements designation rows contain 189 unique strings.
- Euclid/Elements language also appears in 78 non-Elements representatives, 14.0% of the non-Elements corpus.

**Why it fits:**

It preserves the old "canon" point but avoids flattening the corpus. It also gives a more interesting historical result: canonical identity is strong precisely because it tolerates many title-page forms.

### New argument: the Elements is a corpus, not one package

**Claim to add:**

The title-page *Elements* is not only one ancient work repeatedly printed. It is repackaged through different book coverages and functions.

**Evidence:**

- First six books: method/order 48.1%, demonstrations 24.7%, translation 21.0%, utility 6.2%.
- First six plus solid geometry: method/order 64.1%, propositions 35.9%, utility 28.2%, Jesuit 33.3%.
- Near-complete/expanded: translation 41.8%, augmentation 55.2%, demonstrations 34.3%, scholia/commentary 23.9%, utility 1.5%.

**Why it fits:**

It gives the old "text in motion" claim a concrete internal structure.

## Add This Near "Local Roots And Cross-European Circulation"

### New argument: use controlled locality, not author bibliographies

**Claim to add:**

The corpus is complete for the metadata-defined Elements corpus, including reprints currently identified in the metadata, but it is not a complete bibliography for every author or for all mathematical print. Therefore, local-root claims should be tested through controlled city/language/format/period comparisons rather than same-author career claims.

**Evidence:**

- Sparse authoritative presentation is strongly associated with city: Cramer's V = 0.487.
- It is also associated with language: Cramer's V = 0.306.
- Format and period matter more modestly: Cramer's V = 0.179 and 0.165.

**Why it fits:**

It keeps the local/cross-European frame but makes it methodologically stronger and more honest.

### New argument: Euclidean authority travels outside Elements editions

**Claim to add:**

The metadata-defined Elements corpus is distinctive, but Euclidean functions travel into practical geometry, mixed mathematical systems, visual arts, and reformed geometries.

**Evidence:**

- 78 non-Elements representatives, 14.0% of non-Elements, have Euclid-or-Elements language.
- Among these 78: ancient authority/restoration 70.5%, method/order 32.1%, utility/practice 21.8%, visual aids 25.6%.
- Provisional types: practical geometry/measurement 18; mixed mathematical systems 18; selected Euclidean extracts 5; new/reformed Elements-like geometry 4; visual/spatial arts 4.

**Why it fits:**

This is probably one of the best new enrichments. It complicates the old presentation productively: the *Elements* has a corpus boundary, but Euclidean authority has ecological mobility.

## Add This Near "Institutional Affiliations And Mentions"

### New argument: social authority is often indirect

**Claim to add:**

Elements title pages do not usually name occupational readers directly. Their social life often appears through editors, translators, professors, Jesuits, patrons, institutions, and apparatus.

**Evidence:**

- Elements: universities/academies 1.7%, students 3.5%, military users 1.0%, surveyors/engineers 0.0%.
- Elements: Jesuit 8.7%, math professor/lecturer 9.1%, named private patron 22.0%, has any deep social 44.8%.
- Non-Elements have stronger direct practical utility: utility/practice/application 17.8% vs 7.7% Elements.

**Why it fits:**

It keeps the old social-history ambition but makes the argument more precise. The title page does not only say "for whom"; it also says "by what social authority."

### New argument: Jesuit is a route, not the whole social map

**Claim to add:**

Jesuit identity is a significant sub-route in the Elements ecology, especially in certain book packages and formats, but not the dominant social condition of the whole corpus.

**Evidence:**

- Jesuit markers: 8.7% Elements vs 2.3% non-Elements.
- First-six-plus-solid-geometry Elements: Jesuit 33.3%.
- Duodecimo Elements: Jesuit 26.7%.
- Language rates in Elements: Latin 11.5%, French 9.4%, English 15.0%, Dutch/German/Italian 0.0% in current data.

**Why it fits:**

It preserves the Jesuit observation while avoiding overclaim.

## Add This Near "Between Restoration And Adaptation"

### New argument: adaptation often presents itself as fidelity

**Claim to add:**

Restoration and adaptation are not simply opposed. Many adaptations claim to restore Euclid more truly: by restoring text, order, demonstration, clarity, teachability, or use.

**Evidence:**

- Ancient authority/restoration: 83.2% Elements vs 12.0% non-Elements.
- Practical-pedagogical Elements: ancient authority 95.8%, explicit identity 100.0%, utility 30.6%, access/pedagogy 62.5%.
- Reconstructive candidates often retain ancient authority: method/ease/retooling 93.1%; selected/reduced/contracted 96.4%; reordered/new demonstrations 83.3%.
- Strong "new Elements" pole is rare: 2 cases, `Paris_1667` and `Livorno_1709`.

**Why it fits:**

This is a direct upgrade of the old "restoration vs adaptation" section. It turns a binary tension into a historically richer question: which Euclid is being restored?

### New argument: mathematical parts reveal what title pages think Euclid is made of

**Claim to add:**

Elements title pages are distinctive in the mathematical parts they advertise: demonstrations, scholia/commentary, propositions, principles, and theorems. Non-Elements books more often foreground operations, problems, and examples.

**Evidence:**

- Any named mathematical/deductive part: 57.7% Elements vs 34.8% non-Elements.
- Demonstrations/proofs: 21.0% vs 7.5%.
- Scholia/commentary: 13.6% vs 6.5%.
- Propositions: 10.1% vs 4.5%.
- Operations/constructions: 1.0% vs 4.1%.
- Problems: 1.4% vs 3.8%.

**Why it fits:**

This would enrich the intellectual analysis a lot. It moves from values like "clarity" and "utility" to the internal advertised anatomy of mathematical knowledge.

## Add This Near "Conclusions: Plurality And Variety"

### New argument: boundary plus mobility

**Claim to add:**

The metadata Elements corpus has a real boundary, but Euclidean functions are mobile. Euclid can be restored, translated, taught, selected, applied, excerpted, imitated, and used as a foundation outside the Elements corpus.

**Evidence:**

- Elements is distinctive: ancient authority 83.2% vs 12.0%; explicit identity 86.7% vs 26.6%; method/order 42.0% vs 16.3%.
- But 78 non-Elements cases have Euclid/Elements language.
- Bridge routes: 31 usable Elements cases; 67 Euclidean practical geometry cases outside metadata Elements; 126 professional/material practical arts cases.
- Usable Elements often remains canonical: 17 of 31 usable Elements cases also belong to the restored/authoritative route.
- Euclidean practical geometry overlaps with professional/material practical arts: 43 of 67 cases.

**Why it fits:**

This is probably the best final-conclusion upgrade. It turns "plurality and variety" into a structured ecology.

## Suggested Presentation Restructure

1. Keep the old opening: why Euclid, why title pages, why DH/paradata.
2. Update the corpus slide: old 219 Elements-only corpus becomes metadata Elements + broader ecology.
3. Replace "Base designation is highly consistent" with "strong identity, variable designation."
4. Add one slide: "The Elements corpus is internally repackaged."
5. Add one slide: "Euclid outside the Elements."
6. Rewrite institutional section as "Social authority: readers, offices, institutions, patrons."
7. Rewrite restoration/adaptation as "Which Euclid is being restored?"
8. End with "A bounded corpus with mobile functions."

## Best One-Sentence Upgrade

Old:

> The title pages present the Elements as a canonical text in motion.

New:

> The title pages present the *Elements* as a bounded canonical corpus whose authority made it unusually available for mediation, while Euclidean methods and authority also traveled beyond the corpus into practical, institutional, visual, and reconstructive mathematical print.
