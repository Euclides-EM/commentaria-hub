# Jesuit-Marked Non-Elements Compared With Jesuit-Marked Elements

This note asks whether the distinctive profile of Jesuit-marked Elements editions also appears in Jesuit-marked non-Elements mathematical books.

Scope: title-page feature matrix, to 1705.

| Group | Count |
|---|---:|
| Elements: Jesuit-marked | 24 |
| Elements: other | 235 |
| Non-Elements: Jesuit-marked | 17 |
| Non-Elements: other | 533 |

Important caveat: "Jesuit-marked" means that the title-page classification makes the Jesuit affiliation visible through `inst_jesuit` or `jesuit_religious`. It is not a complete census of all Jesuit-authored or Jesuit-used books.

## Main Finding

The Jesuit-marked non-Elements books partly match the Elements pattern, but they do not simply reproduce it.

Shared Jesuit pattern:

- institution is always visible by definition in the marked group;
- practical utility is elevated;
- augmentation/composition is elevated;
- Latin is overrepresented compared with other non-Elements books;
- student/school language is more visible than in comparable non-Jesuit books.

Elements-specific Jesuit pattern:

- method/order is much stronger in Jesuit-marked Elements;
- correction/revision is much stronger in Jesuit-marked Elements;
- books 1-6 plus solids is an Elements-specific packaging pattern;
- observed multi-region print reach is strongest for Jesuit-marked Elements named lines;
- translation is absent from Jesuit-marked Elements title pages, while also low but not absent in Jesuit non-Elements.

Non-Elements-specific Jesuit pattern:

- subject matter is much more applied and diverse;
- visual/spatial arts, instruments/measurement, mechanics/military, music, astronomy/navigation, and mixed mathematical works are prominent;
- practical utility is even higher than in Jesuit-marked Elements;
- student/school language is more visible than in Jesuit-marked Elements.

## Subject Families

Jesuit-marked Elements remain, unsurprisingly, mostly in Geometry/Theory or no additional primary subject family.

Jesuit-marked non-Elements are much more dispersed:

| Subject family | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Visual/Spatial Arts | 3 / 17 | 17.6% |
| Applied Mechanics/Military | 2 / 17 | 11.8% |
| Geometry/Theory | 2 / 17 | 11.8% |
| Geometry/Theory + Arithmetic/Commerce + Music | 2 / 17 | 11.8% |
| Instruments/Measurement | 2 / 17 | 11.8% |
| Music | 1 / 17 | 5.9% |
| Cosmos/Earth | 1 / 17 | 5.9% |
| Visual/Spatial Arts + Applied Mechanics/Military | 1 / 17 | 5.9% |

Interpretation: outside the Elements, Jesuit-marked mathematical books are not primarily about preserving or methodizing Euclid. They are spread across applied mathematical arts: perspective, optics, instruments, mechanics, astronomy/navigation, music, and mixed mathematical compendia.

- `ZGOHUR` / Rome 1581 / Clavius, *Gnomonices libri octo*: instruments/measurement; method/order.
- `35H84Y` / Bologna 1642 / Mario Bettini, *Apiaria universae philosophiae mathematicae*: mechanics; practical; method/order; demonstrations.
- `AWKSO4` / Paris 1642 / Jean Du Breuil, *La perspective practique*: visual/spatial arts; practical.

- `bib-86` and `bib-85` / Bologna 1664, 1685 / Paolo Casati: instruments/arithmetic; practical and method/order.
- `EJGRZN` / Paris 1671 / Pardies, *Élémens de géométrie*: geometry/theory; method/order; adjacent to the "new Elements" question.

## How It Matches The Elements Pattern

The match is strongest at the level of **institutional mathematical usefulness**:

- Jesuit-marked Elements: practical 29.2% vs 5.1% in other Elements.
- Jesuit-marked non-Elements: practical 41.2% vs 16.7% in other non-Elements.

In both corpora, Jesuit-marked books are more likely to present mathematics as usable, organized, and institutionally authorized.

## How It Differs

The Elements case is more specifically about **methodizing and correcting a canonical text**:

- method/order: 70.8% in Jesuit Elements vs 29.4% in Jesuit non-Elements;
- correction/revision: 50.0% in Jesuit Elements vs 5.9% in Jesuit non-Elements;
- ancient restoration: 83.3% in Jesuit Elements vs 11.8% in Jesuit non-Elements.

The non-Elements case is more about **applied mathematical production**:

- practical utility is higher in Jesuit non-Elements, 41.2%, than in Jesuit Elements, 29.2%;
- subject families are broader and more applied;
- student/school language is more visible in Jesuit non-Elements, 23.5%, than in Jesuit Elements, 12.5%.

## Revised Historical Claim

> Jesuit-marked mathematical books share an institutional-practical orientation, but the form of that orientation changes by corpus. In the Elements, Jesuit visibility attaches to methodized, corrected, curricular Euclid, especially books 1-6 plus solids and multi-region print profiles. Outside the Elements, Jesuit visibility attaches more to applied mathematical arts: perspective, instruments, mechanics, astronomy/navigation, music, and mixed mathematical compendia.

This means the Jesuit pattern is not simply "Jesuits make mathematics practical." More precisely: Jesuit-marked title pages present mathematics as institutionally usable. For Euclid, that means corrected and ordered course-texts; outside Euclid, it means practical and applied mathematical arts.
