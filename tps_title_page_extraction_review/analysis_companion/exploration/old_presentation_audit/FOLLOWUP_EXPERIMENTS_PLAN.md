## Experiment 4: Local Circulation Network

Status: first pass complete.

Purpose:

Test the old claim about local roots and cross-European circulation without relying on incomplete author bibliographies.

Proposed data:

- `ocrflow/store/items_metadata/items_print.csv`
- `ocrflow/store/items_metadata/metadata_elements_print.csv`
- `ocrflow/store/items_metadata/cluster_items.csv`
- `ocrflow/store/items_metadata/clusters.csv`
- `ocrflow/store/items_metadata/translations.csv`
- title-page origin/destination language fields from derived matrices.

Outputs to build:

- city/language/date flow table for metadata Elements;
- reprint cluster city movement table;
- translation origin/destination language table;
- network graph or adjacency table;
- case shortlist for Henrion, Dou, Clavius/Dechales, Commandino, Finé, etc.

First-pass result:

See `LOCAL_CIRCULATION_NETWORK_SUMMARY.md` and `experiment_outputs/local_circulation_network/`.

For the to-1705 Elements corpus, 292 editions are represented across 62 cities and 10 languages. Paris is the dominant center with 77 editions, but the corpus also has secondary centers such as London, Venice, Amsterdam, Rome, Strasbourg, Cologne, Basel, and Lyon.

The reprint network is mostly local and same-language:

| Measure | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Same-city sequential reprint links | 52/71 | 73.2% |
| Cross-city sequential reprint links | 19/71 | 26.8% |
| Same-language sequential reprint links | 69/71 | 97.2% |
| Cross-language sequential reprint links | 2/71 | 2.8% |

Key cross-city routes include Lyon to Geneva, Paris to Cologne, Urbino to Pesaro, Ingolstadt to Bologna, Rotterdam to Utrecht, and Cologne to Paris.

Interpretation:

The old "local roots" claim should be reframed. Do not claim complete author-career locality. Instead, claim that the metadata-defined Elements corpus circulated through strong local printing centers, mostly same-language reprint chains, and a smaller number of identifiable cross-city routes.

Important corpus note:

The corpus is complete for the metadata-defined Elements editions currently in the metadata, including their reprints. It is not a complete bibliography of all works by each author/editor, and not a complete corpus of all mathematical print. Therefore, local-circulation claims should be framed around Elements editions and their reprint/translation paths, not full author careers.

## Experiment 5: Jesuit Display/Suppression Control

Status: planned.

Purpose:

Test whether vernacular title pages suppress Jesuit identity, and under what local/political conditions.

Required denominator:

Do not use all Elements editions. Restrict to editions with known Jesuit author/source/adaptation or clear Jesuit connection. Then compare title-page display of Jesuit identity by:

- language;
- city/region;
- date;
- format;
- Catholic/Protestant or confessional-political context where known;
- book coverage.

First clue:

In all metadata Elements, Jesuit rates are:

- Latin: 16/139 = 11.5%;
- French: 5/53 = 9.4%;
- English: 3/20 = 15.0%;
- Dutch, German, Italian: 0 in current data.

This does not yet prove suppression, because the denominator includes many non-Jesuit works.
