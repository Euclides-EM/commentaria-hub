# Jesuit-Marked Elements Editions

This note profiles metadata-defined Elements editions whose title-page/social classification has a Jesuit marker: `inst_jesuit` or `jesuit_religious`.

Important caveat: this is not a complete list of all editions by Jesuit authors, nor all editions used in Jesuit education. It captures editions where the current title-page classification makes the Jesuit affiliation visible.

## Main Claim

Jesuit-marked Elements editions are distinctive less as a separate subject category than as an institutional-pedagogical print profile. They over-index books 1-6 plus solids, method/order, correction, compact formats, practical utility, and multi-region print reach. They do **not** over-index translation, visual aids, abridgment, or ancient restoration.

In short:

> Jesuit-marked Elements title pages tend to present Euclid as a portable, corrected, methodized, institutionally authorized teaching package, rather than as a vernacular translation project or a purely philological restoration of ancient Euclid.

## Book Coverage

Jesuit-marked Elements editions strongly prefer the middle pedagogical package of books 1-6 plus solids.

| Book coverage | Jesuit-marked | Other Elements |
|---|---:|---:|
| Books 1-6 plus solids | 13 / 24 = 54.2% | 19 / 235 = 8.1% |
| Books 1-6 | 6 / 24 = 25.0% | 66 / 235 = 28.1% |
| Near-complete or expanded | 3 / 24 = 12.5% | 62 / 235 = 26.4% |
| Selected later books | 0 / 24 = 0.0% | 19 / 235 = 8.1% |

Interpretation: the Jesuit-marked editions are not simply "shorter Euclid." They especially favor a teachable Euclid that combines elementary plane geometry with solids. This looks like curricular packaging: enough Euclid to form a mathematical course, but not necessarily the whole ancient corpus.

## Intellectual Values

The main intellectual signals are method/order, correction, and practical use.

| Completeness / system | 8 / 24 = 33.3% | 35 / 235 = 14.9% |

Interpretation: ancient authority is not what distinguishes the Jesuit-marked group, because it is common across the Elements corpus. The distinctive profile is organized, corrected, useful Euclid. The title page is often doing institutional quality-control work: this Euclid is ordered, corrected, adapted, and usable.

## What They Do Not Emphasize

| Marker | Jesuit-marked | Other Elements |
|---|---:|---:|

| Visual aids | 2 / 24 = 8.3% | 40 / 235 = 17.0% |
| Selection / abridgment | 2 / 24 = 8.3% | 40 / 235 = 17.0% |

Interpretation: the Jesuit-marked profile is not primarily a translation profile, even when individual editions appear in French or English. It is also not especially image/diagram-forward or abridgment-forward in the title-page language. Its main work is methodizing and authorizing Euclid, not making Euclid vernacular as such.

| Period | Jesuit-marked | Other Elements |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1600-1649 | 4 / 24 = 16.7% | 70 / 235 = 29.8% |

Interpretation: the Jesuit-marked title-page profile is largely a seventeenth-century phenomenon in this corpus, especially a late-seventeenth-century one. It is Latin-heavy, but not Latin-only; its vernacular appearances are better understood as extensions of an institutionalized Euclidean package than as translation-first projects.

## Observed Print Reach

For to-1705 cases that can be joined to the named-line print-geography analysis:

| Print profile | Jesuit-marked | Other Elements |
|---|---:|---:|
| Single city | 2 / 24 = 8.3% | 129 / 227 = 56.8% |
| Single region | 3 / 24 = 12.5% | 35 / 227 = 15.4% |
| Interregional route | 5 / 24 = 20.8% | 49 / 227 = 21.6% |
| Pan-European / multi-region | 14 / 24 = 58.3% | 14 / 227 = 6.2% |

Interpretation: this is the strongest structural difference. Jesuit-marked Elements are unusually likely to belong to named lines that reappear across multiple print regions. This does not prove book circulation in the sense of sales or readership, but it is a strong print-geographic indicator.

## Representative Cases

Examples to inspect visually:

- `Frankfurt_1654`: Clavius; Latin; near-complete/expanded; method and correction.
- `Würzburg_1661`, `Frankfurt_1674`, `Bamberg_1677`: Gaspar Schott; Latin; books 1-6; method-oriented Jesuit mathematical teaching line.
- `Lyon_1660`, `Paris_1677a`, `Paris_1682`, `Oxford_1685`, `London_1696`, `Amsterdam_1700`, `Oxford_London_1700`: Dechales line; books 1-6 plus solids; practical/method claims become especially visible in later vernacular appearances.
- `Amsterdam_1683`, `Amsterdam_1701b`, `Cambridge_1703`: Tacquet line; Latin; books 1-6 plus solids; method-oriented compact institutional Euclid.

## Slide Claim

> Jesuit-marked Elements editions are not simply more Latin, more ancient, or more complete. They are more likely to package Euclid as a corrected, methodized, institutionally portable course-text, especially through books 1-6 plus solids and through named lines that reappear across multiple print regions.
