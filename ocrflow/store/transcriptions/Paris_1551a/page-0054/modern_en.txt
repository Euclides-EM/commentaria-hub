And those which are commensurable with this, let them be called ῥητά.

By this word commensurable, understand whether they are squares
or any other rectilinear figures whatsoever.  For it is known
that to any parallelogram, an equal square
may be described: by the last theorem of the second book, or by
the finding of a mean proportional line according to the 13th
theorem of book 6. which when you have found, you will gather at once by
17.6. that the right-angled parallelogram contained
by the two extreme lines is equal to the square of the
mean line.  In like manner again, to any square a
parallelogram equal is described, by the finding of a
third proportional line according to the theorem 11.6. here
you see nothing such said of squares & other figures,
as was said before of lines, of which there is both length and
power commensurable. For of figures only the capacity is considered,
which length joined to breadth determines.

But those which are incommensurable with that square, namely the ῥητόν,
let them be called ἄλογα, that is, irrational.

Here too take incommensurable, of whatever sort the rectilinear
figures may have been.
