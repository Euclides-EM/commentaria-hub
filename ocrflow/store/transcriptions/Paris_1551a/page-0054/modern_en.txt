And those which are commensurable with this, let them be called rational.

By this word commensurable, understand whether they are squares,
or any other rectilinear figures whatever.  For it is known
to anyone to describe, to any parallelogram, an equal square:
by the last theorem of the second book, or by
the finding of the mean proportional line according to the 13th
theorem of book 6. which when you have found, you will gather at once by
17.6. that the rectangle parallelogram contained
by the two extreme lines is equal to the square of the
mean line.  By a like reasoning again, to any square a
parallelogram equal is described, by the finding of the
third proportional line according to theorem 11.6. here
you see nothing such said of squares & other figures,
as of lines before, of which there is both length and
power commensurable. For of figures only the capacity is
considered, which length joined to breadth
determines.

But those which are incommensurable with that square, namely the rational,
let them be called irrational, that is, surd.
