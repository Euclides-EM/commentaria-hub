Latin interpreters have named “rationale.” By what reasoning
led, I do not know: to me indeed it is not very approved. Let there-
fore ἄῤῥη be any straight line, about which the discourse ought to be set forth.
Etymology, the reasoning in the stating of things to be signified by simple
words, as the ancients seem to have pursued with great diligence:
so I judge it not to be despised by us, that we may help
the knowledge of the things themselves. The derivation of this word ἄῤῥη seems to me to be from
τοῦ ῥητοῦ whence τὸ ῥητὸν καὶ ῥητὰ. ἔστι, I interpret that
which is speakable, certain, granted, and determinate:
and as if, for example, you should say that to be, which is sayable, and
can be signified by a word. ἄῤῥη therefore will be any line,
of whatever magnitude is proposed. Therefore ἄῤῥη is so
named, because we can divide a given line into as
many parts as we shall wish. For it is known from the ninth
proposition of the sixth book, to take away from a given line parts in like manner.
