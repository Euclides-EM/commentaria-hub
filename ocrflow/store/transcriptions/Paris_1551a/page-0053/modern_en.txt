those lines, I say, to be ἀλόγους: that is, to use their word,

who wished them to be called ἄρρητὰς, that is, “rational,” which
are commensurable to the proposed line, either in length
and in power, or in power only: from which
great differences of opinions have arisen in many places of this book.
Thus far Tartalea. And indeed this is not nothing,
and yet in it are not all things. But I would not hesitate
to affirm this, that from here our beginning took its rise, which
has poured such dense darkness upon the truth of the things handed down
in these books, that teachers and learners for the most part all
have been led in different ways: so that when they proceeded further, they understood
nothing more than that they understood nothing. Our
judgment indeed about that matter, when there shall be need, we shall set forth.
