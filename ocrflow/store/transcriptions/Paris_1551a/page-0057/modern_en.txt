EUCLID’S ELEMENTS.

I strongly urge all who wish to become mathematicians
to read diligently those who have written on Euclid;
to whom I shall never deny that I owe very much.
As to the matter, it is of this kind: first, that principle
of Campanus to comprehend at least quantity as a whole
(whether it be that which they call continuous, or whether
it be discrete such as number is). Moreover there is
something else which belongs only to continuous quantity,
namely that you can diminish it to infinity, as in a line.
For whatever is given can be divided into infinite parts,
whether it can be divided or not, of which nevertheless
each will be a line: and that again thence into others which
retain the same nature: nor is it ever reached to a minimum
by section, not even if you should call it a point, although
that by itself is in geometry a minimum. Hence it comes
about that there are certain irrational lines, of which indeed
there is some proportion to one another, but it cannot be
expressed by number; and therefore it is called by
Proclus an irrational number. For wherever there is section
or division to infinity, there also is found that ineffable thing
which they call irrational: but this is not so in numbers. For
there is no number which by dividing you do not reduce to
that minimum, which is unity, from which it is necessary
that all numbers are, as it is agreed among them. For all are
measured by unity. Therefore there are no irrational numbers.
