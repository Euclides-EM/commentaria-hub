TENTH.

DEFINITIONS.

1.  COMMENSURABLE
    magnitudes are those mea-
    sured by one and the same mea-
    sure.

2.  Incommensurable mag-
    nitudes are those which have
    no common measure.

Such magnitudes are the diameter of some square, and the side
of it; for these have no common measure, as will be de-
monstrated in the last prop. of this book. There are also several other
incommensurable lines, namely those to which one cannot give
any common measure, many of which will be explained in this
book; and taught by what ways they can be found:
again, surfaces are said to be incommensurable, and solids
incommensurable, which have no common measure.

3.  Straight lines commensurable in power
    are those whose squares can
    be measured by one and the same surface.
