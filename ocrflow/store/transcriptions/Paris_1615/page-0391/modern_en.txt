THEOR. I. PROP. I.

If two unequal magnitudes are proposed,
if from the greater one subtracts more than
the half, and again from the remainder more than the half,
and continuing always thus: there will remain
at the end a magnitude smaller than
the smaller of the two proposed.

Let two unequal magnitudes AB and
C be proposed, C being the smaller: I say that if one subtracts
more than the half of AB, and from the remainder again more than
the half, and continuing always thus, one will find
at the end a remainder smaller than C.

Let it not be so. Let the magnitude C be multiplied
so many times that it exceeds the greater AB, and let
the product of that multiplication be DE, which
will be a multiple of C, and greater than AB: let it
therefore be divided into parts equal to C, as into
DF, FG, and GE: likewise let there be taken from AB more than the half, namely
AH, and from the remainder HB more than the half, namely HI, and
continuing always this until AB is divided
into as many parts as DE, namely into AH, HI, and IB;
as DE is divided into DF, FG, and GE. Inasmuch as DE
is greater than AB, and the subtracted DF is not greater
than the half of DE; as the subtracted AH is not greater
than the half of AB, the remainder FE will be greater than the remainder
HB: similarly if from the greater remainder FE one subtracts FG,
which is not more than the half, and from the smaller remainder HB one
