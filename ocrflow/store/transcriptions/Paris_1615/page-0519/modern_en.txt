THEOR.  89.  PROP. CXIII.

The square of a rational line being ap-
plied to a binomial, makes the other side a residual,
the terms of which are proportional, and com-
mensurable with the terms of the binomial: moreover
the residual is of the same order as the binomial.

Let the rational (line) be A, and the bi-
nomial BC, of which the greater
term is BD: and to this BC
let the rectangle BE be applied
equal to the square of the rational
A. I say that the other side CE
is a residual such as the propo-
sition states.

For upon the lesser term CD
let the rectangle DF be applied
also equal to the square of the ratio-
nal A: therefore the two rectangles BE, DF, will be equal, and by
the 14th prop. of the 6th (book) their sides will be reciprocal, namely that as
BC is to DC, so CF is to CE, and by division as BD is to DC,
so FE is to EC. But BD is greater than CD: therefore also
FE will be greater than EC. Now from EF let there be cut off
EG equal to EC, and let it be made as FG is to GE, so EC
to CH, by the 12th prop. of the 6th (book); and by composition as FE is to EG (or EC
which are equal) so EH is to CH, and by the 12th prop. of the 5th (book) the two anteced-
ents FEH will be to the two consequents ECH as the one
of the antecedents EH is to the one of the consequents CH: and therefore
