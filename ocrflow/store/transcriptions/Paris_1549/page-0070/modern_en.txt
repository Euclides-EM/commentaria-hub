BOOK X.                                                                34

OF EUCLID.
The tenth book.

Definition 1.

Magnitudes are called commensurable, which
are measured by the same measure.

2

But those are incommensurable which fall under
the measuring of no common measure.

3

Straight lines are commensurable in power,
when the squares from them are measured by the
same area—

4

But incommensurable, when the squares from
them are measured by no area with a common
measure. From these things set forth it is shown, that
a straight line having been proposed, that is, from which
cubits, and palms,
and digits, and feet are taken as measures, those
are straight lines, in infinite multitude, commen-
surable and incommensurable. Commensura-
ble indeed, either in power only, or in power
and length together. But incommensurable,
either in length only, or in length and in
power together.
