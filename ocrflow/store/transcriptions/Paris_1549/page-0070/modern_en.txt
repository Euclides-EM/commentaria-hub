BOOK X.                                  34

OF EUCLID.
Tenth book.

Definition  I.

Commensurable magnitudes are said to be those which
are measured by the same measure.
2
But incommensurable [magnitudes are] those which fall under
the measurement of no common measure.
3
Straight lines are commensurable in power,
when the squares on them are measured by the same area.
4
But [they are] incommensurable, when the squares on them
are measured by no area with a common measure. From these things set forth it is indicated, that a proposed
straight line, that is, from which also cubits, and palms,
and digits, and feet are taken as measures, the straight lines
are infinite in multitude, commensurable and incommensurable. Commensurable indeed, either in power only, or in power
and length together. But incommensurable,
either in length only, or in length and in
power together.
