EUCLID’S  
ELEMENTS  
BOOK TENTH.  

WITH ANCIENT SCHOLIA  
AND COMMENTARIES.  

Of Federicus Commandinus of Urbino.

DEFINITIONS  
I.

COMMENSURABLE magnitudes are
called those which the same measure
measures.

II.

But incommensurable, those for which
it happens that there is no common
measure.

III.

Straight lines are commensurable in
power, when the squares from them
measure the same area.

F. C.  COMMENTARY.

He does not separately distinguish straight lines commensurable in length, because they are included in the first definition
of commensurable magnitudes; for straight lines commensurable in length are those which the same measure measures.

IIII.

But incommensurable, with the squares
from them, it happens that there is no
common area.

V.

These being set down, it is shown that to any proposed straight
line there are straight lines, infinite in multitude, both commensurable
and incommensurable: some indeed in length and in power; others
in power only; and let the proposed straight line be called rational.
