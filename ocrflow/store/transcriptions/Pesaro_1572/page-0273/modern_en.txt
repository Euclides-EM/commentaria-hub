Of Federicus Commandinus of Urbino.

DEFINITIONS  
I.

COMMENSURABLE magnitudes are
said to be those which the same measure
measures.

II.

But incommensurable, those for which
it happens that there is no common
measure.

III.

Straight lines are commensurable in power,
when the squares which are from them
measure the same area.

F. C.  COMMENTARY.

He did not separately distinguish straight lines commensurable in length, because they are included in the first definition
of commensurable magnitudes; for straight lines commensurable in length are those which the same measure measures.

IIII.

But incommensurable, with the squares which are from them,
it happens that there is no common area.

V.

With these posited it is shown, for any proposed straight line, that there are infinitely many straight
lines, both commensurable and incom-
mensurable: some indeed in length and in power; others truly in
power only; but let the proposed straight line be called rational.
