I. C.  C O M M E N T A R Y.

There are also certain common notions, which Euclid uses in this
book, namely these.

C O M M O N  N O T I O N S.

1 Any magnitude multiplied can exceed every proposed magnitude
of the same kind.

2 Whatever magnitude measures some [magnitude], also measures that
which it itself measures.

3 Whatever magnitude measures the whole and the part taken away,
will also measure the remainder.

4 Whatever magnitude measures two or more magnitudes, also measures
that which is composed from them.

THEOREM I.  PROPOSITION I.

With two unequal magnitudes set out, if from the greater there be
taken away more than the half; and from that which remains again
there be taken away more than the half; and if this be done always:
there will at length be left some magnitude which will be less than
the lesser magnitude set out.
