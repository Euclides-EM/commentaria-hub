From Campanus.                         Definitions.

Quantities for which there is one quantity
measuring them in common are called
communicating.
Those for which there is not one common
quantity measuring them are
called incommensurable.
Lines communicating in power are said to be those
whose square surfaces one common surface
measures.
Lines incommensurable in power are said to be those whose
square surfaces one common surface does not measure.
Since these things are so, it is clear that for any given line
there are many others incommensurable: some in length
only; some in length and power.
Every line, however, with which we reason, being set down, is
called rational.
And the lines communicating with it are called rational.
But those incommunicating with it are called irrational
or surd.
Every square surface about which by hypothesis
we reason is called rational.
And the surfaces communicating with it are called rational.
But the surfaces incommensurable with it are called
irrational or surd.
But the sides which make those squares are called
irrational.
