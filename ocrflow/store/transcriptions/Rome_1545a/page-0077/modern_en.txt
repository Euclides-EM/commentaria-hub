And the square, made from the proposed straight line,

And the surface, commensurable with this, is
called Rational.

But the surface, incommensurable with it, is
called Irrational.

And the line, which can make that surface, is called
Irrational. If it is a square: its sides
are called Irrational. But if it is some
other rectilinear figure: that line which describes a
square equal to it, is called Irrational.

The propositions.

1  If there are proposed two unequal magnitudes, if from
the greater there is taken more than the half, and from
the remainder more than the half, and thus always it is done:
there will remain a magnitude smaller than that
proposed magnitude which was smaller.

2  If there are proposed two unequal magnitudes, and always
taking the smaller from the greater, if the remainder
never measures that which is before it, the magnitudes
will be incommensurable.
