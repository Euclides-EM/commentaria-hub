Now to these definitions we shall add (after Clavius) a request, and some
common sentences, the use of which will be found in this book

REQUEST.

That one may multiply any magnitude so many times
that it exceeds any proposed magnitude of the same
kind.

COMMON  SENTENCES.

1 A magnitude measuring as many magnitudes as one wishes,
also measures the compound of them.
2 A magnitude measuring any magnitude, likewise measures
every magnitude that that one measures.
3 A magnitude which measures a whole magnitude, and one cut
off from it, also measures the remainder.

THEOR. 1. PROP. I.

If two unequal magnitudes are proposed, if from the greater
one subtract more than the half, and from the remainder again more
than the half, and in continuing this it is always done thus:
there will remain at the end a magnitude smaller than the
lesser of the two proposed.

Let two unequal magnitudes AB and C be proposed, of
which AB is the greater: I say that if from AB one
subtract more than the half, and from the remainder again more than the
half, and one continues always thus, there will remain at the end
some magnitude smaller than C.
Let it not be so. Let the magnitude C be multiplied so
many times that the product exceeds the greater AB, and let the
product of that multiplication be D E, which will be a multiple
of C, and greater than AB: let it therefore be divided
into parts equal to C, as into DF, F G, G E: likewise
of AB. Let more than the half be cut off, A H, and from the remainder H B
