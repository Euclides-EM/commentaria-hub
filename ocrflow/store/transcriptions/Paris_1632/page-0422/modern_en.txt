Now to these definitions we shall add (after Clavius) a demand, and some
common sentences, of which the wise will be found in this book

D E M A N D.

That one may multiply any magnitude so many times
that it exceeds any proposed magnitude of the same
kind.

COMMON  SENTENCES.

1 A magnitude measuring as many magnitudes as one wishes,
also measures the compound of them.
2 A magnitude measuring any magnitude, likewise measures
every magnitude that that one measures.
3 A magnitude which measures a whole magnitude, and a part
cut off from it, also measures the remainder.

THEOR. 1. PROP. I.

If two unequal magnitudes are proposed, if from the greater
more than the half be taken away, and from the remainder again more
than the half, and if in continuing this it is always done thus:
there will remain at the end a magnitude smaller than the
lesser of the two proposed.

Let two unequal magnitudes AB and C be proposed, of
which AB is the greater: I say that if from AB
more than the half be taken away, and from the remainder again more than the
half, and if one continue always thus, there will remain at the end
some magnitude smaller than C.
Let it not be so. Let the magnitude C be multiplied so many
times that the product exceeds the greater AB, and let the
product of that multiplication be D E, which will be a multiple
of C, and greater than AB: let it therefore be divided
into parts equal to C, as into DF, F G, G E: likewise
