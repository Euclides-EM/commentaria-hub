Measurable. Therefore it is permitted for us to propose any quantity (as certain) which, when
compared to others, makes them uncertain (since they lack a denomination of relation). Yet those
who have tried to draw out the denominations of these uncertain things from the denominations of
certain things (so that, as far as possible, the hidden relations of them may be revealed) have set
forth the proposed certain things by numbers, which, when multiplied, joined, or varied by some
other arithmetical operation, would produce a number lacking a root; and they impose this root,
whether square, or cubic, censive, or whatever else it may be, as the denomination of these
uncertain things (when compared with the proposed certain thing), so that they may be able, as
nearly as possible, to extract the denominations of uncertain things from numbers denoting a
certain quantity. But these numbers they call surds, algebraic, radical, or uncertain, or improperly
irrational (following the former error), which we call hypothetical rather than essential, because
they are taken only from hypothesis, deprived of all essence and denomination. Yet by their
service we obtain better relations of certain things to uncertain, hitherto cultivated by human
measures, as we may make clear by an example: a straight line A B being cut in extreme and mean
ratio at G, let us find A G by the units of A B, which are 12, as nearly as may be. We shall draw
(from the eleventh of the second) A B into itself, making 144, and half of A B, namely B into
itself, makes 36, which joined make 180; and the square root of these, minus B, makes A G.
Wherefore they will denominate the greater segment A G. The root of 180 minus B, which root
(since it is none among numbers) is deprived of all denomination; but if it is to be expressed as
nearly as possible: we shall say that when A B is twelve, A G is 7 and 1/2 nearly greater, or nearly
less, 7 and 11/17. But the remainder B G occupies the remainder of the 12. All which things
agree rather with convenience than with demonstration. We therefore shall carry through the
undertaking.
