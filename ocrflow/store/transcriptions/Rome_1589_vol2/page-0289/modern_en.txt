ALL plane surfaces commensurable with the square on the
proposed Rational line are called Rational; not indeed
from position, as that [is], but because when compared with it they are found to be either
commensurable or incommensurable: just as
also lines which in any way are commen-
surable with a Rational line are called Rational.

BUT SINCE lines “potent” [in power], the spaces themselves commen-
surable with the square on the proposed Rational line, are at least in power
commensurable with the Rational line; from the 3rd definition it is clear
that these also are to be called, according to the 6th definition, Rational.

X.

BUT those incommensurable with this,
let them be called Irrational.

IN NO OTHER WAY are plane surfaces incommensurable with the square
described on a Rational line called Irrational, than as the
lines which are altogether incommensurable with a proposed Rational [line]
have been called Irrational.

XI.

AND the straight lines which can [do] this, [are] Irratio-
nal: if indeed those squares are [squares], the
sides themselves; but if some other rectilinear [figures],
the straight lines which describe squares equal
to spaces incommensurable [with them].

HE CALLS the lines which can [produce] spaces incommensurable with the square on a Rational
line, Irrational; just as above he called the
spaces Irrational: so that if the incommensurable
spaces should indeed be squares, their sides are called Irratio-
nal; but if they should not be squares, but any other recti-
linear figures, the straight lines which describe squares
equal to those incommensurable spaces are called Irrational.
