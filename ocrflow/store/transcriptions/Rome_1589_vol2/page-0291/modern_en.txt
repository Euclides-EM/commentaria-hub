For if two magnitudes of the same kind are unequal, since the greater is not infinite, but the lesser can be increased infinitely; it is clear that the lesser can be multiplied so many times until it exceeds the greater. This is also evident from those things which we wrote in defn. 5. of book 5. For there we said that those magnitudes are then at last judged to be of the same magnitude, when either of them can be so multiplied that it exceeds the other. And from the failure of this condition, we taught that a rectilinear angle and an angle of a tangent are of different kind.

AXIOMS OR
Pronouncements.

I.

A MAGNITUDE measuring however many
magnitudes, also measures the composite
made from them.

II.

A MAGNITUDE measuring any
magnitude, also measures every
magnitude which that one measures.

III.

A MAGNITUDE measuring the whole
magnitude, and the part taken away, also measures
the remainder.
