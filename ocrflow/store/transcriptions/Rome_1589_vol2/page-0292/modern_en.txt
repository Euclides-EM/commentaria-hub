M O R E O V E R, because in this book it was necessary for us to depart
from the numbering which Theon follows in the propositions of Euclid,
as we shall say at prop. 13, just as we have neglected the series of Campanus
in all things; as we taught at the beginning of the first
book; we took care that a double number in the margin opposite
the propositions should be set down, of which the upper shows the order of
Theon, the lower however shows the series of Campanus. But if one
number only is found in the margin, understand that there is then no
disagreement between Theon and Campanus, as far as
the numbering of the propositions is concerned.

THEOR. I. PROPOS. I.                              I.
TWO unequal magnitudes being set forth,
if from the greater there be taken away
more than the half; and from that which
remains, again there be taken away more
than the half; and this be always done:
there will at length be left some magnitude,
which will be less than the proposed lesser magnitude.
