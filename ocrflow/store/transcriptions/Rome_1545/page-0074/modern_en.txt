OF EUCLID’S ELEMENTS
Book 10.

Definitions.

Commensurable magnitudes are said to be those measured by the same
measure.
Incommensurable, however, are those of which no [magnitude] admits a
common measure to come to be.
Straight lines are commensurable in power, whenever the squares on
them are measured by the same [measure].
Incommensurable, however, whenever, for the squares on them, no [line]
admits a common measure to come to be.
