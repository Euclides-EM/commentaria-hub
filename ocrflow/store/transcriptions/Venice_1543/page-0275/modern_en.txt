The Translator.

H Since the author in the preceding definition [shows] what are the surfaces
called rational, now in this coupled [one] he defines the converse,
that is, that all those surfaces which shall not be commensurable with that

Definition x.

4 And those which with any of those (irrational) shall be commu-
5 nicating shall be called irrational.

The Translator.

Q This definition warns us that all those surfaces which are,
or shall be communicating with any irrational surface, shall
likewise be called irrational.

Definition xi.

10 And the sides having power in those surfaces, square, are called irra-
4 tional.

The Translator.

C That is, the sides having power in such irrational surfaces, square likewise
are called irrational; the side having power in a surface (that surface being
square) is understood to be the proper side of such a surface,
but if it were not square it is understood rather as the side of a square surface
equal to it, or of that same reduced into a square which is
the same.
