The Translator.

H Since the author in the preceding definition [declared] which are the surfaces
called rational, now in this, conjointly, he defines the converse,
that is, that all those surfaces which shall not be commensurable with that
same our square surface (said above) shall be called irra-
tional, or deaf.

Definition x.

4 And those which with any of those (irrational) shall be commu-
5 nicating shall be called irrational.

The Translator.

Q This definition admonishes us how all those surfaces which are,
or shall be communicating with any irrational surface, shall
likewise be called irrational.

Definition xi.

10 And the sides having power in those surfaces, squares are called ir-
4 rational.

The Translator.

C That is, that the sides having power in such irrational surfaces, squares like-
wise are called irrational; the side having power in a surface (that surface being
square) is understood to be the proper side of that such sur-
face; but if it were not square it is understood rather as the side of a square surface
equal to it, or of that same reduced into a square which is
the same.
