Definition, viii.

8  And the surfaces commensurable with that are called rational

The Translator.

That is, that all those surfaces which will be communicant, or else commensurable
with that our square surface (said above) are called
rational; but it must be noted that if our square surface is of one
pace, not only a surface of several whole superficial paces (as
would be of paces 450) is called rational, but also of paces, small ounces, and
half ounces will also be called rational (just as of the lines above at the fifth
definition it was said) for being commensurable with the said our square surface
of one pace, and their common measure will always be the least part
of the pace that will be found to be denominated in the said surface; and so that you
understand me better let us suppose that a measured surface is paces twenty-five and one
third superficial; I say the said surface is commensurable with our
surface of one pace and their common measure will be one third of a superficial pace;
likewise if the said measured surface were paces thirty and of feet five
ounces seven and three quarters of a superficial ounce, their common measure will be
then one quarter of a superficial ounce; and therefore the one and the other will be rational;
the same will be found in every other kind of known fraction, that one superficial pace
is feet 25 superficial and one superficial foot is ounces 144 superficial;
and with these evidences you will be able to know in every other kind of measure (call it
as you wish) how many little square surfaces of one of its parts go to make the whole,
because many believe that just as one linear pace is five linear feet,
so likewise one superficial pace is similarly five superficial feet;
indeed it is the square of five, that is twenty-five, as said above;
and finally because one linear foot is divided into ounces 12, they believe that similarly
12 superficial ounces make one superficial foot, because they cannot
know its resolutions, for as is said above one superficial foot
is 144 superficial ounces; and all this (for the reasons adduced above
at the first definition, or supposition of the second) will be manifest, and not only
in the parts of the pace and of the foot but also in the parts of the pike
and of the cane, and of the cauezzo, or of a measure formed at our pleasure, because
what is said of the pace and foot, with the same evidence proceeds
in the parts of whatever measure divided as one wishes, because every famous,
certain form both divides and gives the name to its famous measures: according to what
was said above, take heed.
