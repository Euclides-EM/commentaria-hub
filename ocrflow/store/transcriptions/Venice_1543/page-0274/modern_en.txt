The Translator.

That is, that all those surfaces which shall be commensurable, or
measurable with that our square surface (said above) are called
rational; but it must be noted that if our square surface is of one
pace, not only another surface of more whole square paces (as
would be of paces 450) is also called rational, but also of paces, feet or inches, and
half-inches will also be called rational (as of the lines above at the fifth
definition was said) for being commensurable with said our square
surface, since one pace and their common measure will always be the smallest part
of the pace that shall be found to be named in said surface; and so that you better
understand, let us suppose that a measured surface is eleven and one
third square paces; I say the said surface is commensurable with our
surface of one pace and their common measure will be one third of a square pace;
likewise if the said measured surface were paces thirty and feet five,
inches seven and three quarters of a square inch, their common measure will be
then one quarter of a square inch, and therefore the one and the other will be rational;
and the same will be found in every other kind of known fraction, that one square pace is
25 square feet and one square foot is 144 square inches;
and with these evidences you will be able to know in every other kind of measure (as
you wish) how many small square surfaces of one of its parts go to make the whole,
because many believe that just as one linear pace is five linear feet,
so likewise one square pace is similarly five square feet; rather it is the square
of five, that is twenty-five, as said above; and finally because one linear foot is divided
into 12 inches, they believe that likewise 12 square inches make one square foot, because they cannot
count the two super-solutions, for as is said above one square foot is 144 square inches; and all this
(for the reasons adduced above the first definition, or supposition of the second) will be manifest, and not only
in the parts of the pace and of the foot but also in the parts of the pike
and of the cane and of the cauezzo, or of a measure formed at our pleasure, because
what is said of the pace and foot, with the same evidence proceeds
in the parts of whatever measure divided as one wishes, because every
famous, certain form both divides and gives the name to its famous measures, according to what
was said above before.
