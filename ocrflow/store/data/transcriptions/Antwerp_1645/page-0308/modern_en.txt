But that it is commensurable in power, who does not perceive, since the square of 20 palms, or 1000 palms, in respect of capacity is equal [and] commensurable to the square of 16 palms, according to def. 3.

V I I I.

And the square, which is made from the proposed straight [line],
let it be called Rational.

That is, the square of the proposed rational straight [line] is to be called rational; the square itself giving the name, the rational straight line being set out.

I X.

And those commensurable with it indeed, [let them be] Rational.

Namely, all squares commensurable with the rational [square] aforesaid: let them also be called rational.

X.

But those incommensurable, let them be called Irrational.

That is, all squares incommensurable with the rational [square] aforesaid in def. 8, let them be called irrational.

It must be noted that any plane areas equal to the square of a rational straight line, or also commensurable with it, are called by geometers rational, which by prop. 14 of book 2, if they are rectangles, can be reduced to squares: but if they are not rectangles, they can be reduced to them, or to squares, by geometers. In the same way irrational [areas], plane areas incommensurable with the square of the rational [line] set out, although they are not squares, provided they can be reduced to them, may be so called.

X I.

And straight [lines], which themselves can [be] irrational, if indeed
their squares are [the] sides themselves: but if some other
rectilinear [figure], [then] the straight [lines] which describe squares equal to
areas incommensurable [with the rational square].

What we have just set down in the note explains the last parts of this last definition. Therefore straight lines, or the sides of squares, or of any other plane areas, which can be equal to squares and incommensurable with the square of the rational [line] set out, ought to be called irrational.

I warn that Euclid and all the ancient geometers always joined these particles “in length and power”, or “in power only”, with these words commensurable and incommensurable: but scarcely here and with the other words rational and irrational. which nevertheless some of the more recent geometers are not accustomed to do.
