6. If one number measures another number, it will also measure all
that which is measured by it.
    For if A measures B, and B measures CG, the same CG
will contain B several times: and therefore,
if one divides CG into parts equal to B;
A will measure each of these parts, namely
C D, D F, F G, and consequently
will measure their composite CG, by the 5th
axiom.

7. If one number measures another number, and one of its parts,
it will also measure the remainder.
    For if the number A measures the whole B D, and the part B C;
BD will contain A several times; and B C will also contain
the same A, one or several times. If therefore one
divides the numbers BD, BC into parts equal
to the number A, the number CD will necessarily
be one or several of its parts, (otherwise
the whole BD would not have been divided into parts equal to the num-
ber A) and therefore A will measure CD.

NOTICE.

We have chosen the following 24 propositions among
all those of the three Arithmetical books of Euclid, having
judged them sufficient for our treatise. Those which we have omit-
ted either do not concern it, as are all those of solid numbers,
because no mention is made of solid bodies in
our tenth book; or else must be held as demon-
strated in numbers, since they have been demonstrated in magnitudes in
the fifth book, and elsewhere, there being no numbers which cannot
