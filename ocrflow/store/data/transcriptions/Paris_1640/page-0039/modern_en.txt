23

DEFINITIONS.

1. UNITY is that which can be conceived as a single
thing.
  Thus the most composite things can be arithmetic unities; and a
house, although composed of stone, of wood, and of similar things,
can be taken for a unity, since one can conceive it as a single thing.
But according to what has been said in the 2nd question, arithmetic
unity is the general concept of singularity, or else unity in general,
abstracted and detached from things.
2. Number is a multitude composed of unities.
3. A lesser number is a part of a greater, when it measures it,
that is to say, when it is contained in it so many times.
  Thus 2 is a part of 6 because it measures it, that is to say,
that it is contained exactly in 6, namely three times.
4. A lesser number is several parts of a greater, when it does not
measure it.
  A number does not measure another number when it is not
contained in it exactly. Of this sort 4 does not measure 15 because,
being repeated 3 times, it is less, and being taken 4 times it is
greater. And therefore it is several parts of 15, namely 4/15, or 4
unities, of which each is the 15th part of 15.
5. A greater number is a multiple of a lesser, when the lesser
measures the greater.
  The number 20 is a multiple of 4 because 4 measures 20, in
which it is contained 5 times.
