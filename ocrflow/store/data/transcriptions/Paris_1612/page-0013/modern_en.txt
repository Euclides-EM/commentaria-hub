TWO magnitudes a, & b, are said to be commensurable because they have a common mea-
sure c, which measures each of a, & b.

FROM CLAVIUS.

IN THE SAME way commensurable are a line of 20 palms and a line of 12 palms, because a line, both of one palm,
and of half a palm, and of a third part of one palm measures them.
Likewise surfaces are said to be commensurable, which one and the same surface measures.
Also bodies, or solids, and commensurable, which the same solid body measures.

DEFINITION II.

INCOMMENSURABLE are those for which it happens that no common measure
