DEMAND.
That one may multiply any magnitude
so many times that it may exceed any
magnitude proposed of the same kind.
For, two unequal magnitudes of the same kind
being proposed, the greater not being infinite, &
the lesser being able to be increased infinite-
ly; it is manifest that the lesser can be
multiplied so many times that it exceeds the
greater, which also appears by the 5th definition
of the 5th book.

Common sentences, or notions.

1. A magnitude measuring as many magnitudes
as one wishes, also measures the magnitude com-
posed of them.
2. A magnitude which measures any
magnitude; likewise measures every other
magnitude that that one measures.
3. A magnitude measuring a whole mag-
nitude, and that taken away from it, also measures the
remainder.

These common sentences are shown in num-
bers in the 7th book, in the 3 last common sentences
which is the same thing as for magnitudes &c.
It must be understood that the proposi. are noted in the margin
by 2 numbers, & that the upper denotes the order of Theon,
& the lower the order of Campanus, that if there is only
a single number; that is to say that they do not differ &c.
