If one proposes some straight line of known
and determined magnitude: of all the
lines compared with it, some will
be commensurable in length and power
with it, some in power only. Likewise
some will be incommensurable in length
only with it; and some in length and
power, as will be shown in this book.
But this proposed line, by reason of which
the others are called commensurable or incommensurable,
is called by the Greeks [uncertain: ῥητή]: that is to say rational;
inasmuch as it is always posited of known
magnitude, and according to it one reasons; but
the others compared with it are not al-
ways known: although according to them,
taken separately, they are known, each
of them being able to be divided into such equal
parts as one will.
