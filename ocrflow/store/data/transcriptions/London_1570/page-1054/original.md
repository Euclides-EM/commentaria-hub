# ¶ The ſixtenth booke of

[Subhead]
the Elementes of Geometrie  
added by Fluſſas.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
The argument of the 16. booke.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:I|lines=14|style=decorated|decoration="foliate design with grotesque figures"}N the former fiuetenth booke hath bene taught how to inſcribe the fiue regular ſolides one with in an other. Now ſemeth to reſt, to cōpare thoſe ſolids ſo inſcribed, one to an other, and to ſet forth their paſſions and proprieties: which thing, Fluſſas conſidering, in this ſixtenth booke added by him, hath excellently well and moſt conningly performed. For which vndoubtedly he hath of all them which haue a loue to the Mathematicals, deſerued much prayſe and commendacion: both for the great trauailes and paynes (which it is moſt likely) he hath taken in inuenting ſuch ſtraunge and wonderfull propoſitions with their demonſtrations, in this booke contayned, as alſo for participating and communicating abrode the ſame to others. Which booke alſo, that the reader ſhould want nothing conducing to the perfection of Euclides Elementes: I haue with ſome trauaile tranſlated, & for the worthines thereof haue added it, as a ſixtenth booke to the 15. bookes of Euclide. Vouchſafe therefore gentle reader diligently to read and peyſe it, for in it ſhall you finde not onely matter ſtrange and delectable, but alſo occaſion of inuention of greater things pertayning to the natures of the fiue regular ſolides.

## ¶ The 1. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
A Dodecahedron, and a cube inſcribed in it, and a Pyramis inſcribed in the ſame cube, are contained in one and the ſelfe ſame ſphere.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:O|lines=7|style=decorated|decoration="seated human figure"}r the angles of the pyramis are ſet in the angles of the cube wherein it is inſcribed (by the firſt of the fiuetenth: and all the angles of the cube are ſet in the angles of the dodecahedron circumſcribed about it: (by the 8. of the fiuetenth): And all the angles of the Dodecahedron, are ſet in the ſuperficies of the ſphere, by the 17. of the thirtenth. Wherefore thoſe three ſolides inſcribed one within an other, are contained in one and the ſelfe ſame ſphere, by the firſt diffinition of the fiuetenth. A dodecahedron therfore and a cube inſcribed in it, and a pyramis inſcribed in the ſame cube, are contained in one and the ſelfe ſame ſphere.

### ¶ A Corollary.

Theſe three ſolides likewiſe are ſet in one and the ſelfe ſame Icoſahedron, or Octohedron, or Pyramis. For they are inſcribed in one and the ſame Icoſahedron, by the 5, 11. & 12. of the fiuetenth: and they are inſcribed in one and the ſelfe ſame Octohedron, by the 4. 6. and 16. of the ſame: laſtly they are inſcribed in one and the ſelfe ſame pyramis, by the firſt, 18. and 19. of the ſame. For the angles of all theſe ſolides are ſet in the centres of the baſes of the circumſcribed Icoſahedron, or Octohedron, or pyramis.
