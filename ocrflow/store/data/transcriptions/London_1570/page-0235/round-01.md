In this Propoſition are two caſes. For the lines cutting the one the other, do eyther, neyther of them paſſe by the centre, or the one of them doth paſſe by the centre, & the other not. The firſt is declared by the author. The ſecond is thus proued.

[Margin]
Two caſes in this Propoſition.
[/Margin]

Suppoſe that in the circle A B C D the line B D paſſing by the centre doe cut the line A C not paſſing by the centre. Then I ſay that the lines A C and B D do not deuide the one the other into two equall partes.

[Margin]
Conſtruction for the ſecond caſe.
[/Margin]

[Diagram: circle A B C D with diameter B D passing through F and E, and chord A C intersecting it at E]

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

For by the former Propoſition the line B D paſſing by the centre and deuiding the line A C into two equall partes, it ſhall alſo deuide it perpendicularly. And for aſmuch as the line A C deuideth the line B D into two equall partes & right angled wiſe: therfore by the Correlary of the firſt of thys booke, the line A C paſſeth by the centre of the circle: which is cõtrary to the ſuppoſition. Wherfore the lines A C and B D do not deuide the one the other into two equall partes: which was required to be proued.

## The 4. Theoreme: The 5. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If two circles cut the one the other, they haue not one and the ſame centre.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:S|lines=3|style=decorated|decoration="foliate pattern"}Vppoſe that theſe two circles A B C, and C B G do cut the one the other in the poyntes C and B. Then I ſay that they haue not one & the ſame centre. For if it be poſſible let E be centre to them both. And (by the firſt petition) draw a line from E to C. And draw an other right line E F G at all aduentures.

[Margin]
Conſtruction.
[/Margin]

[Diagram: two circles intersecting at C and B, with vertical line C E and horizontal line G F E; A labelled on the right circumference]

And for aſmuch as the poynt E is the centre of the circle A B C, therefore (by the 15. definition of the firſt) the line E C is equall vnto the line E F. Agayne for aſmuch as the poynt E is the centre of the circle C B G, therefore (by the ſame definition) the line E C is equall vnto the line E G. And it is proued that the line E C is equall vnto the line E F: wherfore the line E F alſo is equall vnto the line E G, namely the leſſe vnto the greater: which is impoſſible.

[Margin]
Demonſtration leading to an impoſſibilitie.
[/Margin]

VVherfore the poynt E is not the centre of both the circles A B C, & C B G. In like ſort alſo may we proue that no other poynt is the centre of both the ſayd circles.
