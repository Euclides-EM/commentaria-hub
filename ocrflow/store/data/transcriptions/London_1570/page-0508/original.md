## ¶ The 19. Theoreme. The 19. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
*Three numbers beyng geuen, to ſearch out if it be poſſible the fourth number proportionall with them.*
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
Diuers caſes in this propoſition.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="foliate pattern"}uppoſe that the three numbers geuen be A, B, C. It is required to ſearch out if it be poſſible a fourth number proportionall with them. Now A, B, C, are either in continuall proportion, and their extremes A, C are prime the one to the other: or they are not in continuall proportion, and their extremes are yet prime the one to the other: or they are in continuall proportion, and their extremes are not prime the one to the other: or they are neither in continuall proportion, nor their extremes are prime the one to the other.

[Margin]
The firſt caſe.
[/Margin]

If A, B, C, be in continuall proportion, and their extremes be prime the one to the other: it is manifeſt (by the 17. of the ninth) that it is impoſſible to finde out a fourth number proportionall with them.

[Margin]
The ſecond caſe.
[/Margin]

But now ſuppoſe that A, B, C, be not in continuall proportion, and yet let their extremes be prime the one to the other.

[Diagram: three dotted horizontal lines labelled A, B and C.]

Then I ſay that ſo alſo it is impoſſible to finde out a fourth number proportionall with them. For if it be poſſible, let there be found ſuch a number, and let the ſame be D. So that as A is to B, ſo let C be to D; and as B is to C, ſo let D be to E. And for that as A is to B, ſo is C to D, and as B is to C, ſo is D to E; therefore of equallitie (by the 14. of the ſeuenth) as A is to C, ſo is C to E. But A and C are prime the one to the other, yea they are prime, and the leaſt: but the leſt meaſure the numbers that haue one & the ſame proportion with them equally, the antecedent, the antecedent, and the conſequent the conſequent (by the 21. of the ſeuenth).

[Diagram: five horizontal lines labelled A, B, C, D and E; A, B and C dotted, D and E solid.]

Wherefore A meaſureth C, namely, the antecedent the antecedent, and it alſo meaſureth it ſelfe. Wherefore A meaſureth theſe numbers A and C being prime the one to the other, which is impoſſible. Wherefore it is not poſſible to finde out a fourth number proportionall with theſe numbers A, B, C.

[Margin]
The third caſe.
[/Margin]

But now againe ſuppoſe that A, B, C, be in continuall proportion, and let A and C not be prime the one to the other.

[Diagram: five horizontal lines labelled A, B, C, E and D.]

Then I ſay that it is poſſible to finde out a fourth number proportionall with them. For let B multiplying C produce D. Now A either meaſureth D, or meaſureth it not. Firſt let it meaſure it, and that by E. Wherefore A multiplying E produced D. But B alſo multiplying C produced D. Wherefore that which is produced of AE is equall to that which is produced of BC: wherefore in what proportiõ A is to B, in yͤ ſame is C to E. Wherefore there is found out a fourth number, namely, E, proportionall with theſe numbers A, B, C.
