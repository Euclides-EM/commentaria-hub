## ¶ The 5. Theoreme.  The 7. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
Magnitudes incommenſurable, haue not that proportion the one to the other, that number hath to number.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
Demonſtration leading to an abſurditie.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated}uppoſe that the magnitudes A and B be incommenſurable. Then I ſay, that A hath not to B, that proportion that number hath to number. For if A haue vnto B that proportion that number hath to number; then is A commenſurable vnto B (by the 6. of this tenth). But (by ſuppoſition) it is not. Wherefore A hath not vnto B that proportion that number hath to number. Magnitudes incommenſurable therfore haue not that proportion the one to the other that number hath to number: which was required to be demonſtrated.

## ¶ The 6. Theoreme.  The 8. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If two magnitudes haue not that proportion the one to the other that number hath to number, thoſe magnitudes are incommenſurable.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
This is the conuerſe of the former, and is proued by an indirect demonſtration.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated}uppoſe that theſe two magnitudes A and B, haue not that proportion the one to the other that number hath to number: Then I ſay, that A and B are magnitudes incommenſurable. For if A and B be commenſurable, then ſhall A haue vnto B, that proportion that number hath to number (by the 5. of this tenth). But (by ſuppoſition) it hath not that proportion that number hath to number. Wherefore A and B are incommenſurable magnitudes. If therfore two magnitudes haue not that proportion the one to the other that number hath to number, thoſe magnitudes are incommenſurable: which was required to be proued.

## ¶ The 7. Theoreme.  The 9. Propoſition.

[Margin]
1.
[/Margin]

Squares deſcribed of right lines commenſurable in length, haue that proportion the one to the other, that a ſquare number hath to a ſquare number.

[Margin]
2.
[/Margin]

And ſquares which haue that proportion the one to the other that a ſquare number hath to a ſquare number, ſhall alſo haue their ſides commenſurable in length.

[Margin]
3.
[/Margin]

But ſquares deſcribed of right lines incommenſurable in length, haue not that proportion the one to the other, that a ſquare number hath to a ſquare number.

[Margin]
4.
[/Margin]

And ſquares which haue not that proportion the one to the other that a ſquare number hath to a ſquare number, haue not their ſides commenſurable in length.
