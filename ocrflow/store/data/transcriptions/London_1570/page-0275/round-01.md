pon the right line A B to deſcribe a ſegment of a circle, which ſhall contayne an angle equall to the right angle C. Deſcribe againe vpon the right line A B and to the point in it A an angle B A D equall to the rectiline angle geuen C (by the 23. of the firſt) as it is ſet forth in the ſecond deſcription. And (by the 10. of the firſt) deuide the line A B into two equall partes in the point F. And making the centre the point F and the ſpace F A or F B deſcribe (by the 3. petition) a circle A E B.

[Diagram: circle with diameter A B, centre F, point E at the top, chords A E and E B, line E F, tangent A D, and a separate right angle C]

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

Wherfore the right line A D toucheth the circle A E B: for that the angle B A D is a right angle. Wherfore yᵉ angle B A D is equall to the angle which is in the ſegment A E B, for the angle which is in a ſemicircle is a right angle (by the 31. of the third) But the angle B A D is equall to the angle C. Wherfore there is againe deſcribed vpon the line A B a ſegment of a circle, namely, A E B, which containeth an angle equall to the angle geuen namely, to C.

[Margin]
The third caſe.
Conſtruction.
[/Margin]

But now ſuppoſe that the angle C be an obtuſe angle. Vpon the right line A B and to the point in it A deſcribe (by the 23. of the firſt) an angle B A D equall to the angle C: as it is in the third deſcription. And from the point A rayſe vp vnto the line A D a perpendiculer line A E (by the 11. of the firſt) And agayne by the 10. of the firſt) deuide the line A B into two equall partes in the point F. And from the point F rayſe vp vnto the line A B a perpendicular line F G (by the 11. of the ſame) & drawe a line from G to B.

[Diagram: circle labelled A, B, E and H, with centre G, diameter A E, chords A B, A H and B H, lines F G, G A and G B, tangent A D, and a separate obtuse angle C]

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

And now foraſmuch as the line A F is equall to the line F B, and the line F G is common to them both, therfore theſe two lines A F and F G are equall to theſe two lines B F and F G: and the angle A F G is (by the 4. petition) equall to the angle B F G: wherfore (by the 4. of the ſame) the baſe A G is equall to the baſe G B. Wherfore making the centre G, and the ſpace G A deſcribe (by the 3. petition) a circle and it ſhall paſſe by the point B: let it be deſcribed as the circle A E B is. And foraſmuch as from the ende of the diameter A E is drawen a perpendicular line A D, therefore (by the correllary of the 16. of the third) the line A D toucheth the circle A E B & from the point of the touche, namely, A, is extended the line A B. Wherfore (by the 32. of the third) the angle B A D is equall to the angle A H B which is in the alternate ſegment of the circle. But the angle B A D is equall to the angle C.
