An euen number is that which in one and the ſame diuision is deuided into the greateſt, and into the leaſt: into the greateſt, as touching ſpace, and into the leaſt as touching quantitie. As 10. is deuided into 5 & 5. which are his greateſt partes. (Which greatnes of partes he calleth ſpace) and in the ſame diuision the number 10. is deuided but into two partes: but into leſſe then two partes nothing can be deuided, which thing he calleth quantitie, ſo that 10. deuided into 5 and 5. in that one deuision, is deuided into the greateſt, namely, into his halues, and into the leaſt, namely, into two partes, and no mo.

There is alſo another definition more auncient, which is thus.

[Margin]
An other definition.
[/Margin]

An euen number is that which may be deuided into two equall partes, and into two vnequall partes, but in neyther diuision (to the conſtitution of the whole) to the euen part is added the odde, neither to the odde is added the euen. As 8 may be deuided diuersly, partly into euen partes, as into 4 and 4: likewiſe into 6 and 2: and partly into odde partes, as into 5 and 3. alſo into 7 and 1. In all which deuisions, ye ſee no odde parte ioyned to an euen, nor an euen part ioyned to an odde: but if the one be euen, the other is euen, and if the one be odde, the other is odde. In the two firſt deuisions, both partes were euen, and in the two laſt, both partes were odde. It is to be conſidered that the two partes added together muſt make the whole.

This diffinition is generall and common to all euen numbers, except to the number 2. which can not be deuided into two vnequall partes, but onely into two vnities, which are equall.

[Margin]
An other definition.
[/Margin]

There is yet geuen an other diffinition of an euen number, namely, thus. An euen number is that which onely by an Vnitie either aboue it or vnder it, differeth from an odde. As 8 being an euen number differeth from 9 an odde number, being aboue it but by one. And alſo from 7. vnder it, it differeth likewiſe by one, and ſo of others.

## 7 An odde number is that which cannot be deuided into two equall partes: or that which onely by an vnitie differeth from an euen number.

[Margin]
The ſeuenth definition.
[/Margin]

As the number 5 can be by no meanes deuided into two equall partes, namely, ſuch two which added together, ſhall make 5. Or by the ſecond definition, 5 an odde number, differeth from 6 an euen number aboue it, by 1. And the ſame 5 differeth from 4 an euen number vnder it likewiſe by 1.

[Margin]
An other definition of an od number.
[/Margin]

Boetius defineth an odde number after this maner.

An odde number is that, which cannot be deuided into two equall partes, but that an Vnitie ſhall be betwene them. As if ye deuide 5 into 2 and 2, which are two equall partes, there remaineth one or an vnitie betwene them to make the whole number 5.

[Margin]
An other definition.
[/Margin]

There is yet an other definition of an odde number. An odde number is that, which being deuided into two vnequall partes, howſoeuer, the one is euen euen, and the other odde. As if 9 be deuided into two partes, which added together, maketh the whole, namely, into 4 and 5. which are vnequall: ye ſee the one is euen, namely, 4. and the other is odde, namely, 5. ſo if ye deuide 9. into 6 and 3: or into 8 and 1. the one part is euer euen and the other odde.

## 8 A number euenly euen, (called in latine pariter par) is that number, which an euen number meaſureth by an euen number.

[Margin]
The eight definition.
[/Margin]

As 8 is a number euenly euen. For 4 an euen number meaſureth 8 by 2, which is alſo an euen number. This definition hath much troubled many, and ſeemeth not a true definition, for that there are many numbers, which euen numbers do meaſure, and that by euen numbers, which yet are not euenly euen numbers, after moſt mens minds: as 24. which 6 an euen number doth meaſure by foure, which is alſo an euen number, and yet as they thinke is not 24 an euenly euen number, for that 8 an euen number doth meaſure alſo 24 by 3. an odde number.

[Margin]
Campane.
[/Margin]

Wherfore Campane to make his ſentence plaine, after this maner ſetteth forth this definition. Pariter par eſt, quem cuncti pares eum numerantes, paribus vicibus numerant, that is:

[Margin]
An other definition of an euenly euen number.
[/Margin]

An euenly euen number is, when all the euen numbers which meaſure it, doo meaſure it by euen times, that is, by euen numbers, as 16. All the euen numbers which meaſure 16, as are 8. 4. and 2. do meaſure it by euen numbers. As 8 by 2, twiſe 8 is 16: 4 by 4, foure times 4 is 16: and 2 by 8, 8 times 2 is 16. Which particle (all euen numbers) added by Campane maketh 24 to be no euenly euen number: For that ſome one euen number meaſureth it by an odde number as 8 by 3.

[Margin]
Fluſſates.
[/Margin]

Fluſſates alſo is plainly of this minde, that Euclide gaue not this definition in ſuch maner as it is by Theon written, for the largenes & generalitie therof, for that it extendeth it to infinite numbers which are not euenly euen as he thinketh, for which cauſe in place therof, he geueth this definition.

[Margin]
An other definition.
[/Margin]

A number euenly euen, is that which onely euen numbers do meaſure. As 16 is meaſured of none but of euen numbers, and therfore is euenly euen.
