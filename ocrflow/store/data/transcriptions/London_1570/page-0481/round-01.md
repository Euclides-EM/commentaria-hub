## ¶ The 15. Theoreme.  The 17. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If a cube number meaſure not a cube number, neither ſhall the ſide of the one meaſure the ſide of the other. And if the ſide of the one meaſure not the ſide of the other, neither ſhall the cube number meaſure the cube number.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="ornamental design"}uppoſe that the cube number A do not meaſure the cube number B: and let the ſide of A be C: and the ſide of B be D. Then I ſay, that C ſhall not meaſure D. For if C do meaſure D, then (by the 15. of the eight) A alſo ſhall meaſure B. But (by ſuppoſition) A meaſureth not B: wherefore neither ſhall C meaſure D.

[Margin]
A negatiue propoſition.
[/Margin]

[Margin]
The firſt parte of this propoſition.
[/Margin]

[Diagram: four dotted horizontal lines labelled A, B, C, and D]

But now ſuppoſe that the ſide C meaſure not the ſide D. Then I ſay, that neither ſhall the cube number A meaſure the cube number B. For if A do meaſure B, then alſo (by the 15. of the eight) ſhall C meaſure D. But (by ſuppoſition) C meaſureth not D. Wherefore neither ſhall A meaſure B: which was required to be proued.

[Margin]
The ſecond part is the conuerſe of the firſt.
[/Margin]

## ¶ The 16. Theoreme.  The 18. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
Betwene two like plaine or ſuperficiall numbers there is one meane proportionall number. And the one like plaine number is to the other like plaine number in double proportion of that, which the ſide of like proportion, is to the ſide of like proportion.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="ornamental design"}uppoſe that there be two like plaine or ſuperficiall numbers A & B. And let the ſides of A be the numbers C, D: and the ſides of B be the numbers E, F. And foraſmuch as like plaine numbers are thoſe which haue their ſides proportionall (by the 22. definition of the ſeuenth) therefore as C is to D, ſo is E to F. Then I ſay that betwene A and B there is one meane proportionall number, and that A is vnto B in double proportion of that which C is vnto E, or of that which D is vnto F, that is, of that which ſide of like proportion is to ſide of like proportion.

[Margin]
Demonſtration of the firſt part of this propoſition.
[/Margin]

[Diagram: seven dotted horizontal lines labelled A, G, B, C, D, E, and F]

For for that as C is to D, ſo is E to F, therefore alternately (by the 13. of the ſeuenth) as C is to E, ſo is D to F. And foraſmuch as A is a plaine or ſuperficiall number, and the ſides thereof are C and D: therefore D multiplying C produced A. And by the ſame reaſon alſo E multiplying F produced B. Let D multiplying E produce G. And foraſmuch as D multiplying C produced A, and multiplying E produced G, therefore (by the 17. of the ſeuenth) as C is to E, ſo is A to G. But as C is to E, ſo is D to F, wherefore as D is to F, ſo is A to G.
