## ¶ The 29. Theoreme. The 31. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
Euery prime number is to euery number which it meaſureth not, prime.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
Demonſtration leading to an abſurditie.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="interlaced geometric ornamental design"}Vppoſe that there be geuen a prime number, namely, A: and let B be an other number, which it meaſureth not. Then I ſay, that the numbers B & A are prime the one to the other. For if A and B be not prime the one to the other, then ſome number meaſureth them. Let there be a number that meaſureth them, and let the ſame be C. Now C is no vnitie. And foraſmuch as C meaſureth B, but A meaſureth not B, therefore C is not one and the ſame number with A.

[Diagram: two horizontal dotted lines, the upper labelled A and B, and the lower labelled C]

And foraſmuch as C meaſureth A & B, it alſo meaſureth A being a prime number, and being not one and the ſame with it: which is impoſſible (by the 13. definition of the ſeuenth). VVherefore no number meaſureth theſe numbers A and B. And therefore A and B are prime the one to the other: which was required to be proued.

## ¶ The 30. Theoreme. The 32. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If two numbers multiplying the one the other produce any number, and if alſo ſome prime number meaſure that which is produced of them: then ſhall it alſo meaſure one of thoſe numbers which were put at the beginning.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=3|style=decorated|decoration="floral ornamental design"}Vppoſe that two numbers A and B multiplying the one to the other do produce the number C: and let ſome prime number, namely, D meaſure C. Then I ſay that D meaſureth one of theſe numbers either A or B. Suppoſe that it meaſure not A: now D is a prime number. VVherefore A and D are prime the one to the other (by the propoſition next going before). And how often D meaſureth C, ſo many vnities let there be in E.

[Diagram: five horizontal dotted lines labelled E, D, C, B, and A]

And foraſmuch as D meaſureth C by thoſe vnities which are in E, therefore D multiplying E produceth C: but A alſo multiplying B produced C, wherefore that which is produced of D into E is equal to that which is produced of A into B. VVherefore (by the 19. of the ſeuenth) as A is to D, ſo is E to B: but D and A are prime numbers: and therefore the leaſt numbers in that proportion: but the leaſt in any proportion meaſure the numbers hauing the ſelfe ſame proportion with them equally, the greater, the greater, and the leſſe, the leſſe, that is the antecedent, the antecedent, and the conſequent, the conſequent (by the 21. of the ſeuenth). VVherefore the conſequent D meaſureth the conſequent B. In like ſort may we proue that if D meaſure not B it meaſureth A. VVherefore D meaſureth one of theſe numbers A or B: which was required to be proued.
