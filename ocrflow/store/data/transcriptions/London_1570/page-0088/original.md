[Margin]
Two thinges geuen in this propoſition. Diuers caſes in it.
[/Margin]

This propoſition, which is a Probleme, hath two thinges geuen, namely, two vnequall right lines: the thing required is, from the greater to cut of a line equall to the leſſe. It hath alſo diuers caſes. For the lines geuen either are diſtinct th’one from the other: or are ioyned together at one of their endes: or they cut the one the other, or the one cutteth the other in one of the extreames. VVhich may be two wayes. For ether the greater cutteth the leſſe, or the leſſe the greater. If they cut the one the other, either ech cutteth th’other into equall partes: or into vnequall partes: or the one into equall partes, and the other into vnequall partes. VVhich may happen in two ſorts, for the greater may be cut into equall partes, and the leſſe into vnequall partes: or contrariwiſe.

[Margin]
The firſt caſe.
[/Margin]

When the vnequall lines geuen are diſtinct the one from the other, the figure before put ſerueth.

[Margin]
The ſecond caſe.
[/Margin]

If they be ioyned together at one of their endes, it is eaſie to do. For making the centre that end where they are ioyned together, & the ſpace the leſſe line, deſcribe a circle: whiche ſhall of neceſſitie (by the definition of a circle) cut of from the greater line a line equall to the leſſe line, as it is playne to ſee in the figure here put.

[Diagram: circle with joined lines labelled A, B, C, and D]

[Margin]
The third caſe.
[/Margin]

But if the one cut the other in one of the extreames. As for exãple: Suppose that the vnequall right lines geuen be AB and CD, of which let the line CD be the greater: And let the line CD cut the line AB in his extreame C. Then making the centre A and the ſpace AB, deſcribe a circle BF. And vpon the line AC deſcribe an equilater triangle (by the firſt) which let be AEC: & produce the lines EA and EC. And againe making the centre E and the ſpace EF deſcribe a circle GFL. Likewiſe making the centre C and the ſpace CG, deſcribe a circle GL. Now, foraſmuch, as the line EF is equall to the line EG (For E is the centre) of which the line EA is equall to the line EC: therfore the reſidue AF is equall to the reſidue CG. But the line AF is equall to the line AB, for A is the centre, wherefore alſo the line CG is equall to the line AB. But the line CG is alſo equall to the line CL, for the point C is the centre. VVherefore the line AB is equall to the line CL. VVherefore from the line CD is cut of the line CL, which is equall to the line AB.

[Diagram: three overlapping circles and intersecting lines labelled A, B, C, D, E, F, G, and L]

[Margin]
The fourth caſe.
[/Margin]

But now let CD be leſſe then AB, and let it cut AB by his extreame C. Now then eyther it cutteth it in the middeſt or not in the middeſt. Firſt let it cut it in the middeſt: then CD is ether the halfe of AB, & ſo is AC equall to CD.

[Diagram: circle with horizontal line AB and perpendicular line CD meeting at C]

[Margin]
The fifth caſe.
[/Margin]

Or it is leſſe then the halfe: and then making the centre C & the ſpace CD deſcribe a circle, which ſhall cut of from the line AB a line equal to the line CD.

[Margin]
The ſixt caſe.
[/Margin]

Or it is greater then the halfe. And thẽ vnto the poynt A put the line AF equall to the line CD, by the ſecond. And making the centre A & the ſpace AF deſcribe a circle, which ſhall cut of from the line AB a line equall to the line AF, that is, vnto the line CD.

[Diagram: circle with lines labelled A, B, C, D, and F]
