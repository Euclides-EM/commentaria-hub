The old Philoſopher Thales Mileſius was the firſt inuenter of this ſixth propoſition, as alſo of many other.

[Ornament: pointing hand]

## The third Theoreme. The ſixt Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If a triangle haue two angles equall the one to the other: the ſides alſo of the ſame, which ſubtend the equall angles, ſhalbe equall the one to the other.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
Conſtruction.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=7|style=decorated|decoration="foliate design"}Vppoſe that ABC be a triangle, hauing the angle ABC equall to the angle ACB. Then I ſay, that the ſide AB is equall to the ſide AC. For if the ſide AB be not equall to the ſide AC, then one of them is greater. Let AB be the greater. And by the third propoſition, from AB being the greater cut of a line equall to the leſſe line, which is AC. And let the ſame be DB. And drawe a line from the poynt D to the poynt C.

[Margin]
Demonſtration leading to an impoſſibility.
[/Margin]

[Diagram: triangle ABC with D on AB and a line from D to C]

Now foraſmuch as the ſide DB is equall to the ſyde AC, and the line BC is common to them both: therefore theſe two ſydes DB and BC are equall to theſe two ſydes AC & CB the one to the other. And the angle DBC is by ſuppoſition equall to the angle ACB. VVherfore (by the 4. propoſition) the baſe DC is equall to the baſe AB: & (by the ſame) the triangle DBC is equall to the triangle ACB: namely, the leſſe triangle vnto the greater triangle, which is impoſſible. VVherfore the ſyde AB is not vnequall to the ſide AC. VVherfore it is equall. If therefore a triangle haue two angles equall the one to the other: the ſydes alſo of the ſame, which ſubtende the equall angles, ſhall be equall the one to the other: which was required to be demonſtrated.

[Margin]
The chiefeſt and moſt proper kind of conuerſion.
[/Margin]

In Geometrie is oftentimes vſed conuerſion of propoſitions. As this propoſition is the conuerſe of the propoſition next before. The chiefeſt and moſt proper kind of conuerſion is, when that which was the thing ſuppoſed in the former propoſition, is the concluſion of the conuerſe and ſecond propoſition: and contrary wiſe that which was concluded in the firſt, is the thing ſuppoſed in the ſecond. As in the fifth propoſition it was ſuppoſed the two ſides of a triangle to be equall, the thing concluded is, that the two angles at the baſe are equall, & in this propoſition, which is the conuerſe therof is ſuppoſed that the angles at the baſe be equall. VVhich in the former propoſition was the concluſion, And the concluſion is, that the two ſydes ſubtending the two angles are equall, which in the former propoſition was the ſuppoſition. This is the chiefeſt kind of conuerſion vniforme and certayne.
