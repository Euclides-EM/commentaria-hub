### Here Fluſſates addeth this Corollary.

[Subhead]
If two numbers hauing one and the ſame proportion with two other numbers, do multiply the one the other alternately, and produce any numbers, the numbers produced of them ſhall be equall the one to the other.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
A Corollary added by Fluſſates.
[/Margin]

Suppoſe that there be two numbers A and B, and alſo two other numbers C and D, hauing the ſame proportion that the numbers A and B haue: and let the numbers A and B multiply the numbers C & D alternately, that is, let A multiplieng D produce F, and let B multiplieng C produce E. Then I ſay that the numbers produced namely, E & F are equall. Let A and B multiply the one the other in ſuch ſort, that let A multiplieng B produce G, and let B multiplieng A produce H. Now then the numbers G and H are equall by the 16. of this booke. And foraſmuch as A multiplieng the two numbers B and D, produced the numbers G and F, therfore G is to F, as B is to D by this propoſition. So likewiſe B multiplieng the two numbers A and C produced the two numbers H and E. VVherfore by the ſame H is to E as A is to C. But alternately (by the 13. of this booke) A is to C as B is to D, but as A is to C ſo is H to E, and as B is to D, ſo is G to F. VVherfore by the ſeuenth common ſentence, as H is to E, ſo is G to F. VVherfore alternately (by the 13. of this booke) H is to G as E is to F. But it is proued that G & H are equall: wherfore E and F (which haue the ſame proportion that A and B haue) are equall. If therefore there be two numbers, &c. Which was required to be proued.

## ¶ The 16. Theoreme. The 18. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If two numbers multiply any number, & produce other numbers: the numbers of them produced, ſhall be in the ſame proportion that the numbers multiplying are.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="latticework"}uppoſe that two numbers A and B multiplying the number C, doo produce the numbers D and E. Then I ſay that as A is to B, ſo is D to E. For foraſmuch as A multiplying C produced D, therfore C multiplying A produceth alſo D (by the 16. of this booke.) And by the ſame reaſon C multiplying B produceth E. Now then one number C multiplying two numbers A and B, produceth the numbers D and E. VVherfore by the 17. of the ſeuenth, as A is to B, ſo is D to E: which was required to be demonſtrated.

[Diagram: five horizontal dotted lines labelled A, B, C, D, and E]

¶ This Propoſition, and the former touching two numbers, may be extended to numbers how many ſoeuer. So that if one number multiply numbers how many ſoeuer, and produce any numbers, the proportion of the numbers produced, and of the numbers multiplied, ſhall be one and the ſelfe ſame. Likewiſe if numbers how many ſoeuer multiply one number, and produce any numbers, the proportion of the numbers produced, and of the numbers multiplying ſhall be one and the ſelfe ſame: which thing by this and the former propoſition repeted as often as is needefull, is not hard to proue.

[Margin]
This propoſition and the former may be extended to numbers how many ſoeuer.
[/Margin]

## ¶ The 17. Theoreme. The 19. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If there be foure numbers in proportion: the number produced of the firſt and the fourth, is equall to that number which is produced of the ſecond and the third. And if the number which is produced of the firſt and the fourth be equall to that which is produced of the ſecond & the third: thoſe foure numbers ſhall be in proportion.
[/Subhead]
