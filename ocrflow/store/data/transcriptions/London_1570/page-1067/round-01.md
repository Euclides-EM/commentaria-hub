## ¶ The 26. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
To proue that a trilater equilater Pyramis is duple to an Octohedron inſcribed in it.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:L|lines=5|style=decorated|decoration="floral ornament"}Et there be taken a trilater Pyramis ABCD: whoſe ſixe ſides let be cut into two equall partes, in the pointes E, K, F, L, G, and H: inſcribing thereby an Octohedron in the pyramis, by the 2. of the ſiuetenth. Wherefore the pyramids AEGH, BEFK, CFGL, & DKHL, fall without the Octohedron inſcribed, by the ſame ſecond of the ſiuetenth. But the outward Pyramids (namely, AEGH, and the three other) are like vnto the whole pyramis, by the 7. definition of the eleuenth. For the baſes of the whole pyramis are by parallel lines drawen in them cut into like triangles, by the Corollary of the 2. of the ſixth, of which the foreſayd pyramids are made. Wherefore the whole pyramis is to euery one of them in treble proportion of that in which the ſides of like proportion are, by the 8. of the twelfth. But by conſtruction, the proportion of the ſide AB to the ſide AE is duple. Wherefore the whole pyramis ABCD is octuple to the pyramis AEGH, and ſo is it to euery one of the pyramids which are equall to AEGH. For duple proportion multiplyed into it ſelfe twice maketh octuple. Wherefore it followeth that the 4. pyramids AEGH, BEFK, CFGL, and DKHL, taken together, make the halfe of the whole pyramis ABCD. Wherefore the reſidue, namely, the Octohedron EGLKHF, is the other halfe of the pyramis. Wherfore the pyramis is duple to the Octohedron. Wherefore we haue proued that a trilater equilater pyramis is duple to an Octohedron inſcribed in it.
