## The 22. Theoreme. The 32, Propoſition.

If one of the ſydes of any triangle be produced: the outwarde angle that it maketh, is equal to the two inward and oppoſite angles. And the three inwarde angles of a triangle are equall to two right angles.

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="floral woodcut"}Vppoſe ỹ A B C be a triangle, & produce one of ỹ ſides therof namely, C B to the pointe D. Then I ſay, that the outwarde angle A C D is equall to the two inwarde and oppoſite angles C A B & A B C: and ỹ the three inwarde angles of the triangle, that is, the angles A B C, B C A, and C A B are equall to two right angles. For (by the propoſition going before) rayſe vp frõ the point C, a parallel to the right line A B, and let the ſame be C E. And foraſmuch as A B is a parallel to C E, and vpon them falleth the right line A C: therefore the alternate angles B A C and A C E are equall the one to the other. Agayne foraſmuch as A B is a parallel vnto C E, and vpon them falleth the right line B D, therfore the outward angle E C D is (by the 29. propoſition) equall to the inward and oppoſite angle A B C. And it is proued that the angle A C E is equal to the angle B A C: wherfore the whole outwarde angle A C D is equall to the two inward and oppoſite angles, that is, to the angles B A C and A B C. Put the angle A C B common to them both. VVherfore the angles A C D and A C B, are equall to theſe three angles A B C, B C A, and B A C. But the angles A C D & A C B are equall to two right angles (by the 13. propoſition): wherfore the angles A C B, C B A, and C A B are equall to two right angles.
