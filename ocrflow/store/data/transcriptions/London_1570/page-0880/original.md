For if it be poſſible, let it be to a greater, namely, to the ſuperficies S. Wherefore by conuerſion, as the ſquare of the line F H is to the ſquare of the line B D, ſo is the ſuperficies S to the circle A B C D. * But as the ſuperficies S is to the circle A B C D, ſo is the circle E F G H to ſome ſuperficies leſſe thẽ the circle A B C D: Wherefore (by the 11. of the fift) as the ſquare of the line F H is to the ſquare of the line B D, ſo is the circle E F G H, to ſome ſuperficies leſſe then the circle A B C D: which is in the firſt caſe proued to be impoſſible. Wherefore as the ſquare of the line B D is to the ſquare of the line F H, ſo is not the circle A B C D to any ſuperficies greater then the circle E F G H. And it is alſo proued that it is not, to any leſſe. Wherefore as the ſquare of the line B D is to the ſquare of the line F H, ſo is the circle A B C D to the circle E F G H. Wherefore circles are in that proportion the one to the other, that the ſquares of their diameters are: which was required to be proued.
