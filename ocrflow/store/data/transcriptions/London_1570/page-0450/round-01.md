## ¶ The 12. Theoreme. The 14. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If there be a multitude of numbers how many ſoeuer, and alſo other numbers equall vnto them in multitude, which being compared two and two are in one and the ſame proportion: they ſhall alſo of equalitie be in one and the ſame proportion.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
This in diſcrete quantity anſwereth to the twenty one propoſition of the fifth booke in continuall quantitie. Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="ornamental latticework"}uppoſe that there be a multitude of numbers how many ſoeuer, namely, A,B,C, and let the other numbers equall vnto them in multitude be D,E,F: which being compared two and two, let be in one and the ſame proportion: that is, as A to B, ſo let D be to E: and as B is to C, ſo let E be to F. Then I ſay, that of equalitie, as A is to C, ſo is D to F. For foraſmuch as by ſuppoſition, as A is to B, ſo is D to E: therefore alternately alſo (by the 13. of the ſeuenth) as A is to D, ſo is B to E. Againe, for that as B is to C, ſo is E to F: therefore alternately alſo (by the ſelf ſame) as B is to E, ſo is C to F. But as B is to E, ſo is A to D. VVherfore (by the ſeuenth common ſentence of the ſeuenth) as A is to D, ſo is C to F. VVherfore alternately (by the 13. of the ſeuenth) as A is to C, ſo is D to F: which was required to be demonſtrated.

[Margin]
Certaine additions of Campane.
[/Margin]

After this Propoſition, Campane demonſtrateth in numbers theſe foure kindes of proportionalitie, namely, proportion conuerſe, compoſed, deuided, and euerſe: which were in continuall quantitie, demonſtrated in the 4. 17. 18. and 19. propoſitions of the fift booke. And firſt he demonſtrateth conuerſe proportion in this maner.

[Margin]
Two caſes in this propoſition. The firſt caſe.
[/Margin]

Suppoſe that the number A be to the number B, as the number C is to the number D. Then I ſay, that conuerſedly B is to A, as D is to C. For if A be leſſe then B, C alſo ſhall be leſſe then D, and what part or partes A is of B, the ſelfe ſame part or partes is C of D. VVherefore B is equemultiplex to A, as D is to C. VVherefore (by the 21. definition of this booke) as B is to A, ſo is D to C.

[Diagram: four horizontal dotted lines labelled A, B, C, and D]

[Margin]
The ſecond caſe.
[/Margin]

But if A be greater then B, C alſo is greater then D: and what part or partes B is of A, the ſelfe ſame part or partes is D of C. VVherefore (by the ſame definition) as B is to A, ſo is D to C: which was required to be proued.

### Proportionalitie deuided, is thus demonſtrated.

[Margin]
Proportionalitie deuided.
[/Margin]

Suppoſe that the number A B be to the number B, as the number C D is to the number D. Then I ſay, that deuided alſo, as A is to B, ſo is C to D: for for that as A B is to B, ſo is C D to D: therefore alternately (by the 14. of this booke) as A B is to C D, ſo is B to D. VVherefore (by the 11. of this booke) as A B is to C D, ſo is A to C. VVherefore as B is to D, ſo is A to C: and for that as A is to C, ſo is B to D, therefore alternately as A is to B, ſo is C to D.

### Proportionalitie compoſed, is thus demonſtrated.

[Margin]
Proportionalitie compoſed.
[/Margin]

If A be vnto B, as C is to D: then ſhall A B be to B, as C D is to D. For alternately A is to C, as B is to D. VVherefore (by the 13. of this booke) as A B, namely, all the antecedentes are to C D, namely, to all the conſequentes, ſo is B to D, namely, one of the antecedentes to one of the conſequentes. VVherefore alternately as A B is to B, ſo is C D to D.
