## ¶ The 7. Theoreme.  The 9. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If two numbers be prime the one to the other, and if betwene them ſhall fall numbers in continuall proportion: how many numbers in continuall proportion fall betwene them, ſo many alſo ſhall fall in continuall proportion betwene either of thoſe numbers and vnitie.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
Conſtruction.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="ornamental floral design"}uppoſe that there be two numbers prime the one to the other A and B: and let there fall betwene them in continuall proportion theſe numbers C and D: and let E be vnitie. Then I ſay, that how many numbers in continuall proportion fall betwene A and B, ſo many alſo ſhall fall in continuall proportion betwene A and vnitie E: and likewiſe betwene B and vnitie E. Take (by the 35. of the ſeuenth) the two leaſt numbers that are in the ſame proportion that A,C,D,B, are: and let the ſame be F and G: and then take three of the leaſt nũbers that are in the ſame proportion that A,C,D,B, are: and let the ſame be H,K,L: and ſo alwaies in order one more, vntill the multitude of them be equall to the multitude of theſe numbers A,C,D,B: and thoſe being ſo taken let them be M,N,X,O.

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

Now it is manifeſt, that F multiplying him ſelfe produced H, and multiplying H produced M. And G multiplying him ſelfe produced L, and multiplying L produced O: And foraſmuch as M,N,X,O, are (by ſuppoſition) the leaſt of all numbers that haue the ſame proportion with G,F: and A,C,D,B, are (by the firſt of the eight) the leaſt of all numbers that haue the ſame proportion with G,F: and the multitude of theſe numbers M,N,X,O, is equall to the multitude of theſe numbers A,C,D,B: therefore euery one of theſe numbers M,N,X,O, is equall to euery one of theſe numbers A,C,D,B. Wherefore M is equall vnto

A, and O is equall vnto B. And foraſmuch as F multiplying him ſelfe produced H: therfore F meaſureth H by thoſe vnities which are in F: and vnitie E meaſureth F by thoſe vnities which are in F: wherfore (by the 15. of the ſeuenth) vnitie E, ſo many times meaſureth the number F, as F meaſureth H: wherefore as vnitie E is to the number F, ſo is F to H. Againe, foraſmuch as F multiplying H produced M, therfore H meaſureth M by thoſe vnities which are in F. And vnitie E meaſureth F by thoſe vnities which are in F: wherefore (by the ſelfe ſame) vnitie E ſo many times meaſureth F, as H meaſureth M. Wherefore as vnitie E is to the number F, ſo is H to M. But it is proued, that as vnitie E is to the number F, ſo is F to H: wherefore as vnitie E is to the number F, ſo is F to H, and H to M. But M is equall vnto A: wherefore as vnitie E is to the number F, ſo is F to H, & H to A. And by the ſame reaſon as vnitie E is to the number G, ſo is G to L and L to B.
