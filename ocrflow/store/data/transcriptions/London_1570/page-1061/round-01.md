## ¶ The 15. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
The ſide of the Octohedron, is in power quadruple ſeſquialter to that right line, whoſe greater ſegment is the ſide of the Dodecahedron inſcribed in the ſame Octohedron.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:F|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="foliate design"}Oraſmuch as in the 14. of this booke, it was proued, that the ſide of the Octohedron is in power quadruple ſeſquialter to the ſide of the cube inſcribed in it: but the ſide of the cube being cut by an extreme and meane proportion, maketh the greater ſegment the ſide of the Dodecahedron circumſcribed about it, by the 3. Corollary of the 13. of the fiuetenth: therefore the ſide of the Octohedron is in power quadruple ſeſquialter to that right line (namely, to the ſide of the cube) whoſe greater ſegment is the ſide of the Dodecahedron inſcribed in the cube. But the Dodecahedron and the cube inſcribed one within an other, are inſcribed in one and the ſelfe ſame Octohedron, by the Corollary of the firſt of this booke. The ſide therefore of the Octohedron, is in power quadruple ſeſquialter to that right line, whoſe greater ſegment is the ſide of the Dodecahedron inſcribed in the ſame Octohedron.

## ¶ The 16. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
The ſide of an Icoſahedron, is the greater ſegment of that right line, which is in power duple to the ſide of the Octohedron inſcribed in the ſame Icoſahedron.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:S|lines=5|style=decorated|decoration="interlace design"}Vppoſe that there be an Icoſahedron A B G D E H F C: whoſe ſide let be B G or E C: and let the Octohedron inſcribed in it be A K D L: and let the ſide therof be, A L. Then I ſay, that the ſide E C is the greater ſegment of that right line which is in power duple to the ſide A L. For foraſmuch as figures inſcribed and circumſcribed haue one & the ſelf ſame centre, by the Corollary of the 21. of the fiuetenth, let the ſame be the point I. Now right lines drawen by that centre to the midle ſections of the oppoſite ſides, namely, the lines A I D and K I L, do, in the point I cut the one the other into two equall partes, and perpendicularly, by the Corollary of the 14. of the fiuetenth: and foraſmuch as they couple the midle ſections of the oppoſite lines B G and H F, therefore they cut them perpendicularly: wherefore alſo the lines B G and H F, are parallels, by the 4. Corollary of the 14. of the fiuetenth. Now then draw a line from B to H: and the ſayd line B H ſhall be equall and parallel to the line K L, by the 33. of the firſt. But the line B H ſubtendeth two ſides of the pentagon which is compoſed of the ſides of the Icoſahedron, namely, the ſides B A and A H: Wherfore the line B H being cut by an extreme and meane proportion maketh the greater ſegment the ſide of the pentagon, by the 8. of the thirtenth: which ſide is alſo the ſide of the Icoſahedron, namely, E C. And vnto the line B H the line K L, is equall: and the line K L is in power duple to A L the ſide of the Octohedron, by the 47. of the firſt: For in the ſquare A K D L the angle K A L is a right angle. Wherefore E C the ſide of the Icoſahedron, is the greater ſegment of the line B H or K L, which is in power duple to A L the ſide of the Octohedron inſcribed in the Icoſahedron. Wherefore the ſide of an Icoſahedron, is the greater ſegment of that right line, which is in power duple to the ſide of the Octohedron inſcribed in the ſame Icoſahedron.

## ¶ The 17. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
The ſide of a Cube is to the ſide of a Dodecahedron inſcribed in it, in duple proportion of an extreame and meane proportion.
[/Subhead]
