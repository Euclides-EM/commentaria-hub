## The 25. Theoreme.  The 27. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
Like ſolide numbers are in the ſame proportion the one to the other, that a cube number is to a cube number.
[/Subhead]

### ¶ A Corollary added by Fluſſates.

[Subhead]
If two numbers be in the ſame proportion the one to the other that a ſquare number is to a ſquare number: thoſe two numbers ſhall be like ſuperficiall numbers. And if they be in the ſame proportion the one to the other that a cube number is to a cube number, they ſhall be like ſolide numbers.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
A Corollary added by Fluſſates.
[/Margin]

Firſt let the number A haue vnto the number B the ſame proportion, that the ſquare number C hath to the ſquare number D. Then I ſay, that A and B are like ſuperficiall numbers. For foraſmuch as betwene the ſquare numbers C and D there falleth a meane proportionall (by the 11. of this booke) there ſhall

[Diagram: four dotted horizontal lines arranged in two columns, labelled B and A on the left and D and C on the right]

alſo betwene A and B (which haue one and the ſame proportion with C and D) fall a meane proportionall (by the 8. of this booke). Wherefore A and B are like ſuperficiall numbers (by the 20. of this booke).

But if A be vnto B, as the cube number C, is to the cube number D. Then are A & B like ſolide numbers. For foraſmuch as C and D are cube numbers, there falleth betwene them two meane proportionall numbers

[Diagram: four dotted horizontal lines arranged in two columns, labelled A and B on the left and C and D on the right]

(by the 12. of this booke). And therefore (by the 8. of the ſame) betwene A and B (which are in the ſame proportion that C is to D) there falleth alſo two meane proportionall numbers. Wherefore (by the 21. of this booke) A and B are like ſolide numbers.
