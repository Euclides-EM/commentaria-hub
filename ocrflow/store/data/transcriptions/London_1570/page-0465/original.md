[Margin]
A Corollary.
[/Margin]

Hereby it is manifeſt that the leaſt commõ meaſure to numbers howmanyſoeuer, meaſureth euery number which the ſayd numbers howmanyſoeuer meaſure.

## ¶ The 34. Theoreme. The 39. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If a number meaſure any number: the number meaſured ſhall haue a part after the denomination of the number meaſuring.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="foliate letter in a square frame"}Vppoſe that there be a number B, which let meaſure the number A. Then I ſay, that A hath a part taking his denomination of the number B. For how often B meaſureth A, ſo many vnities let there be in C. And let D be vnitie. And foraſmuch as B meaſureth A, by thoſe vnities which are in C, and vnitie D meaſureth C by thoſe vnities which are in C, therefore vnitie D, ſo many times meaſureth the number C, as B doth meaſure A. Wherefore alternately (by the 15. of the ſeuenth) vnitie D, ſo many times meaſureth B, as C doth meaſure A. Wherefore what part vnitie D is of the number B, the ſame part is C of A. But vnitie D is a part of B hauing his denomination of B. Wherefore C alſo is a part of A hauing his denomination of B. Wherefore A hath C as a part taking his denomination of B: which was required to be proued.

[Diagram: four horizontal dotted lines labelled A, B, C, and D]

The meaning of this Propoſition is, that if three meaſure any number, that number hath a third part, and if foure meaſure any number the ſayd number hath a fourth part. And ſo forth.

## ¶ The 35. Theoreme. The 40. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If a number haue any part: the number whereof the part taketh his denomination ſhall meaſure it.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
The conuerſe of the former propoſition. Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="interlaced letter in a patterned square frame"}Vppoſe that the number A haue a part, namely, B: and let the part B haue his denomination of the number C. Then I ſay, that C meaſureth A. Let D be vnitie. And foraſmuch as B is a part of A, hauing his denomination of C: and D being vnitie is alſo a part of the number C, hauing his denomination of C: therefore what part vnitie D is of the number C, the ſame part is alſo B of A: wherefore vnitie D ſo many times meaſureth the number C, as B meaſureth A. Wherefore alternately (by the 15. of the ſeuenth) vnitie D ſo many times meaſureth the number B, as C meaſureth A. Wherefore C meaſureth A: which was required to be proued.

[Diagram: four horizontal dotted lines labelled A, B, C, and D]

This Propoſition is the conuerſe of the former: and the meaning therof is, that euery number hauing a third part is meaſured of three, and hauing a fourth part is meaſured of foure. And ſo forth.
