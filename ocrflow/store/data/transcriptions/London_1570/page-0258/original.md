## ¶ The 19. Theoreme. The 21. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
In a circle the angles which conſiſt in one and the ſelfe ſame ſection or ſegment, are equall the one to the other.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:S|lines=3|style=decorated|decoration="foliate ornament"}Vppoſe that there be a circle ABCD, & in the ſegment therof BAED, let there conſiſt theſe angles BAD and BED. Then I ſay, that the angles BAD and BED are equall the one to the other.

[Margin]
Conſtruction.
[/Margin]

Take (by the firſt of the third) the centre of the circle ABCD, and let the ſame be the point F. And (by the firſt petition) draw theſe lines BF and FD.

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

Now for aſmuch as the angle BFD is ſet at the centre, and the angle BAD at the circumference, and they haue both one and yͤ ſame baſe, namely, the circumference BCD, therefore the angle BFD is (by the Propoſition going before) double to the angle BAD: and by the ſame reaſon the angle BFD is alſo double to the angle BED. Wherefore (by the 7. common ſentence) the angle BAD is equall to the angle BED: Wherefore in a circle the angles which conſiſte in one and the ſelfe ſame ſegment, are equall the one to the other: which was required to be proued.

[Diagram: circle with points A, B, C, D and E, centre F, intersecting chords, and lines BF and FD]

[Margin]
Three caſes in this Propoſition.
[/Margin]

In this propoſition are three caſes. For the angles conſiſting in one and the ſelf ſame ſegment, the ſegment may either be greater then a ſemicircle, or leſſe then a ſemicircle, or els iuſt a ſemicircle.

[Margin]
The firſt caſe.
[/Margin]

For the firſt caſe the demonſtration before put ſerueth.

[Margin]
The ſecond caſe.
[/Margin]

But now ſuppoſe that the angles BAD and BED do conſiſt in the ſectiõ BAD, which let be leſſe then a ſemicircle. Euen in this caſe alſo I ſay that the angles BAD and BED are equall. For draw a right line from A to E. And let the lines AD and BE cutte the one the other in the poynt G, wherefore the ſegment ACE is greater then a ſemicircle. And therfore by the firſt part of this propoſition the angles which are in it, namely, the angles ABE and EDA are equall the one to the other. And foraſmuch as in the triangle ABG the inward and oppoſite angles ABG and GAB are equall to the outwarde angle BGD, and by the ſame reaſon the two angles EDG and GED of the triangle DEG are equall to the ſelfe ſame outward angle BGD. Wherefore the two angles ABG and GAB are equall to the two angles EDG and GED.
