### Third Corollary.

[Subhead]
The ſide of a cube, is equall to the ſides of a Dodecahedron inſcribed in it, and circumſcribed about it.
[/Subhead]

For it was manifeſt by this propoſition, that the ſide of a cube maketh the leſſe ſegment, the ſide of a Dodecahedron inſcribed in it, namely, as in the firſt figure the line B S the ſide of the Dodecahedron inſcribed, is the leſſe ſegment of the line G F the ſide of the cube. And it was proued in the 17. of the thirtenth, that the ſame ſide of the cube ſubtendeth the angle of the pentagon of the Dodecahedron circumſcribed: and therefore it maketh the greater ſegment the ſide of the Dodecahedron or of the pentagon, by the firſt corollary of the ſame. Wherefore it is equal to bothe thoſe ſegments.

### The 14. Probleme.

### The 14. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
In a cube geuen, to inſcribe an Icoſahedron.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
Conſtruction.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="foliate"}uppoſe that the cube geuen be A B C, the Centres of whoſe baſes let be the points D, E, G, H, I, K: by whiche poyntes draw in the baſes vnto the other ſides parallels not touching the one the other. And deuide the lines drawn from the centres, as the line D T &c. by an extreme and meane proportion in the poyntes A, F: L, M: N, B: P, Q: R, S: C, O: by the 30. of the ſixth: and let the greater ſegmentes be about the centres. And draw theſe right lines, A L, A G, A M, and T G.

[Diagram]

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

And foraſmuch as the lines cut are parallels to the ſides of the cube: they ſhall make right angles the one with the other by the 29. of the firſt: and foraſmuche as they are equal: their ſections ſhall be equall, for that the ſections are alike by the 2. of the fourtenth. Wherfore the line T G is equal to the line D T, for they are eche, halfe ſides of the cube. Wherfore the ſquare of the whole line T G, and of the leſſe ſegment T A, is triple to the ſquare of the line A D the greater ſegment (by the 4. of the thirteth). But the line A G containeth in power the lines A T & T G, for the angle A T G is a right angle. Wherfore the ſquare of the line A G is triple to the ſquare of the line A D. And foraſmuch as the line M G L is erected perpendicularly to the plain paſſing by the lines A T, & which is parallel to the baſes of the cube (by the corollary of the 14. of the eleuenth) therefore the angle A G L is a right angle. But the line L G is equall to the line A D, for they are the greater ſegments of equall lines: Wherfore the line A G (which is in power triple to the line A D) is in power triple to the line L G. Wherfore adding vnto the ſame ſquare of the line A G, the ſquare of the line L G, the ſquare of the line A L, which (by the 47. of the firſt) containeth in power the two lines A G and G L, ſhalbe quadruple to the line A D or L G. Wherefore the line A L is double to the line A D (by the 20. of the ſixth:) and therfore is equal to the line A F, or to the line L M. And by the ſame reaſon may we proue that euery one of the other lines which couple the next ſections of the lines cut, as the lines A M, P F, P M, M Q, and the reſt are equal. Wherfore the triangles A L M, A P F, A M P, P M Q, and the reſt ſuch like, are equal, equiangle, and equilater, by the 4. and eight of the firſt. And foraſmuch as vpon euery one of the lines cut of the cube are ſet two triangles, as the triangles A L M, and B I M, there ſhalbe made 12. triangles. And foraſmuch as vnder euery one of the 8. angles of the cube, are ſubtended the other 8. triangles, as the triangle A M P. &c. of 12. and 8. triangles, ſhall be produced 20. triangles equal and equilater containing the ſolide of an Icoſahedron, by the 25. diffinition of the eleuenth, which ſhalbe inſcribed in the cube geuen A B C by the firſt diffinition of this booke. The inuention of the demonſtration of this dependeth of the ground of the former. Wherfore in a cube geuen, we haue deſcribed an Icoſahedron: which was required to be done.
