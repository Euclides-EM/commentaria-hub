## ❧ The 12. Theoreme. The 13. Proposition.

[Subhead]
A circle can not touch an other circle in moe poyntes then one, whether they touch within or without.
[/Subhead]

[Illustration: Euclid teaching students]

[Margin]
Of circles which touch the one the other inwardly.
[/Margin]

Or if it be poſſible, let the circle ABCD touch yͤ circle EBFD firſt inwardly in moe poyntes then one, that is, in D and B. Take (by the firſt of the third) the centre of the circle ABCD, and let the ſame be yͤ poynt G: and likewiſe yͤ centre of the circle EBFD, and let the ſame be yͤ poynt H. Wherefore (by the 11. of the ſame) a right line drawen from the poynt G, to the poynt H and produced, will fall vpon the poyntes B and D: let it ſo fall as the line BGHD doth. And for aſmuch as the poynt G is the centre of the circle ABCD: therefore (by the 15. definition of the firſt) the line BG is equall to the line DG. Wherefore the line BG is greater then then the line HD: Wherefore the line BH is much greater then the line HD. Agayne for aſmuch as the poynt H is the centre of the circle EBFD, therefore (by the ſame definition) the line BH is equall to the line HD: and it is proued that it is much greater then it: which is impoſſible. A circle therefore can not touch a circle inwardly in moe poyntes then one.

¶ Now ſay that neither outwardly alſo, a circle toucheth a circle in moe poyntes then one. For if it be poſſible, let the circle ACK touch yͤ circle ABCD outwardly in moe poyntes then one, that is, in A and C: And (by the firſt petition) draw a line from the poynt A to the poynt C. Now for aſmuch as in yͤ circumference of either of the circles ABCD and ACK, are taken two poyntes at all aduentures, namely, A and C; therefore (by the ſecond of the third) a right line ioyning together thoſe poyntes ſhall fall within both the circles. But it falleth within the circle ABCD, & without the circle ACK: which is abſurde. Wherefore a circle ſhall not touch a circle outwardly in moe poyntes then one, and it is proued yͭ neither alſo inwardly.
