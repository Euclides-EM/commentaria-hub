## ¶ The third Propoſition.

[Subhead]
*That number which is produced of the multiplication of numbers how many ſoeuer into other numbers how many ſoeuer, is equall to that number which is produced of the multiplication of the number compoſed of thoſe firſt numbers, into the number compoſed of theſe latter numbers.*
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

As if the numbers A, B, C doo multiply the numbers D, E, F, ech one eche other, and if the numbers produced be added together. Then I ſay that the number compoſed of the numbers produced is equall to the number produced of the number compoſed of the numbers A, B, C into the number compoſed of the numbers D, E, F.

[Diagram: two horizontal dotted rows labelled A, B, C and D, E, F.]

For by the former propoſitiõ that which is produced of the number compoſed of A, B, C into D is equall to that which is produced of euery one of the ſayd numbers into D: and by the ſame reaſon that which is produced of the number compoſed of A, B, C into E, is equall to that which is produced of euery one of the ſayd numbers into E: and ſo likewiſe that which is produced of the number compoſed of A, B, C into F is equall to that which is produced of euery one of the ſayd numbers into F. But by the firſt of theſe propoſitions that which is produced of the number compoſed of theſe numbers A, B, C into euery one of theſe numbers D, E, F is equall to that which is produced of the number compoſed into the number compoſed: wherefore that is manifeſt which was required to be proued.

## ¶ The fourth Propoſition.

[Subhead]
*If a number be deuided into partes how many ſoeuer: that number which is produced of the whole into himſelfe, is equall to that number which is produced of the ſame number into all his partes.*
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
This anſwereth to the 2. of the ſecond.
[/Margin]

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

This proueth in numbers that which the ſecond of the ſecond proued in lines. As if the number A, be deuided into the numbers B and C, and D. Then I ſay, that that which is produced of A into himſelfe, is equall to that which is produced of A into all the ſayd numbers B, C, and D. For putting the number E equall to the number A, it is manifeſt by the firſt of theſe propoſitions that that which is produced of E into A, is equall to that which is produced of E into all the partes of A.

[Diagram: A above a horizontal dotted row labelled B, C, D, with E below C.]

But by the common ſentence that which is produced of E into A is equall to that which is produced of A into himſelfe: and that which is produced of E into the partes of A is equall to that which is produced of A into the ſelfe ſame partes. Wherefore that is manifeſt which was required to be proued.

## ¶ The fift Propoſition.

[Subhead]
*If a number be deuided into two partes: that which is produced of the whole into one of the partes, is equall to that which is produced of the ſelfe ſame part into him ſelfe, and into the other part.*
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
This anſwereth to the 3. of the third.
[/Margin]

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

This proueth in numbers that which in the 3. of the ſecond was proued in lines. For let the number A be deuided into the numbers B and C. Then I ſay that that which is produced of A into C, is equall to that which is produced of C into himſelfe and into B. For by the 16. of the ſeuenth, that which is produced of A into C is equall to that which is produced of C into A. Now then put the number D equall to the number C. Wherefore that which is produced of A into C is equall to that which is produced of D into A.

[Diagram: A above a dotted line, B and C on a second dotted row, and D below A.]

But by the firſt of theſe propoſitions that which is produced of D into A is equall to that which is produced of D into B and of D into C. Wherefore foraſmuch as that which is produced of D into A and into B and into C is equall to that which is produced of C into A, and into B, and into himſelfe, by reaſon of the equalitie of C and D: that is manifeſt which was required to be proued.
