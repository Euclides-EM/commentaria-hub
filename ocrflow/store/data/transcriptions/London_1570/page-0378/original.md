[Margin]
The firſt part of this Theoreme.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=3|style=decorated}Vppoſe that there be a triangle ABC, vnto one of the ſides whereof, namely, vnto BC, let there be drawen a parallel line DE cutting the ſides AC and AB in the pointes E and D. Then I ſay firſt that as BD is to DA, ſo is CE to EA. Draw a line from B to E, & alſo from C to D. Wherfore (by the 37. of the firſt) the triangle BDE is equall vnto the triangle CDE: for they are ſet vpon one and the ſame baſe DE, and are contained within the ſelfe ſame parallels DE and BC. Conſider alſo a certaine other triangle ADE. Now thinges equall (by the 7. of the fifth) haue to one ſelfe thing one and the ſame proportion. Wherfore as yͤ triangle BDE is to yͤ triangle ADE, ſo is yͤ triangle CDE to the triangle ADE. But as yͤ triangle BDE is to yͤ triangle ADE, ſo is yͤ baſe BD to yͤ baſe DA (by the firſt of this booke:) For they are vnder one and the ſelfe ſame toppe, namely, E, and therfore are vnder one and the ſame altitude. And by the ſame reaſon as the triangle CDE is to the triangle ADE, ſo is the lyne CE to the lyne EA. Wherfore (by the 11. of the fifth) as the line BD is to the line DA, ſo is the line CE to the line EA.

[Margin]
Demonſtration of the ſecond part.
[/Margin]

But now ſuppoſe that in yͤ triangle ABC the ſides AB & AC be cut proportionally, ſo yͤ as BD is to DA, ſo let CE be to EA, & draw a line from D to E. Then ſecondly I ſay yͤ the line DE is a parallel to yͤ lyne BC. For the ſame order of conſtruction being kept, for yͤ as BD is to DA, ſo is CE to EA, but as BD is to DA, ſo is yͤ triangle BDE to yͤ triangle ADE (by the 1. of the ſixt) & as CE is to EA, ſo (by yͤ ſame) is the triangle CDE to yͤ triangle ADE: therfore (by the 11. of the fifth) as the triangle BDE is to the triangle ADE, ſo is the triangle CDE to the triangle ADE. Wherfore either of theſe triangles BDE and CDE haue to the triangle ADE one and the ſame proportion. Wherfore (by the 9. of the fifth) the triangle BDE is equall vnto the triangle CDE, and they are vpon one and the ſelfe baſe, namely, DE. But triangles equall and ſet vpon one baſe, are alſo contained within the ſame parallel lines (by the 39. of the firſt.) Wherfore the line DE is vnto the line BC a parallel. If therfore to any one of the ſides of a triangle be drawen a parallel line, it cutteth the other ſides of the ſame triangle proportionally. And if the ſides of a triangle be cut proportionally, a right lyne drawen from ſection to ſection, is parallel to the other ſide of the triangle: which thing was required to be demonſtrated.
