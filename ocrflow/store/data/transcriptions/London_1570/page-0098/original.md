## The 4. Theoreme. The 7. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If from the endes of one line, be drawen two right lynes to any pointe: there can not frõ the ſelf ſame endes on the ſame ſide, be drawen two other lines equal to the two firſt lines, the one to the other, vnto any other point.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:F|lines=6|style=decorated|decoration="foliate and floral design"}Or if it be poſſible: then from the endes of one & the ſelf ſame right line, namely, AB, from the pointes (I ſay) A and B, let there be drawen two right lines AC and CB to the point C: and from the ſame endes of the line AB, let there be drawen two other right right lines AD and DB equall to the lines AC and CB the one to the other, is, eche to his correſpondent line, and on one and the ſame ſide, and to an other pointe, namely, to D: ſo that let CA be equall to DA beyng both drawen from one end, that is, A: & let CB be equall to DB, beyng both alſo drawen from one ende, that is, B. And (by the firſt petition) draw a right line from the point C, to the point D. Now foraſmuch as AC is equall to AD, the angle ACD alſo is (by the 5. propoſition) equall to the angle ADC: wherfore the angle ACD is leſſe thẽ the angle BDC. VVherfore the angle BCD is much leſſe then the angle BDC. Againe foraſmuch as BC is equall to BD, and therfore alſo the angle BCD is equall to the angle BDC. And it is proued that it is much leſſe then it: which is impoſſible. If therfore from the endes of one line, be drawen two right lines to any pointe: there can not from the ſelfe ſame endes on the ſame ſide, be drawen two other lines equall to the two firſt lines, the one to the other, vnto any other point: VVhich was required to be demonſtrated.
