## ¶ The 14. Theoreme. The 14. Proposition.

If there be geuen the leaſt number, whom certayne prime numbers geuen, do meaſure: no other prime number ſhall meaſure that nũber, beſides thoſe prime numbers geuen.

And theſe prime numbers B, C, D, meaſure A. But if two numbers multiplying the one the other, produce any number. And if ſome prime number meaſure that which is produced, it ſhall alſo meaſure one of thoſe numbers which were put at the beginning (by the 32. of the ſeuenth) Wherefore thoſe numbers B,C,D, meaſure one of theſe numbers E or F. But they meaſure not E, for E is a prime number, and is not one and the ſame with any one of theſe numbers B,C,D. Wherefore they meaſure F being leſſe then A which is impoſſible. For A is ſuppoſed to be the leaſt whom B,C,D, meaſure. Wherefore no prime number beſides B,C,D, meaſureth A: which was required to be demonſtrated.
