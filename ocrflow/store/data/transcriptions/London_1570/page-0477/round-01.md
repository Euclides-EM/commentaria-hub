The firſt part of this propoſition demonſtrated.

And foraſmuch as A is a ſquare number, & the ſide thereof is C, therefore C multiplying himſelfe produced A. And by the ſame reaſon D multiplying himſelfe produced B. Now foraſmuch as C multiplying C produced A, and multiplying D produced E, therefore (by the 17. of the ſeuenth) as C is to D, ſo is A to E. Againe foraſmuch as C multiplying D produced E, and D multiplying himſelfe produced B, therefore theſe two numbers C and D multiplying one number, namely, D, produce E and B. Wherfore (by the 18. of the ſeuenth) as C is to D, ſo is E to B. But as C is to D, ſo is A to E. Wherefore as A is to E, ſo is E to B. Wherefore betwene theſe ſquare numbers A and B, there is one meane proportionall number, namely, E.

[Margin]
The ſecond part demonſtrated.
[/Margin]

Now alſo I ſay that A is vnto B in double proportion of that which C is to D. For foraſmuch as there are three numbers in continuall proportion, A, E, B, therefore (by the 10. definition of the fift) A is vnto B in double proportion of that which A is to E. But as A is to E, ſo is C to D. Wherefore A is vnto B in double proportion of that which the ſide C is vnto the ſide D: which was required to be proued.

## ¶ The 10. Theoreme.  The 12. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
Betwene two cube numbers there are two meane proportionall numbers. And the one cube is to the other cube in treble proportion of that which the ſide of the one is to the ſide of the other.
[/Subhead]

Vppoſe that there be two cube numbers A and B, and let the ſide of A be C, and let the ſide of B be D. Then I ſay that betwene thoſe cube numbers A and B, there are two meane proportionall numbers, and that A is vnto B in treble proportion of that which C is to D. Let C multiplying himſelfe produce E, and multiplying D let it produce F, and let D multiplying himſelfe produce G. And let C multiplying F produce H, and let D multiplying F produce K.
