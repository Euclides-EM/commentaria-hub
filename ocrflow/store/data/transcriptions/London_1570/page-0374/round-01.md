betwene them. Suppoſe three quantities A, B, C, ſo that let A haue to B ſeſquialtera proportion, namely, 6. to 4. And let B to C haue ſeſquitertia proportion, namely, 4. to 3.

[Diagram: three horizontal lines labelled A, B, and C, with proportional division marks]

Now the proportion of A to C, the firſt to the thyrd, is made of the proportion of A to B, and of the proportion of B to C added together: If ye will adde them together, ye muſt by this definition multiply the quantitie or denominator of the one, by the quantitie or denominator of the other. Ye muſt firſt therefore ſeeke the denominators of theſe proportions, by the rule before geuen in the declaration of the definitions of the fift Booke. As if ye deuide A by B, namely, 6. by 4, ſo ſhall ye haue in the quotient 1 1/2 for the denominator of the proportion of A to B: likewiſe if ye deuide B by C, namely, 4. by 3. ye ſhall haue in the quotient 1 1/3 for the denominator of the proportion of B to C: now multiply theſe two denominators 1 1/2 and 1 1/3 the one into the other, by the rule before taught, namely, by multiplying the numerator of the one into the numerator of the other, and alſo the denominator of the one into the denominator of the other: the numerator of 1 1/2 or of 3/2 which is all one, is 3, the denominator is 2: the numerator of 1 1/3, which reduced are 4/3 is 4, the denominator is 3: then multiply 3. by 4, numerator by numerator, ſo haue ye 12. for a new numerator: likewiſe multiply 2. by 3. denominator by denominator, ye ſhall produce 6. for a new denominator:

[Calculation]
    12
3   4
—   —
2   3
    6
[/Calculation]

ſo haue you produced 12. and 6, betwene which there is dupla proportion. Which proportion is alſo betwene A and C, namely, 6. to 3, the firſt quantitie to the third. Wherfore the proportion of A to C is ſayd to be made of the proportion of A to B and of the proportion of B to C, for that it is produced of the multiplication of the quantitie or denominator of the one, into the quantitie or denominator of the other. And ſo of all others be they neuer ſo many.

[Margin]
Example in numbers.
2. 3. 15. 18.
[/Margin]

As in theſe examples in numbers here ſet 2. 3. 15. 18. In this example the leſſe numbers are compared to the greater, as in the former the greater were compared to the leſſe: the denominator of the proportion of 2. to 3. is 2/3 that is, ſubſeſquialtera, the denomination betwene 3. and 15. is 3/15 or 1/5 which is all one, that is, ſubquintupla, betwene 15. and 18. the denomination of the proportion is 5/6. That is, ſubſeſquiquinta: multiply all theſe denominations together: firſt the numerators: 2. into 1. produce 2, then 2. into 5. produce 10: which ſhall be a new numerator. Then the denominators, 3. into 5. produce 15: and 15. into 6. produce 90: which ſhall be a new denominator.

[Calculation]
10     2   1   5 │ 1
—      —   —   — │ —
90     3   5   6 │ 9
[/Calculation]

So haue you brought forth 10/90 or 1/9 which is proportion ſubnoncupla: which is alſo the proportion of 2. to 18. Wherefore the proportion of 2. to 18. that is, of the extremes, namely, ſubnoncupla, is made of the proportions of 2. to 3: of 3. to 15: and of 15. to 18: namely, of ſubſeſquialtera, ſubquintupla, and ſubſeſquiquinta.

An other example.
[/Margin]

An other example, where the greater inequalitie and the leſſe inequalitie are mixed together. 6. 4. 2. 3. the denomination of the proportion of 6. to 4, is 1 1/2, of 4. to 2, is 2/1, and of 2. to 3, is 2/3: now if ye multiply as you ought, all theſe denominations together, ye ſhall produce 12. to 6, namely, dupla proportion.
