## An other addition of Pelitarius.

[Margin]
An other aditiõ of Pelitarius.
[/Margin]

[Subhead]
The diameter of a ſquare being geuen, to geue the ſquare thereof.
[/Subhead]

This is eaſie to be done. For if vpon the two endes of the line be drawen two halfe right angles, and ſo be made perfect the triangle then ſhalbe deſcribed half of the ſquare: the other halfe whereof alſo is after the ſame manner eaſie to be deſcribed.

[Margin]
A Corrollary.
[/Margin]

Hereby it is manifeſt, that the ſquare of the diameter is double to that ſquare whoſe diameter it is.

## The 34. Theoreme. The 48. Propoſition.

If the ſquare which is made of one of the ſides of a triangle, be equall to the ſquares which are made of the two other ſides of the ſame triangle: the angle comprehended vnder thoſe two other ſides is a right angle.

{dropcap:S|lines=3|style=decorated}Vppoſe that A B C be a triangle, and let the ſquare which is made of one of the ſides there{printer-error-correction:thereof}, namely, of the ſide B C, be equall to the ſquares which are made of the ſides B A and A C. Then I ſay that the angle B A C is a right angle. Rayſe vp (by the 11. propoſitiõ) from the point A vnto the right line A C a perpendicular line A D. And (by the thirde propoſition) vnto the line A B put an equall line A D. And by the firſt peticion draw a right line from the point D to the point C. And foraſmuch as the line D A is equall to the line A B, the ſquare which is made of the line D A is equall to the ſquare whiche is made of the line A B Put the ſquare of the line A C, common to them both. VVherefore the ſquares of the lines D A and A C are equal to the ſquares of the lines B A and A C. But (by the propoſition going before) the ſquare of the line D C is equal to ŷ ſquares of the lines. A D and A C. (For the angle D A C is a right angle) and the ſquare of B C is (by ſuppoſition) equall to the ſquares of A B and A C. VVherefore the ſquare of D C is equall to the ſquare of B C: wherefore the ſide D C is equall to the ſide B C. And foraſmuch as A B is equall to A D and A C is common to them both, therefore theſe two ſides D A and A C are equall to theſe two ſides B A and A C, the one to the other, and the baſe D C is equall to the baſe B C: wherfore (by the 8. propoſition) the angle D A C is equall to the angle B A C. But the angle D A C is a right angle, wherefore alſo the angle B A C is a right angle. If
