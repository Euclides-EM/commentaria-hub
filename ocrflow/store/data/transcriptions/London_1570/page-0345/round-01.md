The ſecond caſe of the firſt part.

But now ſuppoſe that AE be greater then EB. Now then EB beyng the leſſe, and being oftentimes multiplied, will at the length be greater then D. Let it be ſo multiplied, and let the multiplex of EB, namely, GH, be greater then D. And how multiplex GH is to EB ſo multiplex let FG be to AE and K to C. And by the former reaſon may we proue, that FH and K are equemultiplices to AB and C. Likewiſe alſo let N be multiplex of D, & alſo let it be ẏ firſt multiplex which is greater then FG: wherefore agayn FG is not leſſe then M. But GH is greater then D: wherefore the whole FH, exceedeth D & M, that is, N: but K exceedeth not N. For FG which is greater then GH, that is, then K exceedeth not N. And ſo followyng the ſame order we did before, we ſhall performe the demonſtration. Wherefore vnequall magnitudes beyng taken, the greater hath to one and the ſame magnitude a greater proportion then hath the leſſe: and that one and the ſame magnitude hath to the leſſe a greater proportion then it hath to the greater: which was required to be proued.

[Margin]
Firſt difference of the firſt parte.
[/Margin]

Suppoſe that there be two vnequall magnitudes, of which let AB be the greater, and C the leſſe: and let there be a certaine other magnitude, namely, D. Then I ſay firſt, that AB hath to D a greater proportion then hath C to D. For foraſmuch as by ſuppoſition AB is greater then the magnitude C: therefore the magnitude AB contayneth the ſame magnitude C, and an other magnitude beſides. Let EB be equall vnto C: and let AE be the part remayning of the ſame magnitude. Now AE and EB are eyther vnequall or equall the one to the other. Firſt let them be vnequall: and let AE be leſſe then EB. And vnto AE the leſſe take any multiplex whatſoeuer, ſo that it be greater then the magnitude D: and let the ſame be FG.
