### ¶ An other demonſtration for the firſt part after Campane.

[Margin]
An other demonſtration of the firſt part after Campane.
[/Margin]

Suppoſe that there be a circle A B D C, whoſe centre let be the poynt E. And draw in it two equall lines AB and CD. Then I ſay that they are equally diſtant from the centre. Draw from the centre vnto the lines AB and CD, theſe perpendicular lines EF and EG. And (by the 2. part of the 3. of this booke) the line AB ſhall be equally deuided in the poynt F, and the line CD ſhall be equally deuided in the poynt G. And draw theſe right lines EA, EB, EC, and ED. And for aſmuch as in the triangle AEB the two ſides AB and AE are equall to the two ſides CD and CE of the triangle CED, & the baſe EB is equall to the baſe ED, therefore (by the 8. of the firſt) the angle at the point A ſhall be equall to the angle at the point C. And for aſmuch as in the triangle AEF the two ſides AE and AF are equall to the two ſides CE and CG of the triangle CEG, and the angle EAF is equall to the angle CEG, therefore (by the 4. of the firſt) the baſe EF is equall to the baſe EG: which for aſmuch as they are perpendicular lines, therefore the lines AB & CD are equally diſtant frõ the centre, by the 4. definition of this booke.
