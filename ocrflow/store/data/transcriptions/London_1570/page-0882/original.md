### Note.

[Margin]
Difference betwene the firſt probleme and the ſecond.
[/Margin]

The difference betwene this Probleme, and that next before, is this: there, although we had two circles geuen, and two lines were found in that proportion the one to the other, in which the geuen circles were: and here likewiſe are two circles geuẽ, and two lines alſo are had in the ſame proportiõ, that the geuẽ circles are: yet there we tooke at pleaſure the firſt of the two lines, whereunto we framed the ſecond proportionally, to the circles geuen. But here the firſt of the two lines, is aſſigned, poynted, and determined to vs: and not our choyſe to be had therein, as was in the former Probleme.

### A Probleme. 3.

[Subhead]
A circle being geuen, to finde an other circle, to which the geuen circle is in any proportion geuẽ in two right lines.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
Conſtruction.
[/Margin]

Suppoſe the circle A B C geuen, and therefore his ſemidiameter is geuen: whereby his diameter alſo is geuen: which diameter let be A C. Let the proportion geuen, be that which is betwene E, F, two right lines. I ſay, a circle is to be found, vnto which A B C hath that proportion that E hath to F. As E is to F, ſo let A C the diameter, be to an other right line, by the 12. of the ſixth. Which line ſuppoſe to be H. Betwene A C and H, finde a middle proportionall line, by the 13. of the ſixth: which let be L N. By the 10. of the firſt, deuide L N, into two equall partes: and let that be done in the point O. Now vpon O L, (O being made the center) deſcribe a circle: which let be L M N. I ſay that A B C, is to L M N, as E is to F.

[Diagram: a circle labelled A, B, and C with horizontal diameter A C; horizontal lines E and F; a circle labelled L, M, N, and O with horizontal diameter L N; and a horizontal line H]

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

For ſeing that A C, L N, and H, are three right lines in continuall proportion (by conſtruction) therefore (by the Corollary of the 20. of the ſixth) as A C is to H, ſo is the ſquare of A C to the ſquare of L N. But A C is to H, as E is to F, by conſtruction. Wherefore the ſquare of A C is to the ſquare of L N, as E is to F: but as the ſquare of the diameter A C, is to the ſquare of the diameter L N, ſo is the circle A B C to the circle L M N, by this 2. of the twelueth, wherefore by the 11. of the fiueth, the circle A B C is to the circle L M N, as E is to F. A circle being geuen (therefore) an other circle is founde, to which the geuen circle is in any proportion geuen betwene two right lines: which ought to be done.

### A Probleme. 4.

[Subhead]
Two circles being geuen, to finde one circle equall to them both.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
Conſtruction.
[/Margin]

Suppoſe the two circles geuẽ, haue their diameters A B & C D. I ſay that a circle muſt be found equall to the two circles whoſe diameters are A B and C D: vnto the line A B, at the point A, erect a perpendicular line A E: from which (ſufficiently produced) cut a line equall to C D, which let be A F. By the firſt petition draw from F to B a right line: ſo is F A B made a triangle rectangle. I ſay now that a circle whoſe diameter is F B, is equall to the two circles whoſe diameters are A B and C D.

[Diagram: a right triangle A F B with F on the perpendicular line A E, and a horizontal line C D]

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

For by the 47. of the firſt, the ſquare of F B is equall to the ſquares of A B & A F. Which A F is (by conſtruction) equall to C D. Wherefore the ſquare of F B is equall to the ſquares of A B and C D. But circles are one to an other, as the ſquares of their diameters are one to the other, by this ſecond of the twelueth. Therefore the circle whoſe diameter is F B is equall to the circles whoſe diameters are A B and C D. Therefore two circles being geuen we haue found a circle equall to them both. Which was required to be done.
