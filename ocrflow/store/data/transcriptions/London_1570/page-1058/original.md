## ¶ The 6. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
The ſide of a Cube containeth in power halfe the ſide of an equilater triangular Pyramis inſcribed in the ſaid Cube.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:F|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="foliate design"}Oraſmuch as the ſide of the pyramis inſcribed in the cube ſubtendeth two ſides of the cube which containe a right angle, by the 1. of the fiuetenth: it is manifeſt, by the 47. of the firſt, that the ſide of the pyramis ſubtending the ſaid ſides, is in power duple to the ſide of the cube: Wherefore alſo the ſquare of the ſide of the cube is the halfe of the ſquare of the ſide of the pyramis. The ſide therefore of a cube containeth in power halfe the ſide of an equilater triangular pyramis inſcribed in the ſaid cube.

## ¶ The 7. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
The ſide of a Pyramis is duple to the ſide of an Octohedron inſcribed in it.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:F|lines=5|style=decorated|decoration="foliate design"}Oraſmuch as by the 2. of the fiuetenth it was proued, that the ſide of the Octohedron inſcribed in a pyramis coupleth the midle ſections of the ſides of the pyramis. Wherefore the ſides of the pyramis and of the Octohedron are parallels, by the Corollary of the 39. of the firſt: and therefore, by the Corollary of the 2. of the ſixth, they ſubtend like triangles. Wherefore (by the 4. of the ſixth) the ſide of the pyramis is double to the ſide of the Octohedron, namely, in the proportion of the ſides. The ſide therefore of a pyramis is duple to the ſide of an Octohedron inſcribed in it.

## ¶ The 8. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
The ſide of a Cube is in power duple to the ſide of an Octohedron inſcribed in it.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:T|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="foliate design"}was proued in the 3. of the fiuetenth, that the diameter of the Octohedron inſcribed in the cube, coupleth the centres of the oppoſite baſes of the cube. Wherefore the ſaid diameter is equall to the ſide of the cube. But the ſame is alſo the diameter of the ſquare made of the ſides of the Octohedron, namely, is the diameter of the Sphere which containeth it, by the 14. of the thirtenth. Wherefore that diameter being equall to the ſide of the cube, is in power double to the ſide of that ſquare, or to the ſide of the Octohedron inſcribed in it, by the 47. of the firſt. The ſide therefore of a Cube, is in power duple to the ſide of an Octohedron inſcribed in it: which was required to be proued.

## ¶ The 9. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
The ſide of a Dodecahedron, is the greater ſegment of the line which containeth in power halfe the ſide of the Pyramis inſcribed in the ſayd Dodecahedron.
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
Conſtruction.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="interlaced design"}Vppoſe that of the Dodecahedron A B G D the ſide be A B: and let the baſe of the cube inſcribed in the Dodecahedron be E C F H, by the 8. of the fiuetenth. And let the ſide of the pyramis inſcribed in the cube be C H, by the 1. of the fiuetenth. Wherefore the ſame pyramis is inſcribed in the Dodecahedron, by the 10. of the fiuetenth. Then I ſay, that A B the ſide of the Dodecahedron is the greater ſegment of the line which containeth in power halfe the line C H, which is the ſide of the pyramis inſcribed in the Dodecahedron.
