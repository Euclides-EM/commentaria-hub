Now alſo I ſay that A is vnto B in double proportion of that which ſide of like proportion is to ſide of like proportion, that is, of that which C is vnto E, or of that which D is vnto F: For foraſmuch as A, G, B, are in continuall proportion, therefore (by the 10. definition of the fift) A is vnto B in double proportion of that which A is vnto G: But as A is to G, ſo is C to E, and D to F: wherefore A is vnto B in double proportion of that which C is to E, or D to F: which was required to be demonſtrated.

## ¶ The 17. Theoreme.  Th 19. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
Betwene two like ſolide numbers, there are two meane proportionall numbers. And the one like ſolide number, is to the other like ſolide number in treble proportion of that which ſide of like proportion is to ſide of lyke proportion.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="ornamental design"}uppoſe that there be two like ſolide numbers A and B. And let the ſides of the number A, be the numbers C, D, E. And let the ſides of the number B, be the numbers F, G, H. And foraſmuch as (by the 22. definition of the ſeuenth) lyke ſolide numbers haue their ſides proportionall, therfore as C is to D, ſo is F to G, and as D is to E, ſo is G to H. Then I ſay that betwene A and B, there are two meane proportionall numbers. And that A is vnto B in treble proportion of that which C is to F, or of that which D is to G, or moreouer of that which E is vnto H.

For let C multiplying D produce K. And let F multiplying G, produce L. And foraſmuch as C, D, are in the ſelf ſame proportion that F, G, are, & of C & D is produced K, and of F and G is produced L, therefore K and L are like plaine numbers. And therefore betwene thoſe numbers K and L, there is one meane proportionall number (by the 18. of the ſeuenth) Let the ſame be M. Wherefore M is produced of D and F, as it is manifeſt by the propoſition goyng before. Wherefore as K is to M, ſo is M to L. And foraſmuch as D multiplying C produced K, and multiplying F produced M: therefore (by the 17. of the ſeuenth) as C is to F, ſo is K to M, but as K is to M, ſo is M to L. Wherefore theſe numbers K, M, L, are in continuall proportion, and in the ſame proportion that C is to D. And for that as C is to D, ſo is F to G, therefore alternately (by the 13. of the ſeuenth) as C is to F, ſo is D to G. Againe, for that as D is to E, ſo is G to H, therefore alternately alſo as D is to G, ſo is E to H. Wherefore theſe numbers K, M, L, are in continuall proportion, and in the ſame proportion that C is to F, and that D is to G, and moreouer that E is to H.
