But now ſuppoſe that the numbers A and B C be not prime the one to the other. Then B C either meaſureth A or not meaſureth it. If B C meaſure A, then is B C a part of A. But if not, take (by the 2. of the ſeuenth) the greateſt common meaſure of A and B C, and let the ſame be D. And let B C be deuided into as many partes as it hath equall vnto D, that is, into B E, E F, and F C.

[Diagram: horizontal dotted lines labelled A; B, C; B, C; B, E, F, C; and D]

And foraſmuch as D meaſureth A, therefore D is a part of A. But D is equall vnto euery one of theſe partes B E, E F, and F C. Wherefore alſo euery one of theſe partes B E, E F, and F C, is a part of A. Wherefore the number B C is partes of A. Wherefore euery leſſe number is of euery greater nũber either a part or partes: which was required to be proued.

## ¶ The 3. Theoreme. The 5. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If a number be a part of a number, and an other nũber the ſelfe ſame part of an other number, then both the numbers added together ſhall be the ſelfe ſame part of both the numbers added together, which one number was of one number.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="ornamental foliage"}uppoſe the number A to be a part of the number B C, and let an other number namely, D, be the ſelfe ſame part of an other number, namely, of E F. Then I ſay, that the numbers A and D added together, are the ſelfe ſame part of the nũbers B C and E F added together, that A is of B C.

[Margin]
This propoſition, and the 6. propoſition in diſcrete quantitie, anſwer to the firſt of the fifth in continuall quantitie.
[/Margin]

For foraſmuch as what part the number A is of the number B C, the ſelfe ſame part is the number D of the number E F, therefore how many numbers there are in B C equall vnto A, ſo many numbers are there in E F equall vnto D. Deuide I ſay, B C into the numbers that are equall vnto A, that is, into B G, and G C, and likewiſe deuide E F into the numbers that are equall vnto D, that is, into E H and H F. Now then the multitude of theſe B G and G C, is equall vnto the multitude of theſe E H and H F.

[Diagram: horizontal dotted lines labelled A; B, G, C; D; and E, H, F]

[Margin]

And foraſmuch as B G is equall vnto A, and E H vnto D, therefore B G and E H are equall vnto A & D. And by the ſame reaſon, foraſmuch as G C is equall vnto A, and H F vnto D: therefore G C and H F are alſo equall vnto A and D. Wherefore how many numbers there are in B C equall vnto A, ſo many are there in B C and E F equall vnto A and D. Wherefore how multiplex B C is to A, ſo multiplex are both the numbers B C and E F, to both the numbers A and D. Wherefore what part A is of B C, the ſelfe part alſo are A & D added together, of B C and E F added together: which was required to be proued.

## ¶ The 4. Theoreme. The 6. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If a number be partes of a number, and an other number the ſelfe ſame partes of an other number: then both numbers added together ſhall be of both numbers added together the ſelfe ſame partes, that one number was of one number.
[/Subhead]
