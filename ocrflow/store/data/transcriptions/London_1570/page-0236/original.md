## The 5. Theoreme. The 6. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If two circles touch the one the other, they haue not one and the ſame centre.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:S|lines=3|style=decorated|decoration="foliate pattern"}Vppoſe that theſe two circles A B C, & C D E do touch the one the other in the poynt C. Then I ſay that they haue not one and the ſame centre. For if it be poſſible let the point F be centre vnto them both. And (by the firſt petition) draw a line from F to C: and drawe the line F E B at all aduentures.

[Margin]
Demonſtration leading to an impoſſibilitie.
[/Margin]

And for aſmuch as the poynt F is the centre of the circle A B C, therfore (by the 15. definition of the firſt) the line F C is equall vnto the line F B. Agayne for aſmuch as the poynt F is the centre of yᵉ circle C D E, therefore (by the ſame definition) the line F C is equall vnto the line F E. And it is proued, that the line F C is equall vnto the line F B, wherefore the line F E alſo is equall vnto the line F B, namely the leſſe vnto yᵉ greater: which is impoſſible.

[Diagram: two circles touching internally at C, with centres at F and lines C F and F E B; circumferences labelled A B C and C D E]

VVherefore the poynt F is not the centre of both the circles A B C and C D E. In like ſort alſo may we proue that no other poynt is the centre of both the ſayd circles. If therefore two circles touch the one the other: they haue not one and the ſame centre: which was required to be demonſtrated.

In thys Propoſition are two caſes: for the circles touchyng the one the other, may touch eyther within or without. If they touch the one the other within, then is it by the former demonſtration manifeſt, that they haue not both one and the ſelfe ſame centre. It is alſo manifeſt if they touch the one the other without: for that euery centre is in the middeſt of hys circle.

[Margin]
Two caſes in thys Propoſition.
[/Margin]
