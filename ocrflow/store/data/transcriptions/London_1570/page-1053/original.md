{dropcap:I|lines=5|style=decorated|decoration="floral design"}N the 13. of the thirtenth and the other 4. propoſitions following, it was declared that the 5. regular ſolides, are ſo contayned in a ſphere, that right lines drawne from the centre of the ſphere or of the ſolide inſcribed, to euery one of the angles of the ſolide inſcribed, are equall. Which right lines therefore make pyramids, whoſe toppes are the centre of the ſphere, or of the ſolide, and the baſes are euery one of the baſes of thoſe ſolides. And foraſmuch as thoſe baſes are in euery ſolide equall and like the one to the other, and deſcribed in equall circles: thoſe circles ſhall cutte the ſphere: for the angles which touch the circumference of the circle, touch alſo the ſuperficies of the ſphere. Wherefore perpendiculars drawne from the centre of the ſphere to the baſes, or to the playne ſuperficies of the equall circles, are equall, by the corollary of the aſſumpt of the 16. of the twelfth. Wherefore making the centre the centre of the ſphere which contayneth the ſolide, and the ſpace ſome one of the equall perpendiculars, deſcribe a ſphere, and it ſhall touch euery one of the baſes of that ſolide: neither ſhall the ſuperficies of the ſphere paſſe beyond thoſe baſes: when as thoſe perpendiculars are the leſt lines which are drawne from the centre to the baſes, by the 3. corollary of the ſame aſſumpt. Wherefore we haue in euery one of the regular bodies inſcribed a ſphere: which regular bodies are in number onely fiue, by the corollary of the 18. of the thirtenth.

[Margin]
This propoſition Campane hath, & is the laſt alſo in order of the 15. booke with him.
[/Margin]

### A Corollary.

The regular figures inſcribed in ſpheres, and alſo the ſpheres circumſcribed about them, or contayning them, haue one and the ſelfe ſame centre. Namely, their pyramids, the angles of whoſe baſes touch the ſuperficies of the ſphere, doo from thoſe angles cauſe equall right lines to be drawne to one and the ſelfe ſame poynt, making the toppes of the pyramids in the ſame poynt: and therefore they make the centres of the ſpheres in the ſelfe ſame toppes: when as the right lines drawne from thoſe angles to the crooked ſuperficies, wherein are ſet the angles of the baſes of the pyramids, are equall.

### An aduertiſment of Fluſſas

Of theſe ſolides, onely the Octohedron receaueth the other ſolides inſcribed one within an other. For the Octohedron contayneth the Icoſahedron inſcribed in it: and the ſame Icoſahedron contayneth the Dodecahedron inſcribed in the ſame Icoſahedron: and the ſame dodecahedron contayneth the cube inſcribed in the ſame Octohedron, and finally the ſame cube circumſcribeth the Pyramis inſcribed in the ſayd Octohedron. But this happeneth not in the other ſolides.
