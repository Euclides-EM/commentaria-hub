## The 6. Theoreme. The 6. Propoſition.

If two magnitudes be equemultiplices to two magnitudes, & any partes taken away of them alſo, be equemultiplices to the ſame magnitudes: the reſidues alſo of them ſhal vnto the ſame magnitudes be either equall, or equemultiplices.

[Margin]
Two caſes in this Proportion.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=3|style=decorated|decoration="woodcut initial with face and foliage"}Vppoſe that there be two magnitudes AB and CD equemultiplices to two magnitudes E and F, and let the partes take away of the magnitudes AB and CD, namely, AG and CH be equemultiplices to the ſame magnitudes E and F. Then I ſay that the reſidues GB and HD, are vnto the ſelfe ſame magnitudes E and F either equall, or els equemultiplices.

[Margin]
The firſt.
[/Margin]

Suppoſe firſt that GB be equall vnto E. Then I ſay that HD is equall vnto F. Vnto F put an equall magnitude CK.

[Margin]
Conſtruction. Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

And foraſmuch as AG is equemultiplex vnto E, as CH is vnto F: but GB is equall vnto E, & KC vnto F: therfore AB is equemultiplex to E, as KH is to F. But AB is put equemultiplex vnto E, as CD is to F. Wherfore KH is equemultiplex vnto F, as CD is to F. And foraſmuch as either of theſe KH and CD are equimultiplices vnto F, therfore (by the 1 common ſentence) KH is equall vnto CD. Take away CH which is common to them both. Wherfore the reſidue KC is equall vnto the reſidue HD. But KC is equall vnto F, wherfore HD is equall vnto F. Wherfore if GB be equall vnto E, DH alſo ſhall be equall vnto F.
