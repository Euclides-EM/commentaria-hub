[Subhead]
*extremes be prime the one to the other, the leſſe ſhall not be to any other number, as the firſt is to the ſecond.*
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
Demonſtration leading to an abſurditie.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="geometric lattice"}uppoſe that there be theſe numbers in continuall proportion A, B, C, D, and let their extremes A and D be prime the one to the other. Then I ſay that D is not to any other number as A is to B. For if it be poſſible, as A is to B, ſo let D be to E. Wherfore alternately by the 13. of the ſeuenth, as A is to D, ſo is B to E. But A and D are prime, yea they are prime and the leaſt. But the leaſt numbers (by the 21. of the ſeuenth) meaſure the numbers that haue one and the ſame proportion with them equally, the antecedent the antecedent, and the conſequent.

[Diagram: five horizontal lines labelled A, B, C, D and E.]

Wherefore the antecedent A meaſureth the antecedent B: but as A is to B, ſo is B to C. Wherfore B alſo meaſureth C. Wherfore A alſo meaſureth C (by the 5. common ſentence of the ſeuenth) and foraſmuch as B is to C, ſo is C to D, but B meaſureth C. Wherfore C meaſureth D. But A meaſureth C. Wherfore A alſo meaſureth D by the ſame common ſentence; and it alſo meaſureth it ſelfe: Wherefore A meaſureth theſe numbers A and D being prime the one to the other, which is impoſſible. Wherfore D is not to any other number as A is to B: which was required to be proued.

## ¶ The 18. Theoreme. The 18. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
*Two numbers being geuen, to ſearche out if it be poſſible a third number in proportion with them.*
[/Subhead]

[Margin]
Three caſes in this propoſition. The firſt caſe.
[/Margin]

{dropcap:S|lines=3|style=decorated|decoration="foliate pattern"}uppoſe that the two numbers geuen be A and B. It is required to ſearche out if it be poſſible a third number proportionall with them. Now A, B are either prime the one to the other or not prime. If they be prime, then (by the 16. of the ninth) it is manifeſt that it is impoſſible to finde out a third number proportionall with them. But now ſuppoſe that A B be not prime the one to the other. And let B multiplying himſelfe produce C.

[Margin]
The ſecond caſe.
[/Margin]

Now A either meaſureth C, or meaſureth it not. Firſt, let it meaſure it and that by D. Wherfore A multiplying D produceth C. But B alſo multiplying himſelfe produced C.

[Diagram: three horizontal lines labelled A and B.]

Wherfore that which is produced of A into D, is equall to that which is produced of B into himſelfe. Wherefore (by the ſecond part of the 19. of the ſeuenth) as A is to B, ſo is B to D. Wherfore vnto theſe numbers A, B is found out a third number in proportion, namely, D.

[Diagram: four horizontal lines labelled A, B, D and C.]

[Margin]
The third caſe.
[/Margin]

But now ſuppoſe that A do not meaſure C. Then I ſay that it is impoſſible to finde out a third number in proportion with theſe numbers A, B. For if it be poſſible, let there be found out ſuch a number, and let the ſame be D.

[Diagram: four horizontal lines labelled A, B, D and C.]
