Wherfore the triangle A B K conſiſteth of three vnequal ſides. And ſo haue ye vpon the line geuen, deſcribed all the kindes of triangles.

This is to be noted, that if a man wil mechanically and redely, not regarding demonſtration vpon a line geuen deſcribe a triangle of three equall ſides, he needeth not to deſcribe the whole foreſayd circle, but onely a little part of eche: namely, where they cut the one the other, and ſo from the point of the ſection to draw the lines to the endes of the line geuen. And likewiſe, if vpon the ſaid line he will deſcribe a triangle of two equall ſydes, let him extende the compaſſe according to the quantitie that he will haue the ſyde to be, whether longer then the line geuen or ſhorter: and ſo draw onely a little part of eche circle, where they cut the one the other, & frõ the point of the ſection draw the lines to the endes of the line geuen. As in the figures here put. Note that in this the two ſydes muſt be ſuch, that beyng ioyned together, they be longer then the line geuen.

And ſo alſo if vpon the ſayd right line he will deſcribe a triangle of three vnequall ſydes, let him extend the compaſſe. Firſt, according to the quantitie that he will haue one of the vnequall ſydes to be, and ſo draw a little part of the circle, & then extend it according to the quantitie that he will haue the other vnequall ſyde to be, and draw likewyſe a little part of the circle, and that done, from the point of the ſection draw the lines to the endes of the line geuen, as in the figure here put. Note that in this the two ſides muſt be ſuch, that the circles deſcribed according to their quãtitie may cut the one the other.
