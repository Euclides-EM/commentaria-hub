## ¶ The 20. Theoreme.  The 22. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If three numbers be in continuall proportion, and if the firſt be a ſquare number, the third alſo ſhall be a ſquare number.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="foliate ornament"}uppoſe that there be three numbers in continuall proportion A, B, C, and let the firſt be a ſquare number. Then I ſay that the third is alſo a ſquare number.

[Diagram: three dotted horizontal lines labelled A, B and C]

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

For foraſmuch as betwene A and C there is one meane proportionall number, namely B, therefore (by the 20. of the eight) A and C are like playne numbers. But A is a ſquare number. Wherfore C alſo is a ſquare number: which was required to be proued.

## ¶ The 21. Theoreme.  The 23. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If foure numbers be in continuall proportion, and if the firſt be a cube number, the fourth alſo ſhall be a cube number.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="foliate ornament"}uppoſe that there be foure numbers in continuall proportion A, B, C, D. And let A be a cube number. Then I ſay that D alſo is a cube number.

[Diagram: four dotted horizontal lines labelled A, B, C and D]

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

For foraſmuch as betwene A and D there are two meane proportionall numbers B, C. Therfore A, D, are like ſolide numbers (by the 21. of this booke) But A is a cube number: wherfore D alſo is a cube number: which was required to be demonſtrated.

## ¶ The 22. Theoreme.  The 24. Propoſition.

[Subhead]
If two numbers be in the ſame proportion that a ſquare number is to a ſquare number, and if the firſt be a ſquare number, the ſecond alſo ſhall be a ſquare number.
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="interlaced lattice ornament"}uppoſe that two numbers A and B be in the ſame proportion, that the ſquare number C is vnto the ſquare number D. And let A be a ſquare number. Then I ſay that B alſo is a ſquare number.

[Diagram: four dotted horizontal lines labelled A, B, C and D]

[Margin]
Demonſtration.
[/Margin]

For foraſmuch as C and D are ſquare numbers. Therfore C and D are like plaine numbers. Wherfore (by the 18. of the eight) betwene C and D there is one meane proportionall number. But as C is to D, ſo is A to B. Wherfore betwene A and B there is one meane proportionall number (by the 8. of the eight) But A is a ſquare number. Wherfore (by the 22. of the eight) B alſo is a ſquare number: which was required to be proued.
