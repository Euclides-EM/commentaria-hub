The third irrational line which is called a firſt bimediall line, is ſhewed by this propoſition, and the definition thereof is by it made manifeſt, which is this. A firſt bimediall line, is an irrationall line, which is compoſed of two mediall lines commenſurable in power onely contayning a rationall parallelograme. It is called a firſt bimediall line, bycauſe the two mediall lines or partes whereof it is compoſed contayne a rationall ſuperficies, which is preferred before an irrationall.

## ¶ The 26. Theoreme. The 38. Propoſition.

If two mediall lines commenſurable in power onely contayning a mediall ſuperficies, be added together: the whole line is irrationall, and is called a ſecond bimediall line.

{dropcap:L|lines=6|style=decorated|decoration="woodcut with winged figure and beast"}Et theſe two medial lines A B and B C being commenſurable in power onely, and contayning a mediall ſuperficies (the 28. of the tenth teacheth to finde out two ſuch lines) be added together. Then I ſay that the whole line A C is irrationall. Take a rationall line D E. And (by the 44. of the firſt) vpon the line D E apply the parallelograme D F, equal to the ſquare of the line A C, whoſe other ſide let be the line D G. And foraſmuch as the ſquare of the line A C is (by the 4. of the ſecond) equall to that which is compoſed of the ſquares of the lines A B and B C, together with that which is contained vnder the lines A B and B C twiſe: but the ſquare of the line A C is equall to the parallelograme D F. Wherefore the parallelograme D F is equall to that which is compoſed of the ſquares of the lines A B and B C together with that which is contayned vnder the lines A B and B C twiſe. Now then agayne (by the 44, of the firſt) vpon the line D E apply the parallelograme E H equall to the ſquares of the lines A B and B C. Wherefore the parallelograme remayning, namely, H F, is equall to that which is contained vnder the lines A B and B C twiſe. And foraſmuch as either of theſe lines A B and B C is mediall, therefore the ſquares of the lines A B and B C are alſo mediall. And that which is contained vnder the lines A B and B C twiſe is (by the corollary of the 24. of the tenth) mediall. For by the 6. of this booke it is commẽſurable to that which is contained vnder the lines A B and B C once, which is by ſuppoſition medial. But vnto the ſquares of the lines A B and B C is equall the parallelograme E H, and vnto that which is contayned vnder the lines A B and B C twiſe is equall the parallelograme H F: wherefore either of theſe parallelogrames H E and A F is mediall: and they are applyed vpon the rationall line E D. Wherefore (by the 22. of the tenth) either of theſe lines D H and H G is a rationall line, and incommenſurable in length vnto the line D E. And foraſmuch as (by ſuppoſition) the line A B is incommenſurable in length vnto the line B C. But as the line A B is to the line B C, ſo is the ſquare of the line A B to the parallelograme which is contayned vnder the lines A B and B C (by the firſt of the ſixt).
