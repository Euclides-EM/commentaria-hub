## ¶ The 6. Propoſition. The 6. Probleme.

In an Octohedron geuen, to inſcribe a trilater equilater Pyramis.

{dropcap:S|lines=9|style=decorated|decoration="foliate woodcut initial"}Vppoſe that the Octohedron wherein the Tetrahedron is required to be inſcribed, be A B G D E I. Take fower baſes of the Octohedron, that is, three which cloſe in the loweſt triangle E G D, namely, A E G, B E D, I G D: and let the fourth be A I B, which is oppoſite to the loweſt triangle before put, namely, to E G D. And take the centres of thoſe fower baſes, which let be the pointes H, C, N, L. And vpon the triangle H C N erecte a pyramis H C N L. Now foraſmuch as theſe two baſes of the Octohedron, namely, A G E and A B I are ſet vpon the right lines E G and B I which are oppoſite the one to the other, in the ſquare G E B I of the Octohedron, from the point A drawe by the centres of the baſes, namely, by the centres H, L, perpendicular lines A H F, A L K, cutting the lines E G and B I into two equall partes in the pointes F, K (by the Corollary of the 12. of the thirtenth). Wherfore a right line drawen frõ the point F to the point K, ſhall be a parallel and equall to the ſides of the Octohedron, namely, to E B and G I (by the 33. of the firſt). And the right line H L which cutteth the equall ſides A F, A K, proportionally (for A H and A L are drawen from the centres of equall circles to the circumferences) is a parallel to the right line F K (by the 2. of the ſixth) and alſo to the ſides of the Octohedron, namely, to E B and I G (by the 9. of the eleuenth). Wherefore as the line A F is to the line A H, ſo is the line F K to the line H L (by the 4. of the ſixth): For the triangles A F K and A H L are like (by the Corollary of the 2. of the ſixth). But the line A F is in ſeſquialter proportion to the line A H: (for the ſide E G maketh H F the halfe of the right line AH, by the Corollary of the 12. of the thirtenth). Wherfore F K or G I the ſide of the Octohedron, is ſeſquialter to the right line H L. And by the ſame reaſon may we proue that the ſides of the Octohedron are ſeſquialter to the reſt of the right lines which make the pyramis H N C L, namely, to the right lines H N, N C, C L, L N, and C H: wherefore thoſe right lines are equall, and therefore the triangles which are deſcribed of them, namely, the triangles H C N, H N L, N C L, and C H L, which make the pyramis H N C L, are equall and equilater. And foraſmuch as the angles of the ſame pyramis, namely, the angles H, C, N, L, do end in the centres of the baſes of the Octohedron, therefore it is inſcribed in the ſame Octohedron, by the firſt definition of this booke. Wherefore in an Octohedron geuen, is inſcribed a trilater equilater pyramis: which was required to be done.

## ❧ A Corollary.

The baſes of a Pyramis inſcribed in an Octohedron, are parallels to the baſes of the Octohedron. For foraſmuch as the ſides of the baſes of the Pyramis touching the one the other, are parallels to the ſides of the Octohedron which alſo touch the one the other, as for example, H L was proued to be a parallel to G I, and L C to D I, therefore, by the 15. of the eleuenth, the plaine ſuperficies which is drawen by the lines H L and L C, is a parallel to the plaine ſuperficies drawen by the lines G I and D I. And ſo likewiſe of the reſt.
