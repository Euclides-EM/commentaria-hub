## The 6. Propoſition.

If there be two triangles wherof the one hath one angle equall to one angle of the other, & the ſides including the equall angles be proportionall: the triangles ſhall be equiangle, and thoſe angles in them ſhall be equall, vnder which are ſubtended ſides of like proportion.

{dropcap:S|lines=3|style=decorated|decoration="foliate design"}uppoſe that there be two triangles ABC, and DEF, which let haue the angle BAC of the one triangle equall vnto the angle EDF of the other triangle, and let the ſides including the equall angles be proportionall, that is, as BA is to AC, ſo let ED be to DF. Then I ſay, that yͤ triangle ABC is equiangle vnto the triangle DEF: and the angle ABC is equall vnto the angle DEF, and the angle ACB equall vnto the angle DFE, which angles are ſubtended to ſides of like proportion.

[Margin]
Conſtruction.
[/Margin]

Vnto the right line DF, and to the poynt in it D (by the 23. of the firſt) deſcribe vnto either of yͤ angles BAC and EDF, an equall angle FDG. And vnto the right line DF, and vnto the point in it F (by yͤ ſame) deſcribe vnto yͤ angle ACB an equall angle DFG. And foraſmuch as the two angles BAC and ACB, are (by the 17. of the firſt) leſſe then two right angles: therefore alſo the two angles FDG and DFG, are leſſe then two right angles. Wherefore yͤ lines DG & FG being produced, ſhall concurre (by the 5. petition). Let thẽ concurre in the point G. Wherefore DFG is a triangle.
