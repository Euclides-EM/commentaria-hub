{dropcap:S|lines=4|style=decorated|decoration="floral ornamental design"}Vppoſe that the two numbers geuen be A and B. It is required to finde out the leſt number which they meaſure. Now A and B. are either prime the one to the other, or not. Suppoſe firſt that A and b be prime the one to the other: and let A multiplying B produce C: wherefore B, multiplying A produceth alſo C (by the 16. of the ſeuenth). Wherefore A and B meaſure C. Now alſo I ſay, that C is the leſt nũber which they meaſure. For if it be not, thoſe numbers A and B meaſure ſome number leſſe then C: let them meaſure ſome number leſſe then C, and let the ſame be D: and how often A meaſureth D, ſo many vnities let there be in E: and how often B meaſureth D, ſo many vnities let there be in F. Wherefore A multiplying E produceth D, and B multiplying F produceth alſo D. Wherefore that which is produced of A into E, is equall to that which is produced of B into F: wherefore (by the 19. of the ſeuenth) as A is to B, ſo is F to E. But A and B are prime: yea they are prime and alſo the leſt in that proportion (by the 23. of the ſeuenth): but the leſt numbers in any proportion meaſure thoſe numbers that haue one and the ſame proportion with them equally: the greater the greater: and the leſſe the leſſe (by the 21. of the ſeuenth). Wherefore B meaſureth E, namely, the conſequent the conſequent. And foraſmuch as A multiplying B and E produced C and D: therefore (by the 17. of the ſeuenth) as B is to E, ſo is C to D. But B meaſureth E. Wherefore C alſo meaſureth D, the greater, the leſſe: which is impoſſible. Wherefore if thoſe numbers A and B be prime, they ſhall meaſure no number leſſe then C. Wherefore C is the leſt number which A and B meaſure.

But now ſuppoſe that A and B be not prime the one to the other, and take (by the 35. of the ſeuenth) the leſt numbers that haue one and the ſame proportion with A and B, and let the ſame be F and E. Wherefore that which is produced of A into E, is equall to that which is produced of B into F (by the 19. of the ſeuenth). Let A multiplying E produce C: wherefore B multiplying F produceth alſo C. Wherefore A and B meaſure C. Then I ſay, that C is the leſt number that they meaſure. For if it be not, thoſe numbers A and B ſhall meaſure ſome number leſſe then C: let them meaſure a number leſſe then C, and let the ſame be D. And how often A meaſureth D, ſo many vnities let there be in G. And how often B meaſureth D, ſo many vnities let there be in H. Now then A multiplying G produceth D. And B multiplying H produceth alſo D. Wherefore that which is produced of A into G, is equall to that which is produced of B into H. Wherefore (by the 19. of the ſeuenth) as A is to B, ſo is H to G. But as A is to B, ſo is F to E. Wherefore as F is to E, ſo is H to G: but the leſt numbers in any proportion meaſure the numbers that haue the ſame proportion with them equally, the greater the greater, & the leſſe the leſſe (by the 21. of the ſeuenth). Wherefore E meaſureth G. And foraſmuch as A multiplying G and E produced C and D; therefore (by the 17. of the fiſt) as E is to G, ſo is C to D. But E meaſureth G. Wherefore C alſo meaſureth D, the greater the leſſe: which is impoſſible. Wherefore thoſe numbers A and B do not meaſure any number leſſe then C. Wherefore C is the leſt number that is meaſured by A and B: which was required to be done.
