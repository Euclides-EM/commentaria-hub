Definitions.

Commensurable magnitudes are said to be those which, when
measured, are measured by the same measure.
Incommensurable, however, are those of which no common
measure is admitted to come to be.
Straight lines are commensurable in square when the squares
on them are measured by the same measure.
Incommensurable, however, when the squares on them admit
no common measure to come to be.
