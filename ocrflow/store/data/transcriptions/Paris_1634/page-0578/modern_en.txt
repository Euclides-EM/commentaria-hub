II.

A magnitude measuring
any magnitude, measures also
every magnitude
which that (magnitude) measures.

A magnitude which measures
some magnitude, measures also
every magnitude
which that one measures.

III.

A magnitude measuring
a whole magnitude, measures also
the part taken away and the
remainder.
  These axioms, insofar as they
pertain to numbers, have already
been explained in the last three
statements of the seventh book.

A magnitude measuring
a whole magnitude, and the part
taken away from it, measures also
the rest.
  These common sentences, insofar
as they belong to numbers, have already
been explained in the
last three axioms of Book 7.

THEOREM I.  PROPOSITION I.

With two unequal magnitudes
proposed, if from the greater there be taken away
more than the half; and from that which remains,
again there be taken away more
than the half, and this be always done:
there will at length be left some magnitude
which will be less than the
proposed lesser magnitude.
