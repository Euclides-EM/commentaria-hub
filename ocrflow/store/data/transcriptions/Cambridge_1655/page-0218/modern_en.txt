Axioms.

1. A magnitude measuring any magnitudes
also measures the magnitude composed from them.

2. A magnitude measuring any magnitude
also measures every magnitude
which that magnitude measures.

3. A magnitude measuring a whole magnitude
and the part taken away, also measures the remainder.

PROP.  I.

With two unequal magnitudes
AB, C proposed, if from the greater AB there be taken away
more than the half, (AH)
and from it (HB), which is the remainder, again there be
taken away more than the half (HI),
and this be always done; there will at length be left
a certain magnitude IB, which will be less than
the proposed lesser magnitude C.

Take C so many times, until its
multiple DE nearly exceed AB; and let
DF = FG = GE = C. Take from AB more
than the half AH, and from the remainder HB more than
the half HI, and so on, until the parts AH,
HI, IB are equally many as the parts DF, FG, GE.
Now it is clear that FE, which is not less than 1/2 DE,
is greater than HB, which is less than
1/2 AB — DE. And likewise GE which is not less
than 1/2 FE, is greater than IB, — 1/2 HB, there-
fore C, or GE — IB.  Q. E. D.

The same will be demonstrated, if from AB there be taken away
the half AH, and from the remainder HB again the half
HI, and so on.
