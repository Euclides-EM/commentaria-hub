Again, let AB, CD be straight lines, between which no
common measure falls; these will be incommensurable in
length, and let their squares be AE,
CF; and supposing that the space Z is the common measure of the
squares AE, CF, the straight lines AB, CD, by the third definition, will be
commensurable in power. Let the squares AE, CF be understood to be divided into the
spaces X, and Y, each one equal to the
space Z, and we shall have known the parts
of the powers AE, CF, with respect to the
measure Z; and for that which has been said, the
straight lines AB, CD are also rational; and
these constitute the second kind
of rational straight lines. Those straight lines then,
which have no common measure, neither in
length, nor in power, constitute
the other kind, and are called Irrational.

Finally, because any straight line can be divided into as many equal parts
as one wishes, therefore every straight line can be established as a known
magnitude, that is, as a measure, or rule, to which all the
infinite other straight lines can be compared. For example, let any straight line AB be set down,
which being able at our discretion to be divided into
as many equal parts as we wish, we can
also establish it as a known magnitude, and
express those parts into which we shall have
divided it, or others, into which it can
always be divided: and, comparing to this all the infinite other straight lines, many
of them will be commensurable with this in length, and for that which has been
said, will also be commensurable in power: others will be only
commensurable in power; and others will be incommensurable in length,
and also in power: this straight line AB, or another, which can be put in its
place, established as a quantity known to us, to which all the others
will be compared, is that which Euclid in the fifth definition of this calls
Rational; to which, the others being compared, those which to this will be
commensurable in length and power, these will be only commensurable
in power, he calls in the 6th definition Rational. The others, which to this will be
incommensurable in length and power, in the 7th definition he calls Irrational.
