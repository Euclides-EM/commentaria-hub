Because every finite magnitude can be increased to infinity, therefore, by
multiplying a proposed magnitude more and more times, finally one will
arrive at a magnitude greater than any other finite magnitude of the
same kind.

A X I O M S.
I.

That magnitude which measures many other magnitudes
will also measure the compound of them.

I I.

If one magnitude measures another magnitude, it will also measure
that which is measured by it.

I I I.

If one magnitude measures an entire other magnitude, and measures
a part of it, it will also measure the remainder.

T H E O R E M  I.  P R O P O S I T I O N  I.

If of two unequal magnitudes from the greater there is subtracted
more than the half, and from the remainder there is subtracted more than
the half, and again from what remains there is subtracted more than
the half, and this is done always, finally there will remain a
magnitude less than the other.

Let two unequal magnitudes be proposed,
as AB the greater, & C
the lesser. I say that if from the
greater AB there is subtracted more than the
half, and from the remainder there is subtracted
again more than the half, and again from
what remains there is subtracted more than
the half, and this is done always,
finally there will remain a magnitude less than the given C. Let DE be taken
a multiple of C in such a way that the magnitude DE is the nearest greater to
AB, and let it be divided into parts equal to C, which are DF, FG, GE; let there be subtracted
from the magnitude AB more than the half, which is AH: and from the remainder HB
let there be subtracted likewise more than the half, which is HI: and these subtractions
let be made so many times, in such a way that the parts AH, HI, IB are
as many as there are parts in DE. I say that the last remainder IB is
less than C.
