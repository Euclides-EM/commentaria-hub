IN THE TENTH BOOK.
1  IF, with two incommensurable magnitudes set forth,
the lesser be always subtracted from the greater, by a certain
alternate subtraction: the remainder will never measure the preceding.
To proposition 2.
2  IF, with two commensurable magnitudes set forth,
the lesser be always subtracted from the greater, by a certain
alternate subtraction: some remainder will measure the preceding:
To proposition 2.
3.  A MAGNITUDE measuring two magnitudes, also measures
their greatest common measure.  Corollary.
proposition 3.
4  To consider whether any proposed magnitudes are commensurable
or not. To proposition 3.
5  A MAGNITUDE measuring any magnitudes, also measures
their greatest common measure. Corollary.
proposition 4
6  IF there be any magnitudes, and also as many numbers,
which, taken two and two, are in the same ratio in which two
magnitudes are: then from equality, in the same ratio will be
magnitudes and numbers. To proposition 4.
7  COMMENSURABLE MAGNITUDES have the same proportion
as the numbers by which their greatest common measure measures them.
To proposition 5.

a ratio as number to number.  Corollary.
proposition 6.
