W Hen the magnitude C taken four times measures and
agrees with the magnitude A, but taken three times
the magnitude B: thus the magnitudes A
and B are said to be measurable magnitudes, because the magnitude C, which
is an even part of both, also measures both. In such a way are also the lines, whose
one length is 20 fathoms, and the other 13 fathoms, called measurable lines,
because one fathom, a half fathom, a third fathom, etc. measures both.
Likewise the planes are called measurable, if one and the same plane measures
both these planes: and also the solids measurable, when the
same solid measures both these solids.
