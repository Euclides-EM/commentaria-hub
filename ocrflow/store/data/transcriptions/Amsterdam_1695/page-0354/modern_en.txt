MEASURING-ART. 10. Book. 315

The third Common Notion.

A magnitude measuring a whole magnitude, as also a separated
part, also measures the remainder of the same.

This common notion has the same force in magnitudes as the twelfth common
notion of the seventh book in numbers.

The propositions of the tenth Book.

The First Proposition.

If, two unequal proposed magnitudes A B and C being, al-
ways from the greatest A B more than the half A H is taken away,
and from the remainder H B again more than the half H I, and this al-
so always proceeding: so there shall at length remain a magnitude B I,
which shall be less than the proposed least magnitude C.

The Proof.  Multiplying C so many times,
until its resulting magnitude D E is great-
er than A B, in such manner also that if C had once
been taken less in the multiplication, that the
resulting magnitude would have been less than A B.
Then dividing D E into parts D F, F G and G E, which in par-
ticular are equal to C, and let there be taken after the addition from A B
more than the half A H and for the remainder B H more than the half H I,
and so on, until the parts A H, H I and B I in multitude are equal to the parts D F,
F G and G E in multitude.  Therefore then the parts A H, H I and
I B are just as many times as the parts D F, F G and G E.  Since
D E is greater than A B by the construction, but from D E is taken away
D F less than the half D E, or not greater, if D E is the double
of the magnitude C, and from A B is taken away more than the half
A H: Therefore the remainder E F shall be more than the remainder B H [ for
since D E is greater than A B, if from D E the half is taken away,
as also from A B, the half, then the remainder of the magnitude D E shall be more than
the remainder of the magnitude A B; If then from D E is taken
the half, or less, and from A B more than the half, then all the more the
remainder of the magnitude D E shall be greater than the remainder of the magnitude A B
shall be ].  Again F E being greater than H B, and from F E being taken
the half or less than the half F G, but from H B being taken more than
the half H I, so shall by the same force G F be greater than B I.  And
thus proceeding, it is shown the last part of the magnitude D E, as
here G E, to be greater than the last part of the magnitude A B, as here B I.
