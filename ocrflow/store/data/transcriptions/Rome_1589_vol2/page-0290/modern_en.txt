It is concluded, however, that lines which can [produce] spaces incommen-
surable with the square on a Rational line, ought to be called Irrationa-

that lines which can [produce] spaces commensurable with the same square
on a Rational line, ought to be called Rational. For the lines
which can [produce] those incommensurable spaces are in power in-
commensurable with the Rational line, from def. 4. Since therefore all
lines incommensurable in power are also incommen-
surable in length, as we said at def. 4, and it will be demon-
strated in the corollary of prop. 2, it is manifest, from def. 7, that those
lines which can [produce] spaces incommensurable with the square on a Ra-
tional line, are called Irrational.

Nor is it to be passed over that Euclid and the
ancient geometers almost always add these words: in length and in power, and in
power only, to these words: commensurable and incommensurable; but scarcely,
and very rarely, to these: Rational and Irrational. For lines are rightly said
commensurable in length and in power, or in power only;
likewise incommensurable in length and in power, or in length only.
But less rightly [they are called] Rational in length and in
power, or in power only, or Irrational in length and
power, or in length only. Which indeed, since
Campanus did not notice, gave occasion to many, that
yesterday, and obscurely in this 10th book, concerning commensurable lines,
and incommensurable, and also Rational and Irrational,
they have spoken.
