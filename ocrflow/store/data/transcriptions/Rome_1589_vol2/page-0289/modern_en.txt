ALL plane surfaces commensurable with the square on the
proposed Rational line are called Rational; not indeed
by position, as that one, but because when compared with it they are found to be either
commensurable, or incommensurable: just as
also lines which are in any way commensu-
rable with the Rational line are called Rational.

SINCE however the lines capable (in power) of the spaces commen-
surable with the square on the proposed Rational line are at least in power
commensurable with the Rational line; from the 3rd definition it is clear
that these also are called, according to definition 6, Rational.

X.

But those incommensurable with this,
let them be called Irrational.

In no other way are plane surfaces incommensurable with the square
described on the Rational line called Irrational, than as the
lines which are altogether incommensurable with the proposed Rational
are called Irrational.

XI.

And the straight lines which can (produce) them are Irratio-
nal: if indeed they are squares, the
sides themselves; but if they are some other rectilinear (figures),
the straight lines which describe squares equal to
the incommensurable spaces.
He calls lines which can (produce) spaces incommensurable with the square on the Rational
line, Irrational; just as he called the
spaces themselves Irrational: so that if the incommensurable
spaces should indeed be squares, their sides are called Irratio-
nal; but if they should not be squares, but any other recti-
linear figures, the straight lines which describe squares
equal to those incommensurable spaces are called Irrational.
