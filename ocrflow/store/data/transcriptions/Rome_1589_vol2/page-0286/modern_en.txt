But lines commensurable both in length
and in power have, as it were, this agreement among themselves, and con-
nection, that lines commensurable in length are also commen-
surable in power, so that no lines can be given commensura-
ble in length whose squares are not also commensurable; be-
cause the square described on their common mea-
sure measures, as a common measure, their squares,
as appears in the figure set below; for just as the straight line A B measures
the straight lines CD, EF, so also the square AG measures the squares CH, EI.
But it is not the case that all lines commensurable in power
are also commensurable in length; for many lines
are commensurable in power, that is, have commen-
surable squares, which nevertheless are altogether incommensura-
ble in length, as will be demonstrated in this book. Again, among lines
incommensurable both in length and in power, such
a connection is found, that all lines incommen-
surable in power are also incommensurable in length; but not
conversely, that all lines incommensurable in length
are also incommensurable in power, since many lines are found
incommensurable in length with one another, while nevertheless
in power, that is, according to their squares, they are commensura-
ble. All these things indeed will be made clear from the corollary
of proposition 9 of this book.
