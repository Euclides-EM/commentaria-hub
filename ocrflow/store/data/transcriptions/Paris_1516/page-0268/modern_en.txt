The geometrical Elements.        Book Tenth.
                                   Definitions.
From Campanus.

Quantities for which there is one quantity
that measures them in common are called
commensurable.
But those for which there is not one common
quantity measuring them are
called incommensurable.
Lines commensurable in power are called those
whose square surfaces
one common surface measures.
Lines incommensurable in power are called those whose
square surfaces one common
surface does not measure. And since these things are so, it is clear
that for any line there
can be many others incommensurable; some in length
only; some in length and in power.
But every line with which we reason, when set down, is
called rational.
And the lines commensurable with it are called rational.
But those incommensurable with it are called irrational
or surds.
And every square surface about which by hypothesis
we reason is called rational.
And the surfaces commensurable with it are called rational.
But the surfaces incommensurable with it are called
irrational or surds.
And the sides which can be set on those squares are called
irrational.

which the same measure measures.

no measurement by a common measure.

area.
