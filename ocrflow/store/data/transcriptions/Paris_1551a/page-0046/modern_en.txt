But if there shall be someone who reproaches me for pursuing matters too minute: let him know that our plan is this, that I may render this book, not so much difficult by its own nature as by ignorance of the principles, very easy to understand for those who will wish to explore this most beloved place of geometry.
And indeed, I think no one will doubt that very many have, through the fault of their own imprudence and a perverse understanding of the principles, fallen shamefully into the greatest errors, provided only that, reading our work, he has attained understanding of the things themselves. But of these matters thus far; let us return to our path. We say that this definition of commensurable magnitudes, or if you prefer to call it a principle, is plainly understood by a certain analogy from that place of Campanus. For what is said in numbers, of some numbers measuring other numbers: understand the same or something similar in magnitudes, of which we say that one is measured by another. And that it may be understood more plainly, let us take an example in one kind of magnitude. Let there be two lines a.
