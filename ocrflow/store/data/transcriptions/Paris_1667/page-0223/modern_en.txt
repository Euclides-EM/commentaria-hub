FOURTH LEMMA.                                       V.
THE INCLINATION of a line in a space is consid-
ered by the acute angle which it makes with the one and the other paral-
lel, always making it equal.

Whence it follows that two lines are equally incli-
ned in the same space, or in two different spaces
when the acute angles which the one makes are equal to the ac-
ute angles which the other makes.

And that the less inclined is that which makes its acute angle
less acute and nearer to a right angle.

FIFTH IMPORTANT LEMMA.                              VI.
WHEN two or several lines are drawn from one
same point upon the same line, they are deemed to be
in that same parallel space. For then it is only necessary
to conceive a line drawn through that common point, which is
parallel to that which terminates them. Whence it follows that the
sides of an angle terminated by a base are always deem-
ed to be in the same parallel space.

SIXTH LEMMA.                                        VII.
Two angles are called similar when, being
equal, the angles upon the base of the one are equal to the angles
upon the base of the other, each to each.

And one is assured that this is so; 1. when one knows that they
are equal, and that one of the angles upon the base of the one is equal
to one of the angles upon the base of the other: for from that it follows
that the other is equal also.

2. When, being equal, they are moreover isosceles. VIII.59.
