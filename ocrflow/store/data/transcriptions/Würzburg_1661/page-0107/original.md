## Propoſitio III. Problema.

[Subhead]
*Datis duabus rectis lineis inæqualibus, de majore æqualem minori abſcindere.*
[/Subhead]

{dropcap:S|lines=2|style=plain}Int duæ rectæ inæquales, A minor, & BC major, oporteatque ex majore BC detrahere lineam æqualem minori A.

[Margin]
Lineam æqualem minori abſcindere ex majori.
[/Margin]

Ad alterutrum extremum punctum lineæ majoris BC, nempe ad B, ponatur linea BD, æqualis lineæ minori A, *per 2. hujus.* Deinde centro B, intervallo BD, fiat circulus, *per 3. Pet.* ſecans BC in E. Dico, BE abſciſſam, eſſe æqualem ipſi A. Quoniam enim BE æqualis eſt rectæ BD, *per 15. Def.* & eidem rectæ BD æqualis eſt recta A, per conſtructionem; erunt A & BE inter ſe æquales, *per 1. Ax.* Datis igitur duabus inæqualibus, de majore æqualem minori abſcidimus; quod erat faciendum.

[Diagram: figure 93; circle with centre B, radius BD, line BC cutting circle at E, separate line A]

Quòd ſi duæ rectæ datæ conjungantur in uno extremo, quales ſunt BD, BC, conjunctæ in puncto B; deſcribatur circulus ex B ad intervallum minoris BD: hic enim auferet BE, æqualem ipſi BD, *per 15. Def.*

*In praxi ſufficit accipere circino intervallum minoris lineæ, & poſito uno circini pede in uno extremo majoris, altero abſcindere portionem; hæc enim æqualis erit minori datæ.*
