## IIII.

In circulo æqualiter diſtare à centro rectæ lineæ dicuntur, quando à centro ad ipſas perpédiculares ductę ſūt ęquales.

[Figure: two circles, each with a chord and perpendiculars drawn from the centre]

## V.

Magis autem diſtare à centro dicitur ea, ad quam maior perpendicularis cadit.

## VI.

Portio circuli eſt figura, quę recta linea, et circuli circumferétia continetur.

[Figure: three circle segments of decreasing size, each bounded by an arc and a straight line]

## VII.

Portionis auté angulus eſt, qui recta linea, et circuli circūferentia cóprehéditur.

[Figure: two circle segments, each bounded by an arc and a straight line]

## VIII.

In portione angulus eſt, quando in circū ferentia portionis ſumatur aliquod punctum, atque ab ipſo ad terminos lineæ eius, quæ baſis eſt portionis, rectę lineæ ducantur; angulus vero ductis lineis fit cótentus.

[Figure: circle segment with two straight lines drawn from a point on the arc to the ends of the base, forming an angle]

## VIIII.

Quando autem continentes angulum rectæ lineæ aſſumunt circumferentiam, in illa conſiſtere angulus dicitur.

[Figure: circle with an inscribed angle standing on a circumference]

## X.

Sector circuli eſt, quando angulus ad centrum cōſtiterit, figura contenta rectis lineis angulum comprehendentibus, et circumferentia ab ipſis aſſumpta.

[Figure: circle with a sector formed by two radii meeting at the centre]
