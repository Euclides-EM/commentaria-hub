# Fine_1536 — Transcription Summary

## The book

**Orontii Finei Delphinatis, Regii Mathematicarum professoris, In sex priores libros geometricorum elementorum Euclidis Megarensis Demonstrationes** — Oronce Finé's demonstrations on the first six books of Euclid's *Elements*, printed in Paris by Simon de Colines (Simon Colinæus), 1536, with a ten-year royal privilege.

The edition interleaves Euclid's Greek text with the Latin translation of Bartolomeo Zamberti (Bartholomæus Zambertus of Venice), revised by Finé, followed by Finé's own demonstrations (introduced "ORONTIVS."). The scan is the Gallica/BnF digitization of the Bibliothèque nationale de France copy, shelfmark RÉS. V 118 (1), with provenance notes on the title page including "De Bibliotheca Conuentus parisiensis pp. Minimorum ad Circum Regium. 1638" and the ownership inscription of Francoys Desneux.

## Transcription conventions

- One file per PDF page: `1.md` … `202.md`, transcribed by direct vision from the page images (no OCR software).
- Original early-modern Latin spelling preserved (u/v, æ/œ, abbreviations such as q;, cõ-, -ũ); long s (ſ) rendered as "s"; no modernization or translation.
- Greek theorem headings and passages transcribed as printed where legible.
- Printed marginal notes and manuscript annotations rendered as blockquotes.
- Woodcut figures marked `*[diagram: …]*` with their letter labels; ornaments as `*[engraving]*` etc.
- Illegible words marked `[?]`.

## Caveats

- **Page 2** could not be transcribed: its image repeatedly triggered the vision model's content filter. It belongs to the cover/binding photographs and almost certainly carries no printed text; `2.md` holds a placeholder.
- **Pagination quirk**: around PDF pages 137–151 the printed folio numbers run out of sequence (printed pp. 133–135 appear before pp. 121–125), apparently the binding/scanning order of this copy. Files follow PDF page order and note the printed page number.
- Greek set in ligature-heavy 16th-century type and heavily abbreviated Latin were transcribed on a best-effort basis; ratio tables are approximated as markdown tables.
