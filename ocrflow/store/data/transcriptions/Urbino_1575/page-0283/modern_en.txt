IL COMANDINO.

There are also certain common notions, of which Euclid makes use in this book, namely.

COMMON NOTIONS.
I.
Every magnitude multiplied can exceed every other magnitude
proposed of the same kind.
II.
Whatever magnitude measures another, will also measure that
which the said magnitude measures.
III.
Whatever magnitude measures the whole, and a part taken from
it, will also measure the remainder.
IIII.
Whatever magnitude measures two or more magnitudes, will
likewise measure that which is composed of them.

THEOREM I. PROPOSITION I.

Two unequal magnitudes being proposed, if from the greater
a part greater than the half be taken away, and from what remains
likewise a part greater than the half be taken away, and this be done
always, in the end there will remain a certain magnitude, which will be
less than every proposed lesser magnitude.
