DEFINITIONS.
I.

COMMENSURABLE are called
the magnitudes, which by one and the same
measure are measured.
II.

Incommensurable are called those
magnitudes, of which there is found
no common measure.
III.

Straight lines, in power are commensurable, when the
same area measures the squares made by them.

THE COMMANDINO.

Euclid has not defined straight lines commensurable in length separately, because-
they are included in the first definition of commensurable magnitudes, since
straight lines commensurable in length are those, which by one and the same measure are
measured.
I.III.

Straight lines in power are incommensurable, when for
the squares made by them there is found no common area.
V.

With things standing in this way, it is shown that to any pro-
posed straight line there are infinite lines both commensurable, and in-
commensurable, some in length and power, some in power
only. And the proposed straight line is called rational.
