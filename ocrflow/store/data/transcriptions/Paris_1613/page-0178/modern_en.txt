It must be noted here [that there are] three kinds of rational lines. The first, namely every
proposed line, which can be expressed by some number. The second,
every line commensurable in length with the proposed rational [line]. The third,
every line commensurable in power only with the proposed rational [line]. And
every line incommensurable both in length and in power with the proposed rational
is irrational.

7. The square described on a rational line, is called
rational.
8. The figures commensurable with the rational square, are
also rational.
9. And those which are incommensurable with the rational
square, are irrational & surd.
10. And the lines which can [produce] those irrational figures,
are irrational & surd.

A line is said to be able [to produce] a figure when it can describe a square, equal
to that figure, because every square is the power of the root or of its
side. Thus he said above that two lines are commensurable in power, when
not the lines, but the squares of those lines are commensurable.
