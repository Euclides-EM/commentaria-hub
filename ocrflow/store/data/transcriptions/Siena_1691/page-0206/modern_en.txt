9. And those which are commensurable with this,

10. And those which are incommensurable with this,

11. And the straight lines which themselves are called
Irrational: if indeed they are squares, the side itself;
but if [they are] some other rectilineal [figures], the straight
lines which describe squares equal [to them] in areas
incommensurable [with one another].

P E T I T I O N.

Euclid postulates that any magnitude
can be multiplied as many times as [needed], until it
exceeds a magnitude from given magnitudes [uncertain: expediting].

A X I O M S.
or
P R O N O U N C E M E N T S.

1. A magnitude measuring any magnitude
different [from it], also measures the [magnitude]
composed from them.

2. A magnitude measuring any magnitude,
also measures every magnitude which that [magnitude]
measures.

3. A magnitude measuring the whole magnitude,
and the part taken away, also measures the remainder.
