BOOK X.

DEFINITIONS.

1. Commensurable magnitudes
are said to be those which
the same measure measures.

2. Incommensurable
are those of which it happens
that no common measure
can be found.

3. Straight lines are commensurable
in power, whose squares the same
space measures.

4. But they are incommensurable, of which
for their squares no space, which is
a common measure of them, happens to be
found.

5. These being set down, it is shown that, for any
proposed straight line, there are straight lines
in infinite multitude, both commensurable
and incommensurable: some indeed in
length and in power; others truly in power
only. Let the proposed straight
line be called Rational.

6. And those commensurable with it either in
length and in power, or in power only,
[Rational].

7. But those incommensurable with it,
let them be called Irrational.

8. And the square which is made from the proposed
straight [line], let it be called Rational.
