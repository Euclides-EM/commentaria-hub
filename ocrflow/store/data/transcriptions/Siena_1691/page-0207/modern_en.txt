PROPOSITION I.

IF, with two unequal magnitudes
proposed (AB, & C); from the greater
(AB) there be taken away less than a half;
and from what is left, again there be sub-
tracted less than a half; and this be always
done; there will at length be left some
magnitude; which becomes less than the
proposed lesser magnitude (C)

Let the magnitudes AB, & C be given: Then
take C so many times, until its multiple Z
nearly exceed the given AB: then re-
solve the same Z into its parts, of which each
is equal to C itself: and then according to the
hypothesis; take from AB more than a half,
and (if there be need) from the remainder more than
a half; and so on thereafter, until the unequal
parts of the magnitude AB in number
be equal to the equal parts of the magnitude
Z. Hence there are infinite cases in this
proposition, as namely, whether twice, or thrice, or
four times, &c. C itself being taken, it nearly
exceed the given AB; yet three, and no more,
we have set forth the cases in the adjoining
