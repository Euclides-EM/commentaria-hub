                         Definitions.
From Campanus.

Quantities for which there is one quantity
measuring them in common: are
called communicant.
¶But those for which there is not one
common quantity measuring them, are
called incommensurable.
¶Lines communicant in power
are called: whose square
surfaces one common surface
measures
¶Lines incommensurable in power are called: whose
square surfaces one common
surface does not measure. Since these things are so: it is
manifest that for any line set down: many others are
incommensurable; some in length
only; some in length & power.
¶But every line with which we reason, being set down, is
called rational.
¶And the lines communicant with it are called rational.
¶But those incommunicant with it are called irrational
or surd.
¶And every square surface about which by hypothesis
we reason is called rational.
¶And the surfaces communicant with it are called rational.
¶But the surfaces incommensurable with it are called
irrational or surd.
¶But the sides which can be in those squares are called
irrational.
