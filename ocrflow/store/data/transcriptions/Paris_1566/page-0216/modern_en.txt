NOTICE.

We vary the following corollary from the texture of Zamberti, as foreign, so that we may show that
Euclid in this tenth [book] chiefly will teach the knowledge of incommensurable or uncertain magnitudes,
following the Greek exemplar: for those which he calls commensurable in power only, he understands
to be incommensurable in length; but the rest to be incommensurable in both ways, both in power and
in length, so that those which are compared with the set forth [magnitudes] are all understood to be
incommensurable.

With these things set forth it is indicated that to the proposed straight line (whose quantity is certain
and determined) straight lines are compared in infinite multitude. Some indeed commensurable, in
power only; but incommensurable in length and power.

But if Euclid concludes the business of incommensurable quantities in this tenth [book] (which from
the things said before, to any proposed straight line, that is, of certain and determined or known quantity,
straight lines are compared in infinite multitude. But all are incommensurable, yet in two respects: some
indeed commensurable, although they are only in power, yet incommensurable in length; others however
incommensurable in both, namely in length and in power: and thus all incommensurable to the same
proposed [line] will be in two respects. But that we may conceive the true mind of Euclid, the proposed
straight line is not only that which is signified by a magnitude known to us, as it may be of a cubit, feet,
palms, or a finger (as Zamberti wished) contrary to the Greek exemplar; but whatever is set forth by us,
of whatever kind it may be, as a measure. For since it is free to divide any straight line into as many
segments as [one wishes] (from the ninth and sixteenth of the sixth) we attribute these segments to a
straight line which will be incommensurable with a line of a foot or a cubit, and therefore to this proposed
line of a foot or a cubit it will be incommensurable, and likewise there can be given to it incommensurables
either in length only, or in length and power together. And by this reasoning from this proposed [line] all
the species of incommensurables written in this tenth [book] can be generated by the same way, just as
from a foot or a cubit: as for example, a straight line [uncertain: summus] (by the thirty-sixth of the sixth)
being cut, the greater segment will be incommensurable in length and power with the whole, from the
sixth of the thirteenth of the tenth. Yet nevertheless, that greater segment having been taken, we shall
divide it into as many parts as we please, signified by a chosen number, from which at length we produce
infinite straight lines commensurable with this greater segment, commensurable in power only and
incommensurable in length, and others incommensurable both in length and in power, if indeed we have
taken that segment as the proposed and certain [one]; but if we wish to find uncertain ones, by the same
law we produce uncertain ones in respect of that segment, incommensurable to it, as in a proposed cubit,
either in power only, or in power and length together incommensurable.

NOTICE.

Although these things are not properly delivered in their own place, nevertheless, deeming them worthy
of notice, we have set them here, to be understood more suitably later, lest those who might err after
Zamberti should take away this liberty of Geometry, that it is permitted to propose only those straight
measures observed by certain persons, as they decide the proposed [line], as if it were forbidden that in
different regions feet, or palms, or cubits, if they are incommensurable in two respects, should be had,
since indeed all are proposed straight lines. And therefore upon these proposed [lines] to exhibit lengths
of straight lines by which others incommensurable might be given: which would be absurd. Therefore it
will be permitted to propose any magnitudes of lines, so that incommensurables may be compared to the
same, and thus we shall say that that is proposed which is proposed from the hypothesis.
