TERMS.                        DEFINITIONS.

1. Commensurable magnitudes are said
to be those which the same measure
measures.
2. But incommensurable, those for which
it happens that there is no common measure.
3. Straight lines are commensurable in
power, when the squares from them
measure the same area.
4. But incommensurable, when
it happens that the squares from them
measure no common area.
5. With these laid down it is shown that,
to any proposed straight line, there are
infinitely many straight lines both commensurable
and incommensurable, some indeed in
length and in power, but others in power
only. And let the proposed straight
line be called rational.
6. And those commensurable with it, whether in
length and in power, or in power
only, [are called] rational.
7. But the incommensurable are called irra-
tional.
8. And the square which is made from the
proposed straight line is called rational.
9. And those commensurable with it indeed,
[are called] rational.
10. But the incommensurable are called
irrational.

rable are called irrational:
