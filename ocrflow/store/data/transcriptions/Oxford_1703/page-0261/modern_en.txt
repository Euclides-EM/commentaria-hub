PROP. I. THEOR.                         PROPOSITION I.

With two unequal magnitudes set out, if from the greater there be taken away more
than the half, and from what remains again there be taken away more than the half,
and this be always done; there will at length be left some magnitude which will be
less than the lesser magnitude set out.

Let there be two unequal magnitudes A B, Γ, of which A B is the greater: I say that
if from A B there be taken away more than the half, and from what remains again
there be taken away more than the half, and this be always done; there will at length
be left some magnitude which will be less than the magnitude Γ.

For let Γ, being multiplied, become Δ E:
when the greater magnitude A B is multiplied, and let Δ E be indeed a multiple of Γ,
but greater than A B; and let Δ E be divided into parts equal to Γ, A Z, Z H, H B,
and from A B let there be taken away more than the half, B Θ, and from Θ A again
let there be taken away more than the half, and K, and let this always be done, so
that the divisions which are in A B in number equal the divisions which are in Δ E:
let therefore the divisions A K, K Θ, Θ B be equal in number to the divisions A Z,
Z H, H E.

And since Δ E is greater than A B, and from Δ E there has been taken away less than
the half, namely E H, but from A B more than the half, namely B Θ; the remainder
H A will be greater than the remainder Θ A; again since H Δ is greater than Θ A,
and from H Δ there has been taken away the half H Z, but from Θ A more than the
half Θ K; the remainder Δ Z will be greater than the remainder A K. And Δ Z is
equal to Γ: therefore it is greater than A K. Therefore A K is less than Γ: therefore
from the magnitude A B there has been left the magnitude A K, set out less than the
lesser magnitude Γ, which was to be shown.

And similarly it will be shown, even if halves have been taken away.
